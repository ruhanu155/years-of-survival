# Waters and fish

This tab adds the life of the valley's waters: nine water types as places, 14 new aquatic species, a calendar of runs and ice events, five legendary fish, a record book and eight new fishing structures. It builds on Fishing in depth, Wildlife and fish, Swimming and working in water and Traps in the Blueprint tab, and changes none of their numbers. How water reshapes terrain and draws animals belongs to the Water, calls and encounters tab.

## Water bodies as places

Every water cell belongs to one water body, and every body has one of nine types. The type sets what lives there, how clear and warm it runs, and how it changes through the year. A player who learns the types knows where to go in each month without a pin.

**Warm swims.** In water above 15 °C, warmth falls 1 a game minute instead of 3, so tundra ponds in July and the hot-spring mixing pools are the safe swims. A swim in a bog pool brings a leech 30% of the time: −1 Spirits until it drops off 20 game minutes later.

### Swimming, wading and cold water in Minecraft

This is the build of Blueprint › Swimming and working in water and Danger in detail › Self-rescue. Their rules stand; this fills the gaps (temperature bands, wading cold, wet weight, drying) and says what replaces Minecraft's own swimming.

**Depth in blocks.** A water block is 1 m, so the depth classes read from blocks. Under water, `ColumnBuilder` sets the bed's last fraction as a waterlogged `gravel_layer`, `sand_layer` or `mud_layer` from stage 4's surface in decimetres.

| Water over the bed | Depth class | Rules |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A waterlogged bed layer with 5–7 layers | Ankle, under 0.3 m | 90% speed, all tools |
| A waterlogged bed layer with 1–4 layers | Knee, 0.3–0.6 m | 70% speed, all tools |
| One full water block over a full bed | Thigh, counted as 0.8 m | 50% speed, no running, no bow; vanilla lets the player stand |
| Two or more water blocks, or thigh water over 1.2 m/s | Swimming | Nothing in hand |

**Water cools by temperature band.** Water temperature comes from the Environment field (`env.water`), which reads Conditions › Water temperature and the water type's offset. Wading cools only the legs and feet, at a share of the swim rate.

| Water | Warmth lost swimming, per game minute | Useful time | Wading share: ankle, knee, thigh |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Above 15 °C | 1 | Unlimited; energy and warmth end the swim | 0, 0, 0.1 |
| 12–15 °C | 2 | 20 game minutes | 0.05, 0.1, 0.25 |
| 8–12 °C | 3 | 20 game minutes | 0.1, 0.2, 0.35 |
| 4–8 °C | 4.5 | 10 game minutes | 0.1, 0.25, 0.5 |
| Under 4 °C | 6 | 10 game minutes | 0.15, 0.3, 0.6 |

Wading in thigh water at 2 °C costs 3.6 warmth a game minute, so a 20-minute net set in May costs 72. Before the player steps in, the prompt line reads the crossing: "Too cold to wade far: about 8 minutes before you're numb." It shows when the trip's time at this rate would take warmth under 30. An injured torso shortens every timed band (RECONCILE 120, C11; part bands in Body, injury and sickness): Hurt to 0.8 of it, so 10 game minutes become 8 and 20 become 16; Badly hurt to 0.6, so 10 become 6 and 20 become 12. Above 15 °C the swim stays untimed, but energy falls at the Hurt or Badly hurt work rate.

**Wet clothes weigh.** A worn garment's weight is its `weight_g` × (1 + u × wetness), where u is the garment's water uptake in `data_maps/item/wear.json`: fur and hide 1.0, wool 1.5, down 1.2, canvas and cotton 0.8, fleece 0.4. A soaked 4 kg parka, 3 kg hide pants and 1 kg mukluks add about 8 kg, the Blueprint's figure. Over 6 kg of water in worn clothing is the "soaked winter clothing" condition that halves swim speed. The load meter shows the water weight, and it drains to 70% wetness within 2 game minutes of leaving the water, except fur.

**Carrying in water.**

- **Pack.** Over 15 kg halves swim speed. Q held 0.5 s drops it; it floats 30 real seconds, then sinks if over 20 kg and becomes a `lost_gear` block on the bed.
- **Hand-carried loads.** A small load (under 10 kg) is towed at ×0.7 speed. A medium or larger load drops on entering swim depth and floats or sinks by its body: carcasses float, stones and iron sink.
- **The held item.** It drops on entering swim depth. Wood, bark, hide and feathers float and drift with the current to `#yearofice:snags`; iron, stone and glass sink into `lost_gear` (Minecraft development › Vanilla systems we replace).

**Diving.** Shift sinks and Space rises, as in vanilla. Breath lasts 30 real seconds on the breath ring (HUD layer 13). Items on the bed under 2 m of clear water (turbidity under 0.3) are picked up with RMB; deeper or murkier, they are lost.

**Currents.** The `yearofice:current` push applies on the client for the local player and on the server for everything else. A sweeper (`#yearofice:sweepers`) touched from upstream starts the pull-and-brace overlay: hold RMB to pull, release on each shake, 10 real seconds to get free. Eddies (current under 0.1 m/s) let a swimmer hang still at half the energy cost.

**Falling through ice, in keys.** The self-rescue steps in Danger in detail stand; only the keys follow the client table. Hold RMB to pull and tap Space to kick; Q drops the pack; once out, crawl 3 m from the hole with Shift held while moving. Ice picks (`yearofice:ice_picks`, in Fishing ids, tackle and keys) in the hotbar halve the pull bar.

**Drying off.** Leaving the water sets every immersed zone's wetness to 100% and, when wading, only the zones the water reached. Dry by a fire in about 1 game hour or on the line in about 4 (Clothing). On the body, wetness falls 10% a game hour above 10 °C while moving, 5% at 0–10 °C and not at all below 0 °C, where the soaked rule in Warmth runs. An inner layer under a wet outer layer does not dry until the outer is off.

**Drowning and SOS.** Any one of these ends a swim in `SosFlow` with the cause "drowned": useful time runs out; energy at 0 for 5 real seconds; a blackout in water; 10 real seconds pinned on a sweeper; the breath ring empties under ice. The `left_pack` block goes to the nearest bank cell downstream.

**What replaces Minecraft's swimming.**

| Vanilla | In Year of Ice | Mechanism |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Swim speed about 2 m/s, sprint-swim 5.6 m/s | 1.0 m/s, crawl 1.4 m/s | `neoforge:swim_speed` modifiers by cold, load and clothes (`yearofice:swim_cold`, `swim_load`, `swim_clothes`) |

**Build.** Classes `body.swim.SwimState` (depth class, useful time, causes), `sim.body.ColdWater` (pure: bands, wading shares, wet weight) and `client.swim.StrokeBeat`. Data: `body/standard.json` gains `water_bands`; `data_maps/item/wear.json` gains `water_uptake`. Movement in M2, cold water in M4, wading cold and wet weight in M4, water work in M5.

**GameTests.**

1. `body/swim_speed`: a bot holding W in still water 2 blocks deep covers 10 ±0.5 blocks in 10 real seconds; the crawl on the beat covers 14 ±0.5.
2. `body/swim_crossing`: a 20 m crossing of a 1.5 m/s river lands 25–35 blocks downstream (the engine test).
3. `body/useful_time`: in 3 °C water a player drowns at 10 ±0.2 game minutes; in 16 °C water a rested player is still swimming at 60; in 3 °C water a Hurt torso ends the swim at 8 ±0.2 and a Badly hurt one at 6 ±0.2.
4. `body/wade_cold`: 20 game minutes thigh-deep in 2 °C water cost 72 ±2 warmth.
5. `body/wet_weight`: a soaked parka, hide pants and mukluks weigh 8 ±0.5 kg more and halve swim speed.

### Salmon and char

**Sockeye salmon.** A deep-bodied salmon that runs through the main river to spawn on Big Lake's spring-fed beaches and its inlet creek. It barely takes a hook in fresh water, so it is a dip-net and gill-net fish, and the richest salmon at 2,100 kcal per kg. A 3 kg bright sockeye is 6,300 kcal.

- **Special rule.** Sockeye turn red after they enter the lake: flesh is bright (×1.0) below the outlet, blush (×0.85) in the lake, and spawning red (×0.7) on the beaches. A weir or net across the full width of the outlet stops the run, and every fish it holds counts toward the harvest share h.
- **Moment.** From the ridge above the lake in mid-August, the beach shallows turn red with spawning fish. A grizzly sow and two cubs wade them at dawn, and the player waits on the ridge for them to leave.

**Arctic char.** A closed population in alpine tarns, isolated since the ice age, fished through ice that stays until July. Fall males turn orange-red and crowd the tarn's gravel shoal from September 10 to 30.

- **Special rule.** The tarn has no inflow of fish and r of only 0.05 a month, so 20 char taken in one summer leave the shoal Poor for about 4 years. A tarn fished to under 10% of K stays there until the player stocks it (Habitat work).
- **Moment.** In late September, first black ice skins the tarn and the player sees red-bellied char over the shoal through 4 cm of clear ice. Dall sheep watch from the scree while the player chisels a single hole and spears one.

**Dolly Varden.** A cold-creek char that lives on salmon eggs. It follows spawning salmon up the side streams and winters in spring leads.

- **Special rule.** During a salmon spawn within 300 m, the Dolly Varden stock at that reach is ×3 for the spawning days, and roe bait already counts 2.0. Without salmon that year, Dolly Varden bite ×0.6 in the fall.
- **Moment.** A single salmon egg drifted below a pair of spawning chum takes a Dolly on every cast. Thirty metres upstream a grizzly is working the same pair.

### Whitefish and cisco

**Broad whitefish.** A heavy, blunt-nosed whitefish that feeds on snails and mussels in the oxbows all summer. It rarely bites a baited hook, so it is the coarse gill net's fall fish.

- **Special rule.** Broad whitefish leave an oxbow through its high-water channel between August 25 and September 15, then run upriver to spawn in the canyon gravel (Aquatic events). An oxbow whose channel is blocked by a beaver dam traps them, and the oxbow's oxygen then decides whether they live.
- **Moment.** On a frosty September morning at Salmon Bend, the net in the eddy holds 14 broad whitefish and 30 Bering cisco. It is the wolf team's winter in one lift, and the eagles are already on the bar.

**Round whitefish.** A small, slim, bottom-feeding whitefish of gravel riffles. It eats insect larvae and loose salmon eggs and winters in deep river holes.

- **Special rule.** Shuffle feeding: a player standing in knee-deep riffle water who holds RMB crouched (Shift) for 1 game minute kicks up larvae. Within 2 game minutes, round whitefish and grayling within 10 m gather 1–2 m downstream of the boots, where a hand grab works 20% of the time and a spear by the normal rules.
- **Moment.** A first-week player with no tackle shuffles a riffle and catches two whitefish by hand. It is 1,000 kcal earned with nothing but cold feet.

**Least cisco.** A small, oily schooling fish of open lake water, and the forage fish of the whole lake. It spawns in the lake shallows from October 1 to 25, often under the first skim ice.

- **Special rule.** The lake's cisco stock feeds its predators. Lake trout, sheefish and pike bites in that lake take a forage factor, and loons nest only on lakes where cisco stand above 0.3 of K.

```latex
m_{forage} = 0.6 + 0.6 \cdot \frac{N_{cisco}}{K_{cisco}}
```

- **Moment.** One October night the lake margin is alive with silver under skim ice. A fine net set through it gives 200 cisco by dawn, 54,000 kcal of oily fish, and next winter the lake trout bite noticeably slower.

**Bering cisco.** A fat river cisco that runs up the main river in fall to spawn in the braids. It dries well and is the classic fall fish for winter dog and wolf food.

- **Special rule.** It has no resident stock; it runs like a salmon (Salmon runs in detail). Dried Bering cisco hung outdoors below −5 °C keep until the thaw without smoking, at 0.5 kg each on the sled.
- **Moment.** The net in the eddy fills so fast in the peak week that its floats drag under in the current. The player has 30 game minutes to lift it before it tears.

### Small fish of still water

**Alaska blackfish.** A small, dark, air-breathing fish of bogs and tundra ponds. A pouch in its gut takes oxygen from gulped air, so it lives where every other fish dies.

- **Special rule.** Blackfish ignore winterkill and are the only fish in bog pools and in ponds that freeze almost to the bottom. In winter they gather under breathing holes in the sedge ice, where the blackfish trap takes them. They live 7 days in a bucket of water.
- **Moment.** A bucket of blackfish left outside freezes solid overnight. Thawed by the stove next morning, one in seven starts to swim, because it was chilled, not frozen through; the journal writes "They came back."

**Ninespine stickleback.** A tiny spined fish of every still water and the base of the food web. Males turn black and guard weed nests in June.

- **Special rule.** Stickleback are bait only: live stickleback count 1.2 for char, 1.0 for Dolly Varden and 0.8 for grayling, but live only 2 game hours in a bucket, or 6 if the water is changed every hour. A kingfisher takes about 30 a day, a loon about 200.
- **Moment.** The only way to fish the char tarn well is to carry a bucket of stickleback up 600 m of climb from a bench pond. Half are dead on arrival unless the player stops to change the water.

**Lake chub.** A silvery minnow of creeks and lake inlets, and the valley's live bait.

- **Special rule.** Chub are the hardiest live bait: 3 hours in wet moss, 3 days in a bucket, forever in a live crib. Pike take live chub at 2.0 and burbot at 1.2.
- **Moment.** The first 6 kg pike of the summer comes on a chub the player caught that morning in a willow minnow trap.

### Lamprey, mussel and the larvae

**Arctic lamprey.** An eel-like jawless fish that migrates up the main river under the ice in late fall. It travels at night along the banks in 0.5–1.5 m of water, where the current is slowest. It is almost a third fat: 3,300 kcal per kg.

- **Special rule.** It never takes a hook (b = 0). The run passes any point in 5–10 nights inside its window, 70% of it between 18:00 and 06:00. Rendered over the fire for 1 game hour, 1 kg gives 0.35 kg of oil, which burns at the burbot-oil rate in a fat lamp: about 20 hours of light.
- **Moment.** One silent December night, a lamprey hangs from the edge of the player's burbot hole by its sucker mouth. The next morning the lamprey basket holds 120 of them, frozen into a grey rope within minutes of air at −28 °C.

**Yukon floater mussel.** A large, thin-shelled freshwater mussel buried in soft bottoms. It filters the water and dies where the water fouls.

- **Special rule.** In knee water the player feels for mussels with bare feet or hands: 1 a game minute in a bed. Each gives 25 g of meat (20 kcal) and a shell. One in 300 holds a freshwater pearl, a trophy at +2 Spirits on a shelf. No mussels live within 100 m below a latrine or cleaning spot, which is the water-quality rule in Fire, water and light made visible.
- **Moment.** A muskrat midden of opened shells on an oxbow bank shows the player the mussel bed, and with it the first spoon lure before iron (Gear and structures).

**Caddisfly larva.** A grub that builds a case of sand grains or bits of plant under creek stones.

- **Special rule.** Riffle stones in clearwater creeks can be turned: 6 stones a game minute, a larva under 1 in 3, caddis 70% and stonefly 30%. A stone resets after 3 game days. None live in glacial, bog or hot-spring water, and density is ×2 below a lake outlet. A larva lasts 1 bite or 20 minutes on the hook and 3 days in a damp bark box.
- **Moment.** Evening caddis hatch: on calm evenings from July 1 to August 15, adults fly at dusk and grayling rise ×1.5 for 1 game hour. The player hears the rises before seeing them.

**Stonefly nymph.** A large, flat nymph of fast riffles. In the emergence from June 5 to 20, nymphs crawl out onto rocks and willows and split their skins.

- **Special rule.** During the emergence, grayling and Dolly Varden bite ×1.6 on stonefly bait and flies, and adults can be picked off willows at 3 a game minute. Empty skins on the rocks are a clue the crouch-watch names: "Stonefly shucks on every rock. The fish will be on them."
- **Moment.** The creek mouth's best week of the year begins the morning the player finds shucks on the stones. It ends ten days later whether they fished it or not.

### Spawned-out salmon feed the valley

Every spawner dies, and 90% of them become carcasses that drift, lodge on snags and bars, and feed the land. This is the moment in the video where dead salmon drift downstream "too far gone" to eat but full of bears and eagles.

**How many.** Escapement is the run minus the player's take and the bears' take. Carcasses in reach equal 0.9 × escapement, released along each run's spawning grounds over its last third and the 10 days after. A carcass lasts 10–20 days in water and 5–10 days on a bar, and freezes in place at freeze-up.

**Who comes.** Each feeder reads the count of carcasses C within 2 km of its perch or range.

| Feeder | Rule | What it means for the player |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bald eagle | Eagles at a reach = min(12, C ÷ 40); they arrive from outside the map and leave at freeze-up | A dozen eagles on the bars at Salmon Bend in September in a strong chum year |
| Mew gulls | Flocks of 10–60 over any reach with C over 50, July–September | Screaming gulls mark the run from 1 km |
| Grizzly | Eats 10 kg of salmon a day; when over 20 fresh fish a day are in reach, eats only brains, skin and roe of 1 in 3 and leaves the rest | Half-eaten fish on the snags below, as in the staged hunt |
| Home wolf pack | Fishes spawned-out chum in water under 0.3 m at dusk, August 15 – September 30 | Wolf tracks on every bar; the pack is closer to the river than usual |
| Ravens, fox, marten, mink | Take carcasses on bars by the carcass-succession rules | Tracks and drag marks |
| Dolly Varden, grayling, round whitefish | Bite ×1.5 on roe and flesh baits downstream of a spawning reach | The best fall fishing follows the carcasses |

**The bear year.** A strong run feeds the grizzlies into the den: in a chum year with y × e of 1.2 or more, grizzlies in the C3 range carry 15% more fat. A weak run sends them looking: with y × e under 0.6, grizzly visits to camps August–September are ×1.5. So a fish wheel run all season raises the bear pressure on the player's camps four years later.

**The fertile bend.** Salmon carry sea nitrogen into the valley. Willows, berries and grass within 50 m of a reach that held over 200 carcasses give +15% the next summer, so the best berry patch in the valley is often on the salmon bar.

**What the player can do with a carcass.** A carcass cannot be eaten. It counts as 1 kg of guts for garden fertility and as full bait for cubby sets, and it is a scent point of strength 2 (Bear rules). A Companion wolf eats a carcass under 2 days dead at half value; older ones give it a sick day 20% of the time.

### Fall runs and the sheefish migration

**Whitefish and cisco.** Broad whitefish and Bering cisco pass Salmon Bend at dusk from September into October, rolling on the surface in the eddies. A coarse net takes broad whitefish, a fine net cisco; the fish wheel takes both at half rate. These runs cost nothing in salmon, so they are the honest way to fill a wolf team's winter.

**Sheefish.** Sheefish winter in Big Lake and the lower river (A3) and feed there all summer, which the existing spots cover. From September 15 to October 10 a run of about 120 spawners moves upriver past Salmon Bend to the canyon gravel. On the run, sheefish at the bend and confluence spots bite ×3 on spoons, and 5% draw from the trophy band in Fishing in depth instead of 2%.

### The lamprey run under the ice

The lamprey run is the year's last harvest and its strangest. The window and the start date are drawn on May 1; the run passes each point for 5–10 nights.

1. **The warning.** Two to four days before the run reaches the map, Pete Lund mentions it on the radio: "Lamprey are running at the village. Two days, maybe three." Nothing else warns the player.
2. **The sign.** On the first night, lampreys show at open holes and leads within 2 m of the bank. A lamprey hanging from the edge of a hole is the sight players remember.
3. **The fishery.** A lamprey basket with its fence (Gear and structures) set through the ice at the bank catches lamprey each night. Catch per basket per night has a mean of 40 × run strength × lead factor, where the lead factor is fence length over 5 m, capped at 1.
4. **The end.** After the last night, baskets catch nothing. A basket left in the river until breakup is lost to the ice.

Run strength follows a triangle over the run's nights. One in ten whitefish caught in the main river all year carries a round lamprey scar, which is the only sign the run exists before the player first sees it.

### Winterkill

Shallow, weedy water runs out of oxygen under ice and snow. Each still water body tracks dissolved oxygen DO from freeze-over, starting at 12 mg/L.

```latex
DO_{t+1} = \min\left(12,\ DO_t - \frac{c \cdot (1 + snow_{cm}/50)}{\max(0.1,\ D_{mean} - ice_m)} + o\right)
```

Here c is the type's oxygen demand, D\_mean the body's mean depth in m, ice\_m the ice thickness from Calendar, and o the inflow term: 0.15 mg/L a day for a body fed by a spring or a creek, 0 otherwise.

| Type | c (mg/L·m a day) |
| --- | --- |
| Bog pool | 0.024 |
| Beaver pond | 0.018 |
| Oxbow lake | 0.015 |
| Tundra pond | 0.012 |
| Big lake | 0.006 |

**Deaths.** Below 3 mg/L, each species dies at 5% × (3 − DO) of its stock a day. Stickleback die at a third of that rate, and blackfish never. A normal winter leaves a 1.2 m beaver pond at about 4.4 mg/L in April with no loss. A hard winter's extra snow and ice drive it to zero by March and kill about 76% of its fish, which is the existing hard-winter rule in Fish stocks, now produced by the model.

**The gasping hole.** Below 4 mg/L, fish crowd any open hole in the ice for the air at its surface: bites at a hole in that body ×3. A player who keeps 3 holes open adds 0.02 mg/L a day each. That saves a pond only in a mild winter, so the gasping hole is mostly a feast before a die-off.

**What the player sees.** Nothing shows under the ice until a hole is cut: then fish hang just below it, slow and pale. At ice-out the shore of a killed pond is lined with dead pike for 10 days, and gulls and ravens find them within a day. The messenger says one line at ice-out: "Dead fish along the beaver pond. It winterkilled."

### The fish record book

The record book is the messenger's fish page and a book item on the cabin shelf. It records, per species, the heaviest fish, its length, date, spot and gear, and the year card reads its biggest fish from it.

**Weighing.** A steelyard (Small items) weighs to 0.1 kg. Without one, the measuring stick gives length, and weight is estimated from it at ±15%, written with "≈":

```latex
W_{kg} = a \cdot L_{cm}^{\,b}
```

Each species carries a and b in its data; most use b = 3.0, with a from 0.000008 for a lamprey to 0.000013 for a sockeye. Only weighed fish can set a record; estimated ones are listed under it.

**Arvid's page.** Arvid's record page, found with his other pages, fills the book with his best fish from 1938 to 1971 in a second column. The player is competing with a dead trapper, which makes ordinary fish worth weighing.

### Habitat work

The player can make water better as well as empty it. These are the waters' share of Grow without an end.

- **Stocking.** Releasing 6 or more live fish of one species into a fishless body of a type that suits it sets N to the number released and K to that body's capacity; it then grows by r. Char go only into tarns, blackfish only into ponds and bogs. A creek fish carried 2 km in the stocking bucket survives 70% of the time.
- **Keeping holes open.** Holes add oxygen by the winterkill rule above.
- **Clearing a blocked run.** A beaver dam over 1 m high blocks coho from a side stream except in high water. Notching the dam lets that year's run through, at the cost of a colony that rebuilds it within 3 days. What the water then does to the land is in the Water, calls and encounters tab.

### Passive gear while away

Nets, lines and traps keep fishing when the player is gone, and come out exactly as if the player had stood by them.

**When gear settles.** Every passive fishing block is a `food.traps` block entity: `set_line`, `gill_net`, `fish_weir`, `minnow_trap`, `blackfish_trap`, `lamprey_basket`, `fish_wheel`, `stringer`, `live_crib` and an unattended `tip_up`. Each settles at `ClockEvent.Dawn` and `Dusk`, when the player checks it, and in `ChunkCaughtUpEvent` for every dawn and dusk it missed while unloaded.

**One closed form.** A settle covers the h game hours since the last one.

1. **Catch.** The count is Poisson with mean equal to the sum of the gear's hourly mean (Fishing in depth › Passive gear) over those hours. Each hour reads its own m\_season, water temperature, turbidity and stock N/K from the Environment field's abstract sample.
2. **Catch hours.** Each fish gets a catch hour, uniform in the period. The holds cap (1 a hook, 8 a net segment, 15 a weir, 10 a minnow trap, 30 a wheel, 60 a blackfish trap) turns away fish caught after it fills.
3. **Losses since the catch hour t.** A fish on a set line stays with probability 0.9^t (0.95^t on a gorge). Where an otter's or bear's range covers the gear, each fish survives theft with 0.95^t. A fish dead in a net over 12 hours is eaten 25% of the time.
4. **Spoilage.** A dead fish's `yearofice:fresh` clock starts at its catch hour at the water's temperature, settled lazily (Decay and time).
5. **Stocks.** Every fish kept calls `EcologyService.harvest`, so N falls as it would live.

**Once-a-day rolls at the dawn settle.** Net tearing (5% of days at turbidity over 0.6, 10% when the net took a beaver or otter, always in moving ice); the mink (10% of nights within its 1.5 km shoreline range, taking 1 fish under 1 kg, and emptying a minnow trap on 25% of those visits, by the Bestiary); loons drowned on 2% of net-days and mergansers on 1% in rivers; Kessler's burbot stealing one set line's bait.

**Every roll is named.** All rolls come from `Rng.of(seed, "trap", pos, day, period)`, so a chunk loaded all month and one loaded at its end give the same catch (`layers/live_equals_caught_up`).

**Tip-ups.** With a player within 200 m, a tip-up rolls live each game minute at rod rate ×0.8 and its flag shows from 200 m. Unattended, it settles as a one-hook set line at the same rate, and a hooked fish escapes 50% of the time unless it is a burbot.

**Under the ice.** A hole skins over by the ice rules but the gear under it keeps fishing. A hole frozen over 10 cm must be rechiseled to check. On the day a body's ice turns rotten, gear still under it goes downstream as `lost_gear` at the next snag (Every concept from the video › Gear left on the ice).

**The player learns by looking.** Catches never reach the messenger: `food.traps` posts no `HudLine` for a catch. Floats bobbing, a tip-up flag, gulls over a net and a bear on the bank are the signs.

**GameTests.** `food/net_catchup_equals_live`: a 4-segment net loaded for 30 game days and one unloaded for them give identical catches. `food/net_three_segments`: 3 joined segments never catch. `food/setline_escape`: fish hooked and left 10 game hours are gone 65 ±3% of the time.

### Rules the video rows needed

Five moments from the video pointed at rules that did not quite say them, and each is now a number. The last rule closes a gap with Water, calls and encounters.

**Fish cannot simply be grabbed (video row 25).** RMB on a free-swimming fish entity outside the grab cases (a falls lip in a run, the shuffle, a sedge spawn, tickling, a breathing hole, a stranded fish) never catches it. It flees and adds +20 to the spot's wariness.

**Fish hold at the lip of a falls (row 79).** Every pool below falls has a lip zone: the 2 blocks of slack water just above the drop. In the open-water months it holds grayling and Dolly Varden at a third of the pool's stock, and its m\_place counts 1.0 as if inside the pool. During a run, salmon stage in the lip zone between jumps, which is where the hand-grab happens.

**Deeper water holds bigger fish (row 198).** In the size draw, a bait more than 2 m deep uses u^1.6 instead of u^2, and one over 5 m deep uses u^1.3. A 4.0 kg mean pike becomes about 4.5 kg on a deep drop-off; shallow fishing still gives more bites.

**A channel steers fish into the net (row 136).** A stone or stake wing from the bank to a net's end, built by wading at 20 game minutes a metre, adds its length to the net's joined length for coverage. Wing and net together still cap at the channel's width. A wing is a `fish_weir` fence piece without a basket.

**A raft can capsize in a fish fight (row 229).** Fighting from a raft or canoe, each tension spike over 90% while the player stands, or any jump within 2 m of the hull, rolls a capsize: 3% on a raft, 8% in a canoe, ×0.5 when kneeling (Shift held). A capsize puts the player in the water by the swim rules and unlashed gear floats or sinks (Danger in detail › The river). A fish over 8 kg tows a canoe at its run speed ×0.3, so a big fish is fought from the boat, not landed into it until tired.

**When water moves, stocks move.** A spot's capacity K is q × its type's capacity, so when the terrain changes q (Water, calls and encounters), K rescales at the next change tick and N is clamped to it. A failed dam moves its pond's N to the creek spot below up to that spot's K; the rest are stranded fish, caught by hand 100% for 2 game days. A new oxbow starts with N = 0.3 × K of pike.

## The food web

Fish tie the water to the land through the animals that eat them and the player who competes with them. Most of these animals already exist; the birds and the mink are new entities whose rules here are only about fish. How water draws animals in general is in the Water, calls and encounters tab.

**Readable rules.** Each rule makes an animal a sign. A loon pair on a pond means the pond holds fish other than blackfish. A dipper means the water will be open in January. Eagles mean carcasses within 2 km, and gulls mean a school or a gut pile.

**Otter holes.** In winter an otter keeps 1–3 breathing holes open at its home spot and at riffles. A player who finds one fishes it without chiseling, at bite ×1.2, because fish gather where the otter hunts. Fish remains on the ice beside the hole show which species are below.

### Fish that eat fish

The water's own food chain changes what the player catches. The forage factor for lake predators is under least cisco. Pike in a beaver pond or oxbow without chub or stickleback bite ×0.8 and run 20% smaller in the size draw. Burbot in the main river eat lamprey in December: burbot bite ×1.3 on lamprey chunks during and after the run.

### The player in the web

The player is one more predator, and the valley answers what the player takes.

1. **Taking.** Each salmon the player takes is one fewer carcass, so a heavy harvest means fewer eagles that fall and a smaller berry bonus on the bar next summer.
2. **Overtaking.** A run cut by the echo feeds fewer bears, and hungry bears come to camps (The bear year).
3. **Sharing.** Fish below the grizzly's riffle, a fresh otter hole, gulls over a cisco school: other hunters find fish for the player who watches them.
4. **Feeding.** Guts thrown in the river feed gulls and eagles downstream, a carcass on a cubby set feeds the trapline, and a wolf team turns the fall runs into a necessity.

### Tests

NeoForge GameTests in `gametest.WatersTests`, named `yearofice:ecosystem/<test>` (Minecraft development › Conflicting ids, row 22), using headless saved-data runs where a test spans game months.

1. **Winterkill.** A 1.2 m beaver pond through a normal winter ends at DO 4.4 ±0.3 mg/L on April 19 with under 2% loss. The same pond in a hard winter loses 70–82% of its pike and 0% of its blackfish.
2. **Carcasses.** A chum run of R = 1,500 at y = 1.0 with no player gives 1,150–1,350 carcasses in reach, and the eagle count at Salmon Bend peaks at 8–12.
3. **Lamprey.** Over 100 seeded years the run always starts between November 20 and December 15 and lasts 5–10 nights. One basket with a 5 m fence at the bank averages 40 ±6 lamprey on full-strength nights.
4. **Ice-out.** At 10:00 on clear days, the bite rate at a weed shelf in the first 5 days after the lake clears is 2.5 ±0.1 times the rate on days 15–20.
5. **Old Hookjaw.** In 100 bot fights, every plant-fiber and sinew line snaps on the first run. On monofilament with drag, a bot lands him at least 30% of the time.
6. **Forage.** In a lake without pygmy whitefish, lake trout bite at a drop-off with cisco at 0.2 of K is 0.6 ±0.02 times the rate with cisco at K. In Big Lake, with pygmy whitefish at K, it is 0.73 ±0.02 (Water life in depth › Pygmy whitefish and trout-perch).
