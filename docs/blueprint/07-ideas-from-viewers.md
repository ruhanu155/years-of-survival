# Ideas from viewers

Viewers left over 6,000 comments under the source video; the most-liked asked for more berries, needle tea and real bears. This tab ranks 75 ideas from 580 comments and 263 replies against the blueprint. It gives full rules for the 17 worth adding or adapting, and shows how systems recreate the moments viewers loved.

## How this tab was made

**Source.** The Top-sorted comments under Forge Labs' "1 Year in Alaska in ULTRA REALISTIC Minecraft", read on October 19, 2026, eight days after upload. The video showed 6,036 comments; this tab used the first 580 top-level comments and 263 replies under the 20 most-liked threads.

**Method.** Every wish, complaint, realism note and suggestion was pulled out and grouped with its duplicates. Likes are summed per group. A comment that lists several ideas counts its full likes toward each idea, so the long list comments rank high.

**What was left out.** About 120 "I'm jolly too" comments, 48 requests for the mod's name or release, 22 "play Vintage Story" comments and general praise. Usernames are not recorded, and comments are paraphrased.

**Fixed decisions win.** No hostile mobs, wolves instead of dogs, 365-day years, Minecraft on NeoForge, and explore, don't dig. An idea that breaks one of these is declined with the reason, even when it is popular.

## Berries

The most-liked comment of all came from an Alaskan: fishing and berries feed the state, and many berries are poisonous. Two entries answer it. Berry country adds plants, a peak week and spoiled fruit; Berry leather and preserves gives the harvest a winter.

### Berry country

**What it is.** Five more berries, a peak week for every berry, fruit that ferments on the bush, and a berry rake. They join `plants` data under Foraging and plant knowledge and keep its rules: ripeness stages, patches that remember, the inspect card and the three ways to identify a plant.

**Two named berries stay out.** Salmonberries and huckleberries grow on the coast, south of the Alaska Range, not in a valley at 64°N. The radio can mention them; the valley never grows them.

| Berry | Where | Fruit | Unit | kcal | Vitamin C | Pick time | Keeps | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wild raspberry | Burns 2–10 years old, cut banks, cleared yards | July 20 – August 31 | Cup | 65 | +1.5 | 25 game minutes | 2 days | Thorny canes: gloves or −1 Condition per cup |
| Nagoonberry | Wet meadows, beaver meadows, lake edges | July 10 – August 10 | Cup | 70 | +1.5 | 45 game minutes | 2 days | Rare: 1–3 patches a region; +3 Spirits the first cup each year |
| Northern red currant | Moist birch slopes, creek bottoms | July 15 – August 20 | Cup | 60 | +2 | 20 game minutes | 4 days | Sour: −1 Spirits raw, none in a mixed dish |
| Bearberry | Dry gravel benches, south bluffs | August 10 to the next May, on the plant | Cup | 50 | +0.5 | 20 game minutes; 40 under snow | 30 days | Mealy and dry; bears eat it in spring |
| Bog cranberry | Sphagnum mats in bog and muskeg | September 1 to the next May, on the vine | Cup | 50 | +1 | 40 game minutes | 60 days | Better after a frost; the only bog fruit in winter |

None is in the pocket guide. Raspberry and currant patches sit in the same moist woods as baneberry, so the existing baneberry lookalike now has three neighbors to be mistaken for. Nagoonberry is the treasure: few patches, high value, and the patch card pins it for good.

**Peak week.** Each berry's fruit stage has a 7-day peak in its middle, drawn yearly from the summer's degree-days. During the peak a patch holds 130% of its seed value and picking runs 20% faster, because the branches are heavy. Bears weight that patch ×1.5 as a food target, on top of hyperphagia.

- **Messenger.** One line when the first patch the player has pinned reaches peak: "Blueberries at their peak on the burn slope."
- **Bees.** The June bee index from the bestiary tab multiplies peak yield, so a cold June means a thin August.
- **Birds.** After peak, berry-eating birds strip 5% of an unpicked patch a day.

**Fruit that turns.** In the first 10 days of a berry's past stage, the bush still holds fruit. It picks at half kcal, and 25% of those cups are fermented.

1. **The tell.** Fermented fruit looks dull and split; the inspect card's smell check reads "sour, like wine." Wasps hover the patch from July to August.
2. **The bill.** A fermented cup adds 40 to nausea (Sick stomach) and has a 20% chance of 12 game hours of gut illness.
3. **The safe ones.** Fruit that stays on the plant into frost (lingonberry, crowberry, bearberry, bog cranberry, highbush cranberry, rose hips) never ferments below 0 °C. It freezes instead.

**The berry rake.** A comb-toothed scoop that strips low bushes fast. It is made at the workbench from 1 split birch board and 12 stiff willow tines in 1 game hour; it weighs 0.4 kg (Bushcraft).

| Effect | Number |
| --- | --- |
| Works on | Blueberry, bog blueberry, lingonberry, crowberry, bog cranberry, bearberry |
| Pick time | ×0.4 |
| Crushed | 10% of each cup becomes mash: eat it now or use it for leather |
| Stripping | Each raked cup counts 1.5 toward a patch's 70% stripped rule |
| Cleaning | Leaves and twigs come with it: 5 game minutes per 4 cups, ×0.5 on a breezy day by winnowing |

The rake trades next year for this year. A player who rakes a whole slope in August eats well in winter and finds half the berries the next summer.

**On screen and sound.** Peak bushes droop and show heavier clusters. Fermented fruit has its own darker texture. The rake makes a dry rattling sweep; a full basket thumps. Bears at a patch leave torn branches and purple scat, the existing sign.

**Ties.** Foraging patches and stripping; bears' hyperphagia and patch scent; Sick stomach; Berry leather and preserves; the bee index; Spirits.

**Peak week, exact.** The peak starts at the middle of the fruit window minus 3 days, after the yearly degree-day shift. The shift is ±10 days, so the peak date varies year to year. The messenger line fires once a year, only for a pinned patch.

**Wasps.** From July 15 to August 31, picking a fermenting patch has a 3% chance a cup of a sting: −1 Condition and a 4-hour swollen hand (fine tasks 10% slower). Smoke within 6 m clears them.

**The rake.** It lasts 200 cups, then loses tines; a re-tine is 6 willow tines and 20 game minutes. Winnowing works with wind at 3 m/s or more.

**Edge cases.** A fermented cup in a mixed basket stays separate: the stack splits by the `fermented` flag, so the player can sort it out. Fermented fruit dried into leather is safe (drying kills it) but gives half vitamin C as usual.

**Feel.** A peak patch shows bowed branches and audible bees at noon. A fermenting patch smells sour on the inspect card and shows wasps as small particles. The rake's rattle carries 30 m, so it scares grouse and is heard by a bear in the patch.

### Berry leather and preserves

**What it is.** Three ways to keep berries into winter, each teaching one rule: dried and cooked berries lose vitamin C, frozen and water-kept ones do not. Dried berries and pemmican stay as written in Preserving in detail.

**Berry leather.** Mashed berries dried into flat sheets: the light, sweet trail food.

1. **Mash.** 4 cups mashed in a bowl or the stone mortar, 5 game minutes.
2. **Spread.** On a birch-bark sheet laid on the drying rack, 1 game minute.
3. **Dry.** 1 game day of sun or wind on the rack, or 8 game hours on a shelf beside a stove. Rain on an uncovered rack follows the meat-drying rule and can mold the batch.

The output is 4 sheets of 0.08 kg, 85 kcal each, so no calories are lost. Vitamin C is half the fresh value, as for dried berries. Sheets keep 1 year dry, stack 16 to a slot and break a five-day food streak. Damp sheets mold in 10 game days.

**Syrup preserve.** The viewers' jam, sweetened with birch syrup because there is no sugar.

| Step | Input | Time | Output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cook | 4 cups berries, 0.5 L birch syrup | 30 game minutes simmered, tended | 1.2 kg preserve |
| Seal | A tin can with a lid, a station jar, or a birch-bark box sealed with 50 g pitch | 2 game minutes | Keeps 1 year sealed, 30 days opened |

A 50 g spoonful gives 130 kcal, nearly all carbs. Cooking removes all its vitamin C, by the existing rule. What it gives instead is morale: +2 Spirits once a day with bread, bannock or porridge, and +3 for the first spoon of each jar from December to February.

**Syrup sets the limit.** One tap gives 4 L of sap a day for about 3 weeks, and syrup boils down at 100 : 1 (Blueprint › Cooking: 40 L of sap gives 0.4 L; RECONCILE 62), so a tap makes about 0.8 L of syrup a spring. Four jars need 2 L: about 3 taps for the season and 70 game hours of boiling. Preserves are a luxury the player plans in April.

**Frozen berries.** After the daily mean stays below −5 °C, berries in any container in an unheated cache, cellar or ice house freeze. Frozen berries keep their full vitamin C until thawed, and a thawed cup must be eaten within 1 game day. This is the cheapest winter scurvy defense after rose hips, and mice cannot open a sealed container.

**Lingonberries in water.** Lingonberries packed in a barrel and covered with boiled water keep 180 days with full vitamin C, as northern households kept them. A barrel of 18 slots holds 40 cups. The water freezes in winter and the berries keep anyway.

**The lesson in one line.** The messenger's food card shows vitamin C per serving, so the player can read it: "Preserve: 0. Frozen cup: +2."

**On screen.** Leather sheets curl and darken as they dry. A sealed preserve jar shows a pitch-dark rim; an opened one a spoon in it. Frozen berries rattle in the container.

**Ties.** Vitamin C reserve; birch sap and syrup; containers and cache raiders; Spirits; drying racks and rain; the stone mortar.

**Studio detail (Body and survival).**

**Containers, counted.**

| Container | Where it comes from | How many | Holds |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

A sealed bark box leaks 5% of the time after a freeze-thaw cycle; the preserve then keeps 30 days, like an opened one.

**Mixed berries.** Leather and preserves accept any 4 cups. Vitamin C and kcal average across the mix. Highbush cranberry and red currant lose their sour Spirits cost in either.

**Freezing, exact.** "Frozen" uses the `fresh` component's frozen state. Berries freeze after 1 game day in a container below −5 °C. A thawed cup is back on the fresh clock with 1 game day left. Refreezing does not reset it.

**Spirits rules.** The preserve's +2 counts once a day and needs bread, bannock or porridge in the same meal. The +3 for the first spoon of a jar counts once per jar, at most once a week, December to February. Both break a food streak.

**Night and season.** Leather on the rack overnight below 0 °C stops drying; it resumes in the day. In winter, leather dries only indoors.

**Feel.** Spreading the mash draws a purple sheet on the bark. Sealing shows the pitch rim set dark. The first opened jar in December writes "Opened the jar. Tastes like August."

## Foraging and the camp kitchen

Three viewer ideas widen what the forest feeds and how it is processed: teas from meadow plants, grubs from rotten wood, and a mortar to pound it all.

### Meadow teas

**What it is.** Four plants for tea that viewers named in the replies under the berry comment. Each uses the existing tea rule: 0.25 L of boiled water, 5–10 game minutes, +5 warmth and +8 water on top of its own effect. Two of them grow only where people walk, which makes the player's trails bloom.

| Plant | Where | Season | Prep | Effect | Limit or cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fireweed leaf, fermented | Burns, clearings, river terraces | Leaves June 15 – July 31 | Bruise a handful 5 game minutes, seal 2 game days at 15 °C or more, dry 1 day | +2 Spirits; a handful makes 4 cups; keeps 2 years | Once a day counts. No vitamin C |
| Fireweed leaf, plain | Same | Same | Dried 1 day | +1 Spirits | Once a day counts |
| Pineapple weed | Worn trails, yards, disturbed gravel only | Flower heads July 1 – August 31 | Dried 1 day | Falls asleep 30 game minutes sooner; sleep recovery +10% that night; +1 Spirits | Once a night |
| Dandelion | Within 200 m of Station Nine, the roadhouse, the mining camp and Arvid's cabin; spreads 2% a year along worn trails | Leaves May–June; roots September | Leaves raw; roots roasted 20 game minutes and pounded | Leaves +0.5 vitamin C; root "coffee" +2 Spirits before 09:00 | Leaves bitter: −1 Spirits unless eaten with fat. No caffeine, and the tooltip says so |
| Wild rose petals | Rose bushes | June 10 – July 5 | Fresh or dried 1 day | +1 Spirits, +0.3 vitamin C | Picking petals from a bush cuts its August hips by 30% |

**Fermented fireweed is the everyday tea.** It gives what Labrador tea gives without Labrador tea's 2-cup nausea limit. Making it needs a warm room and a sealed box, so it is a cabin skill, not a trail one.

**Pineapple weed follows the player.** It uses the plantain rule: each worn trail or yard cell has a 5% chance each summer to gain a patch. A first-year camp has none; a third-year camp has a ring of it. The inspect card's smell check reads "pineapple," which separates it from the plain mayweed beside it.

**Rose petals are a real choice.** Rose hips are the main winter scurvy defense. A June player who picks every bush for tea finds 30% fewer hips in August, and the plant card says so when petals are picked: "Fewer hips this fall."

**On screen and sound.** Fermenting leaves turn from green to dark brown in the box over 2 days. Dandelion roots roast black on a stone and crackle. Pineapple weed shows as low green-yellow knobs along the path.

**Ties.** Spirits; Sleep; the vitamin C reserve; worn trails and the plantain rule; rose hips; the stone mortar.

**Studio detail (Body and survival).**

**Once a day, exactly.** Each tea's Spirits counts once per game day, but different teas stack: fermented fireweed, chaga and dandelion coffee on one day give +6. All teas together count at most +6 a day. The tea's +5 warmth and +8 water count every cup.

**Fermenting, edge rules.**

- Needs a sealed birch-bark box or tin and 15 °C or more for 2 game days, in a room or inner pocket.
- Below 15 °C the timer pauses. Over 2 days at 25 °C or more, 20% of batches mold.
- One handful makes 4 cups; a box holds 4 handfuls.

**Pineapple weed, exact.** The trail-wear cells come from the player's footprint record: a cell walked on 20 or more days in a summer counts as worn. A ring needs about 2 summers of daily walking.

**Dandelion spread.** 2% a year per worn trail cell next to an existing patch, so dandelions creep along the player's own paths from Station Nine. The root dig is September only and uses the 2-block soil rule.

**Rose petals, exact.** Picking all petals from a bush cuts its hips by 30% that August. Picking from a third of the bushes in a patch costs nothing visible, which the plant card hints at: "Leave some flowers."

**Sleep tie.** Pineapple weed's 30 minutes sooner means the sleep radial allows sleep 30 game minutes before the energy-below-7 rule would.

**Feel.** Brewing draws the steam colour by tea: amber for fireweed, pale gold for pineapple weed, near-black for dandelion coffee. The first fermented batch writes "The fireweed smells of tea now."

**Ties.** Spirits (Blueprint); Sleep; rose hips and vitamin C; trails (Open world in depth); the stone mortar.

Spreading runs once a year on May 1 per region. ### Grubs and ant brood

**What it is.** Insects as food and bait, as one viewer asked after watching Forge Labs eat worms. Two real sources: sawyer beetle larvae in dead spruce and carpenter ant brood in rotten stumps. They are a small, steady food that works in rain and near camp.

| Source | Where | When | Yield | Time | kcal | Raw risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sawyer beetle larvae | Rotten spruce logs and stumps 2–6 years dead; beetle-killed stands ×3 | All year; frozen in winter | 0–6 grubs per log section per year | Split with the hatchet, 5 game minutes; 10 in winter | 8 each | 2% gut illness |
| Carpenter ant brood | Rotten stumps over 30 cm across | June 1 – August 15 | 1 handful (60 g) per stump per year | 3 game minutes | 80 | 2% gut illness |

**Cooking.** A handful roasts on a hot stone or the skillet in 2 game minutes, which removes the gut-illness chance. Macros are 10 / 40 / 50 (carbs, fat, protein), so grubs carry some fat into a lean-meat diet.

**Rate.** About 80–120 kcal a game hour of searching and splitting, between foraging and good fishing. A log refills in 1 year, so a ring of logs near camp is a slow, renewing pantry.

**Ants bite.** Opening a stump bare-handed costs −1 Condition and 1 game minute of stinging, as with nettle. Gloves prevent it.

**Finding them is reading the land.** The game shows where grubs are before the player swings.

- **Flaked bark.** Woodpeckers flake bark off beetle-killed spruce (bestiary tab), and those trunks hold ×2 larvae.

## Hunting and fishing

The second-biggest list comment asked for a sling, spear fishing and fish that get scared. Three more ideas come from replies: camouflage, warmth from a kill, and a way to haul a moose before snow.

### The sling

**What it is.** A cord with a pouch that throws a stone. It is the weak-body weapon: no strength gate, cheap ammo, quiet, and useless on anything bigger than a hare. It fills the gap between throwing rocks by hand and the light bow.

**No slingshot.** A slingshot needs rubber, which the valley does not have. Viewers in the replies said the same.

**Making it.** Start tier. 2 m of any two-ply cordage and a pouch: a 10 × 6 cm patch of rawhide, a hide scrap or a shirt strip. Knife, 20 game minutes; 0.1 kg. Fiber cords fray after 200 throws, sinew or babiche after 600, and re-cording takes 10 game minutes.

**Ammo.**

| Ammo | Where | Weight | Spread at practice 0 | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| River pebble | Gravel bars and cut banks: 10 a game minute | 0.05 kg | 5° | Stack of 32 in one slot; reusable |
| Clay shot | Clay rolled and dried 1 game day; 20 per 0.5 kg clay | 0.04 kg | 3.5° | Shatters on a hit 50% of the time |

**Throwing.** Hold RMB to whirl and release to throw, the same input as drawing a bow.

1. **Whirl.** Power builds over 1.2 real seconds. The pouch circles in view and the whirl hum rises in pitch.
2. **Snap.** Each whirl cycle lasts 0.6 s. Releasing in the last 0.2 s of a cycle, on the hum's peak, gives +20% speed. Releasing off the beat doubles the spread.
3. **Flight.** Full power is 30 m/s with normal gravity: effective to 20 m, reaching 60 m.
4. **Practice.** The skill "Sling" has N = 60 throws. Spread falls from the table's value to 1.5° at Practiced.

Each throw costs 3 kcal. The whirl hum carries 15 m, so small game within 15 m may freeze or flush before the release: throw fast or from farther away.

**What a hit does.**

| Target | Head hit | Body hit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Grouse, ptarmigan, squirrel, hare | Kills | Stuns 60% of the time (pick it up within 5 real seconds); otherwise it flees injured at half speed for 10 game minutes |
| Duck on the water | Kills | Stuns; it floats, and a Companion wolf can fetch it |
| Beaver, porcupine, fox, marten | Hurts | Hurts; it flees; never kills |
| A testing wolf | Drives it back 30 m | Same: counts as striking a wolf |
| A Curious or Alert bear, 15–40 m away | Hazing: it leaves 60% of the time | Same |
| A Defensive bear, a sow's cub, or a bear on a carcass | Triggers a charge | Same: the wrong response |
| A moose | Irritates it; a rutting bull charges 50% of the time | Same |

**Hazing is the new trick.** Rangers move curious bears off with thrown stones and noise, and the sling lets the player do it from 40 m. The danger is reading the bear wrong: stoning a Defensive bear starts a charge, as Danger in detail would predict.

**Losing stones.** A miss into brush loses the pebble 40% of the time; on gravel or snow 10%. A pebble that lands visibly shows a small glint for 30 real seconds.

**On screen and sound.** A first-person whirl animation, a hum that rises and peaks, a crack of release, then a thock on a hit or a clatter on a miss. A stunned grouse flaps on its back.

**Ties.** Throwing rocks; Practice; small game and snares as food; the bear states in Danger in detail; wolves testing a weak player; gravel bars as a resource; the Companion wolf's Fetch.

**Studio detail (Ecosystem and hunting).** The sling.

**Seasons and weather.**

- Gravel bars lie under snow from November to April. Pebbles then come only from open-water edges and wind-scoured bars (2 a game minute), so a stock gathered in October matters. A belt pouch (`yearofice:pebble_pouch`, Curios belt slot, 64 pebbles) carries it.
- Clay shot is made in summer: wet clay freezes below 0 °C and cannot be rolled.
- Mittens below −25 °C double the spread; bare hands start the frostnip timer.
- Rain soaks the pouch: −10% speed until dried.
- Indoors or within 1.5 m of a wall or trunk the whirl is blocked.

**Hazing has limits.** Each hit on the same bear in one game day works less: 60%, then 40%, then 20%. A bear hazed off 3 times on different days marks the camp as a bad place and loses one step toward Curious with it, the counter to the camp-food rule in Danger in detail.

**Targets that suit it.** Spruce grouse freeze in trees and are the sling's best food. Ruffed grouse flush at 10 m (Bestiary › Ruffed grouse), inside the hum's 15 m. Ducks on water are fetched by a Companion wolf on Fetch (Swimming and cold water).

**Minecraft build.** `yearofice:sling` (`com.yearofice.hunt.SlingItem`, M5) has unlimited use duration. On release the server computes `cycles = useTicks / 12` and `phase = useTicks % 12`. A snap is `phase >= 8`, the last 4 ticks of the 0.6 s cycle. Power is `min(1, useTicks / 24)`. The server does the timing, so a modified client gains nothing. `yearofice:sling_stone` flies at 1.5 blocks a tick at full power, with gravity 0.0245 blocks a tick² (9.81 m/s²). A hit sets `stunned_until` on small game (100 ticks).

| Sound | Plays |
| --- | --- |
| `yearofice:item.sling_whirl` | Loop; pitch rises with phase, peaks in the snap window |
| `yearofice:item.sling_release` | Crack on release |
| `yearofice:item.sling_hit` | Thock on a hit |
| `yearofice:item.sling_miss` | Clatter or a soft thud in brush |

GameTest `yearofice:hunt/sling_snap` (batch `m5`): a release at phase 9 leaves at 1.2 ±0.01 times the speed of one at phase 4, at full power.

**Risk.** Spamming grouse. Guard: 3 kcal a throw, the hum, lost pebbles, and small-game local refill (Populations).

### Fish that see you

**What it is.** Fish notice the player. One viewer complained that salmon stayed put while the player jumped in among them, and another asked for fish that flee from sight and sound. Each fishing spot and each visible fish now has a wariness from 0 to 100. Spears, fish arrows and their refraction rule stay as written.

**What raises wariness.**

| Trigger | Range | Wariness added |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The player's shadow on the water, with sun above 15° and clear sky behind the player | 6 m from the spot | +40 |
| A splash: jumping or falling in, a dropped rock, a felled tree | 15 m | +60 |
| Wading | 4 m | +25 a game minute |
| Footsteps on gravel or shingle | 8 m | +10 a step running, +2 walking, 0 crouched |
| A missed spear thrust or fish arrow | The spot | +50 |
| A cast landing in the spot | The spot | +5, besides the existing nibble rule |
| Footsteps on ice over a hole | 10 m | +5 a step; snow on the ice halves it |

**Spooked at 50.** The fish leave the shallows for the deepest cell within 20 m. Visible fish dart away in a burst of wakes, rise rings stop, bites run ×0.3 and spear targets are gone.

**Settling.** Wariness falls 10 a game minute while the player is still and out of the fish's sight, and 5 while still but visible. When it drops under 30, the first rise ring returns: the cue that fish are back.

**Species differ.**

| Species | Wariness multiplier | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Whitefish | ×1.2 | Shy schooling fish in clear water |
| Grayling | ×1.0 | Sight feeders in clear creeks |
| Pike | ×0.6 | Ambush hunters that hold in weeds |
| Salmon in the run | ×0.4 | Driven upstream to spawn |
| Burbot | ×0.3 | Deep, at night, under ice |

**Counterplay.** Fish face upstream into the current, with a 120° sight cone forward. Approached from downstream and behind, every sight trigger above counts ×0.3. Crouching, keeping the sun in front and wearing drab clothes (Camouflage) all help.

**Spear escapes.** A thrust at an unaware fish misses on a twist 25% of the time, at a wary one (30–49) 50%, at a spooked one always. The spear's point loses 5 edge whenever a miss strikes gravel. Below 30 edge a hit kills only 70% of the time, because the fish twists free; the hone restores it.

**On screen and sound.** Wakes and a quick shadow-flicker when fish bolt. A small "plip" of a tail at the surface when they settle. In first person, the player's own shadow is drawn on the water, so the cause is visible.

**Ties.** Senses by species; Reading the water and spot radii; the spear and fish arrow; sun and weather; Practice ("Spear", N = 40, thrust time −40%); Camouflage; the salmon run.

**Studio detail (Ecosystem and hunting).** Fish that see you.

**Weather and light.**

| Condition | Shadow trigger | Sight triggers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clear sky, sun over 15° | Full | Full |
| Cloud over 50%, or sun under 15° | 0 | Full |
| Rain, or wind over 4 m/s (ripple) | 0 | ×0.5 |
| Night, no light | 0 | ×0.2 |
| Night, a pitch torch held over the water | 0 | ×0.5 for whitefish and grayling within 3 m, which it lights up |

Overcast drizzle is spearing weather, and night torch spearing for whitefish works, tying to Pitch lights.

**Clear ice.** On black ice under 15 cm with less than 2 cm of snow (October–November), the player is seen from below: each step within 6 m adds +10. Snow over 2 cm hides them.

**Driving fish.** A splash sends fish toward the deepest cell within 20 m. A gill net or weir across that path rolls ×2 for 1 game hour, the old method of beating the water. Once per spot per game hour.

**Educated spots.** A spot spooked 3 times in one game day starts the next day at wariness 20 and recovers after 3 days. Trophy fish (Waters and fish › Legendary and trophy fish) count every trigger ×2.

**Minecraft build.** `com.yearofice.ecosystem.fish.Wariness` keeps the value in the fish-spot record and the `yearofice:wariness` attachment on visible fish. Spots are scored once a second, only within 20 m of a player. `ShadowCaster` projects the player's box along the sun vector from `story.Sun`; the client draws the same shadow as a decal, so what the server counts is what the player sees. GameTests (batch `m5`): `yearofice:fish/approach_from_behind`, a crouched approach from downstream outside the fish's 120° cone adds 0.3 ±0.02 of what a head-on approach adds; `yearofice:fish/no_shadow_overcast`, the shadow adds 40 at 5 m in clear sky and 0 at 60% cloud.

**Risk.** Players standing still to reset. That is the intended skill: watching the water.

## Stalking, the kill and the haul

### Camouflage

**What it is.** What the player wears changes how far animals see them. A reply under the ideas list asked for camouflage beside blood trails, scent and noise; trails, scent and noise are already in Hunting in depth. Camouflage multiplies only the sight ranges in Senses by species, never smell or hearing.

**Outer-layer classes.** The torso and legs outer layers set the class; the worse of the two counts.

| Class | Examples | Sight range ×, in forest or tundra | × on snow ground |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bright | Dyed synthetic gear found at set pieces, tagged `#yearofice:bright_outer` | 1.3 | 1.3 |
| Neutral | Canvas, wool, new leather | 1.0 | 1.0 |
| Drab | Smoked hide, fur, wool worn faded (after 30 game days of wear) | 0.85 | 1.0 |
| Snow smock | A white over-smock sewn from 2 m² of canvas or flour sacking: sewing kit, 2 game hours | 1.4 | 0.7 |

**Brush-up.** E hold for 1 game minute tucks spruce sprigs or willow into the hat and pack straps. It gives ×0.8 in forest for 6 game hours, or until the player runs or swims.

**Stacking.** Multipliers multiply, with a floor of ×0.5. Movement still wins: a moving player is seen at the full adjusted range, a still one at half, as the existing rule says.

**The snow smock is seasonal.** It hides a winter hunter on snow and gives them away on bare ground. Between the first lasting snow and breakup it is the best stalking gear; worn in May it is the worst.

**On screen.** The body figure tints the outer layer by class. A brushed-up hat shows sprigs at the edge of view. Animals' alert distance shows only through behavior: a caribou that lifts its head later than before.

**Ties.** Senses by species; Clothing layers and wear; snow cover from the calendar; stalking and tree stands; Fish that see you.

**Studio detail (Ecosystem and hunting).** Camouflage.

**The formula.** Camouflage multiplies only into the moving-sight range; posture, light and cover then apply as written in Senses by species. The ×0.5 floor covers class × brush-up, not posture.

```latex
R_{sight} = R_{species} \cdot \max(0.5,\ c_{class} \cdot c_{brush}) \cdot m_{posture} \cdot m_{light} \cdot m_{cover}
```

**Edge rules.**

- **Snow ground** means at least 5 cm of snow under the player and within 10 m.
- **Snow-loaded forest.** For 48 game hours after a snowfall, until wind tops 6 m/s, the trees are white: the smock gives ×0.9 in forest instead of ×1.4.
- **Night.** Every class moves halfway to 1.0: bright ×1.15, smock on snow ×0.85.
- **Soiling.** Blood, soot or 10 game days of wear turn the smock Neutral until it is washed in warm water (30 game minutes).
- **Fish** use it too: wading and visible triggers in Fish that see you are multiplied by the class value.

**The real trade.** Bright gear from set pieces is the warmest outer layer in the valley. A winter hunter chooses between warmth and being seen.

**Who it matters for.** Dall sheep (300 m) and caribou (150 m) see farthest, so the smock on snow pays most in the mountains and on the tundra. Bears and moose find the player by smell first, and camouflage does nothing for that.

**Minecraft build.** `com.yearofice.hunt.Camouflage.sightMultiplier(player)` is cached on equipment change and refreshed every 20 ticks for ground snow; the `yearofice:sight` sensor reads it. Classes come from the data map `data/yearofice/data_maps/item/camo_class.json` plus the tags `yearofice:bright_outer` and `drab_outer`; wear days come from the clothing wear component. Ships M6. GameTest `yearofice:hunt/smock_by_season` (batch `m6`): a caribou's sight range for a walking player reads 105 ±1 m in a smock on snow, 210 ±1 m in the same smock on bare ground, and 150 ±1 m in neutral clothes.

**Risk.** None of note; the smock's summer penalty keeps it seasonal.

### Warmth from a fresh kill

**What it is.** A freshly killed animal is warm inside for hours, and northern hunters warm numb hands in the body cavity. One viewer suggested it; here it saves fingers and costs meat time.

**Core temperature.** Every carcass entity carries a core temperature that falls toward the air.

```latex
T_{core}(t) = T_{air} + (38 - T_{air}) \cdot e^{-t/\tau}
```

| Animal | τ unopened (game hours) |
| --- | --- |
| Hare, grouse | 1 |
| Beaver, fox, porcupine | 2 |
| Wolf, Dall sheep | 4 |
| Caribou | 8 |
| Black bear, grizzly | 10 |
| Moose | 14 |

Gutting multiplies τ by 0.6, and skinning and quartering by 0.25. While T\_core is 20 °C or more, the carcass is warm.

**What the warmth does.**

1. **Warm hands.** E hold at an opened cavity for 1 game minute. The hand zone counts +25 °C feels-like for 15 game minutes. It clears frostnip at once and counts as slow rewarming for superficial frostbite, with no burn risk, unlike a fire within 1 m.
2. **Thaw a thing.** A frozen bottle, a frozen boot or a frozen pelt laid in the cavity thaws at the inner-pocket rate of +20 °C.
3. **A windbreak.** Within 1 block of the lee side of a caribou, bear or moose carcass, the wind term of feels-like is halved.

**What it costs.**

- **Wet hands.** Each warm-hands use sets the hands' wetness to 60%. Out in −20 °C, wet hands freeze faster by the existing wet rule, so the player must dry or glove them within 10 game minutes.
- **Scent and blood.** Bloody hands until washed, as in The first-person body, and the carcass's scent point stays as strong.
- **The meat clock.** The heat that warms the hands is the heat that sours the meat. The Field care deadlines do not change, and the messenger's kill card adds one line while the core is above 20 °C: "Still warm inside: open it and cool it."

**A story it makes.** A December caribou kill at −32 °C, the player's hands at frostnip after gutting with bare fingers. They warm their hands in the cavity, glove up and finish the quarters before the 4-hour freeze. Without the rule, that kill costs a finger.

**On screen and sound.** Steam rises from an opened cavity below 0 °C, denser below −20 °C, and thins as the core cools. The first-person hands steam for a few seconds after. A soft breath of relief plays the first time frostnip clears this way.

**Ties.** Frostbite and rewarming; Field care deadlines; wet and the hand zone; scent; carcasses as bait.

**Studio detail (Ecosystem and hunting).** Warmth from a fresh kill. The carcass entity itself is specified in Bestiary › Carcasses and bodies; this adds the heat use.

**Warm windows, worked out.** Time until the core falls to 20 °C:

| Animal | At −10 °C, unopened | At −30 °C, unopened | At −30 °C, gutted |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hare | 28 game minutes | 18 game minutes | 11 game minutes |
| Caribou | 3.8 game hours | 2.5 game hours | 1.5 game hours |
| Moose | 6.6 game hours | 4.3 game hours | 2.6 game hours |

The December caribou in the story gets about 1.5 game hours of warm cavity after gutting.

**Edge rules.**

- **Small bodies run out.** Each warm-hands use takes heat: −0.5 °C of core on big game, −3 °C on a hare or grouse. A hare gives about 2 uses.
- **No crawling inside.** A gutted moose cavity holds about 0.3 m³, too small for a person. The game declines the idea.
- **Frozen solid.** Below 0 °C core, the cavity is ice and gives nothing.
- **Presets.** Brutal multiplies τ by 0.8.

**Minecraft build.** `com.yearofice.body.CarcassHeat` uses the closed form, not a tick: the core temperature is computed from the kill time when read, so 100 carcasses cost nothing. The `carcass entity's core_temp_c field (RECONCILE 41, 101)` caches it once a game minute for carcasses within 64 blocks of a player, for steam particles and the kill card. Warm hands is E hold on the cavity hitbox when `opened` is true. GameTest `yearofice:body/warm_hands` (batch `m6`): one use clears frostnip at once and holds the hand zone +25 °C feels-like for 15 ±0.5 game minutes.

### Game drag and hauling before snow

**What it is.** A way to move a big kill home without snow. The sled exists at the Hide-and-bone tier and pulls 120 kg on snow, and wolves pull it later, but the video's moose died in September on bare ground. The 821-like comment asked for exactly this, describing it as a boat on a lead; that is how the Minecraft build works.

**The travois.** Two 3 m spruce poles, 3 crossbars of 1 m and 8 m of cordage, lashed into an A-frame that drags its tail ends. Bushcraft tier, 1 game hour, 450 kcal, 6 kg. It holds 80 kg in 18 slots, and a green hide laid across it keeps meat clean.

**Pulling.** The player holds the drag rope in both hands, so no tool or bow is ready. A load L in kg multiplies the travel kcal by:

```latex
m = 1 + \frac{L}{40} \cdot f_{surface}
```

| Surface | f | Speed on a cut trail | Speed off trail |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wet moss, muskeg | 0.8 | 70% of walking | 50% |
| Meadow, grass | 1.0 | 70% | 50% |
| Forest floor | 1.2 | 70% | 45% |
| Gravel bar, rock | 1.6 | 60% | 40% |
| Snow | 0.5 | 80% | 60% |

Deadfall over 0.5 m high stops it. Lifting the drag over takes E hold for 10 real seconds and 20 kcal, which is why cut trails pay.

**A moose, worked through.** A bull gives 103 kg of meat, 8 kg of organs and a 30 kg green hide: 141 kg. That is 2 travois trips of about 70 kg, against 4 trips with the 45 kg pack frame. Over 1.5 km of forest floor off trail, each loaded trip costs about 375 × 3.1 ≈ 1,160 kcal and about 3.3 game hours.

**Other drags.**

| Drag | Made from | Holds | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hide drag | The fresh moose or caribou hide, folded hair-out, with 4 m cordage | 60 kg | Slides well on snow, wet grass and moss (f × 0.8); over 200 m of gravel it loses 20% of its leather |
| Sled on bare ground | The existing sled | 120 kg | Allowed at f = 2.0; runners take 1% wear per 100 m |
| Wolf travois | A small travois and a harness on one Companion wolf | 25 kg; 45 kg with two wolves | Walking pace in any season; the existing bolting rule below trust 90 applies |

**The drag line.** A loaded drag leaves two parallel furrows that stay visible for 3 game days. While it carries meat, the line is a scent trail of strength 1 for 6 game hours. A bear crossing it may follow it toward camp, so the player chooses between the fast route and the river gravel, where scent washes out.

**On screen and sound.** Poles scrape and thump over roots; the furrows show in moss and mud. The player's breathing grows heavy on the pull. In the rut, a drag line through the willows is the first thing a hunting bear finds.

**Ties.** Field care deadlines and the predator clock; carrying and weight bands; cut trails; scent; the sled and the wolf team; Taming a wolf.

**Studio detail (Ecosystem and hunting).** Game drag and hauling before snow.

**Terrain steps.** Minecraft slopes are 1-block steps. The towed entity steps up 1 block as the player does and treats terrain steps as slope: uphill, f is multiplied by (1 + 5 × grade), with grade taken over the last 10 m. Only felled logs and placed blocks in tag `yearofice:drag_obstacles` over 0.5 m stop it.

**Water.** A travois fords water up to 0.6 m deep at f = 0.6, and everything aboard gets wet. Deeper water or current over 1 m/s stops it. A hide drag floats across slow water (under 0.5 m/s) and is towed while the player wades or swims, the old way to move a moose over a slough.

**Snow by depth.** Under 10 cm, f is 0.8; at 10 cm and more it is 0.5.

**Hands and danger.** The rope fills both hands. Dropping it takes 0.5 s, so a ready spray is 1.5 s away instead of 1 s. Stumbles (Survival › Stumbles) run ×1.5 while pulling, ×2 at night.

**Bears and the line.** A bear whose path crosses a fresh drag line follows it toward its newer end: Gentle 15%, Standard 30%, Brutal 50%. One that reaches the travois is a bear on a carcass (Danger in detail).

**Wolf travois.** It needs a wolf that has learned Harness (Open world in depth › Taming a wolf). Each wolf eats 2 kg that day, as in harness.

**Loads.** Quarters, organs and hides go aboard as the items in Bestiary › Carcasses and bodies. A whole carcass goes aboard only when its body weight is within the drag's limit.

**Minecraft build.** `com.yearofice.travel.TowedVehicle`, ships M6, is a server-authoritative entity held 2.5 blocks behind the player's hip, with the vanilla lead rope render. It has no entity collision and opens its 18-slot menu with E when not towed. `travel.DragLine` writes a furrow decal every 2 m and a scent point every 16 m, about 94 points for 1.5 km, all gone after 6 game hours. GameTest `yearofice:travel/drag_obstacles` (batch `m6`): a 0.6 m felled log stops a loaded travois; a 0.4 m one and a 1-block terrain step do not.

**Risk.** Pulling becoming a chore. Guard: cut trails and the river gravel make the second trip faster, and the drag line keeps every trip tense.

## Body and health

Two comments with over 1,000 likes between them asked for sickness that shows and for hygiene that matters. Gut illness, infection, poisoning and soap already exist; these entries make sickness a visible event, add an emergency treatment and give cleanliness a number.

### Sick stomach

**What it is.** A nausea meter, a vomiting event, fever with chills, and recovery foods. It sits on top of the existing gut illness and plant poisonings and does not change their causes, timers or Condition losses.

**Nausea.** A hidden meter from 0 to 100. At 50 a swirl icon appears on the vitals strip and the view sways slightly.

| Source | Nausea added |
| --- | --- |
| Gut illness, first 12 game hours | +15 a game hour |
| Baneberry, death camas or fly agaric at onset | +100 at once |
| A fermented berry cup (Berry country) | +40 |
| More than 2,500 kcal in 1 game hour while BMI is under 18.5 | +60, the refeeding rule |
| A third cup of Labrador tea in a day | +50 |
| A fourth painkiller in a day | +30 |
| A fat-heavy meal (lard, pemmican, bear fat) during gut illness | +20 |

**Vomiting at 100.**

1. The player drops to their knees for 4 real seconds. Input is blocked and the close clock runs.
2. They lose 50% of the calories eaten in the last 3 game hours, and 0.5 L of water.
3. Nausea resets to 20, and Spirits fall 2.
4. **The purge.** Within 2 game hours of eating a poisonous plant, vomiting halves the Condition loss still to come.
5. The spot becomes a scent point of strength 1 for 6 game hours. Foxes and ravens come.

**Fever.** Infection stage 2 already causes fever; gut illness now does too on its first day. Fever raises water need by 1 L a day and cycles every 3 game hours.

- **Hot phase, 2 game hours.** The player sweats. Bundled up, the inner torso layer gains 10% wet an hour.
- **Chills, 1 game hour.** Feels-like counts 6 °C colder and the camera shivers.
- **Sleep.** Sleep recovery runs ×0.7 while feverish. Willow-bark tea removes fever for 8 game hours, as written.

**Recovery foods.** The viewer asked for soups, soft food and extra water, and the numbers reward that.

| Help | Effect during gut illness |
| --- | --- |
| Broth: stew liquid, fish-head soup, or bone broth (bones and 1.5 L water simmered 2 game hours for 1 L, 150 kcal) | Calories absorbed at 100% instead of 50%; its water counts double |
| Tea | Counts fully as water, with no nausea |
| A full night (6 game hours or more) in a bed | Shortens gut illness by 12 game hours |
| Drinking beyond the day's drain, with the water meter kept above 90 | Each extra litre shortens the illness by 4 game hours, up to 12 |

**Comfort setting.** Options hold "Sickness visuals": Full, Soft or Off. Soft replaces kneeling and retching with a screen dim and a cough. Off shows only a text line. One reply asked for this for players with a fear of vomiting; it is on the onboarding's first-run settings page.

**On screen and sound.** Nausea sway grows with the meter; a stomach gurgle plays at 50 and 80. Fever shows as a faint heat shimmer at the screen edge in the hot phase and the existing shiver in chills.

**Ties.** Gut illness and poisoning; calories and water in the body tick; Sleep; Cooking (broth); Berry country; remedies; scent.

**Studio detail (Body and survival).**

**How nausea falls.** With no source active it falls 10 an hour awake and 15 asleep. A cup of tea or broth takes 5 more off at once. Fresh air does nothing; time and fluids do.

**Vomiting, edge cases.**

| Where | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| On land | As written: kneel 4 real seconds |
| In a canoe | Lean over the side for 3 real seconds; no capsize roll unless the canoe is already rocking |
| Swimming | No kneel; the breath ring loses half; the stroke stops for 2 s |
| With a bear Alert within 50 m | The kneel still happens; the close clock runs; this is one of the game's worst moments, on purpose |
| Asleep | The player wakes, vomits, and loses 1 hour of sleep quality |

Vomiting empties the stomach to 0% (Every concept from the video › Stomach fill). It never removes the poison's own timer, only half its Condition loss inside 2 game hours.

**Fever, exact.** The hot phase raises the inner torso layer's wetness 10% an hour only when the player wears an outer torso layer; stripping to the inner layer stops it and costs warmth instead. Chills add −6 °C to feels-like, which the exposure line reads (Every concept from the video › Exposure time).

**Presets.** Nausea numbers are the same in every preset, so the bill is learnable. The Comfort setting changes only what is shown and heard.

**Feel.** At 50 the swirl appears and the view rolls ±1.5°; at 80 ±3° with a gurgle. At 100 the screen tips, the hands go to the ground, and the retch plays. Soft: the screen dims and a cough plays. The first vomit writes "Lost my breakfast and then some." The messenger never says why unless the plant is identified.

**Sounds.** `yearofice:body.stomach_gurgle`, `body.retch`, `body.vomit_splash`, `body.cough_soft`, `body.fever_breath`.

**Build.** `com.yearofice.body.Nausea` and `body.Fever` in `yearofice:body` (the separate `yearofice:nausea_level` attachment is folded into the body record, so one sync carries it). The sway is client-side in `ViewportEvent.ComputeCameraAngles`, scaled by the synced level and the Comfort setting. The mob effect `yearofice:nausea` only drives the icon. GameTest `yearofice:body/nausea_decay` (batch `m4`): nausea 60 with no source reaches under 10 in 5 game hours awake.

**Risk.** Vomiting can feel cruel. The guards are the meter (the player sees it coming), the purge's upside on poison, and the Comfort setting.

### Cauterizing and grime

**What it is.** An emergency way to stop bleeding with heat, and a grime meter that makes washing pay. Dressings, infection stages, soap and the banya stay as written.

**Cauterizing.** For a deep cut bleeding 3 Condition a game minute, with no dressing that stops it at once.

1. **Heat.** An iron knife or hatchet bit, or a flat dry stone, in a Hot fire or Steady coals for 2 game minutes. Iron shows "glowing"; a stone "spits" when water touches it. A wet stone may crack, by the hot-stones rule.
2. **Press.** E hold for 2 real seconds on the cut. The close clock runs.
3. **Result.** Bleeding stops at once. The cut becomes a bad burn: an open wound for 4 days under the infection rules. Condition −8 at once and Spirits −3.
4. **The blade pays.** Heating an iron knife to glowing draws its temper: −10 edge.
5. **Below 15 Condition.** After the press, there is a 50% chance of a 30 game minute blackout. The bleeding is already stopped, but the player lies where they are.

**When it is right.** Alone far from camp with no dressing, a deep cut loses 6 Condition while the blade heats, against 15 while dry moss works. A medkit bandage is always better, so cauterizing is the choice of a player who has used them up.

**Grime.** Each player has a grime value from 0 to 100, shown only as a word on the vitals screen ("Clean", "Grimy", "Filthy") and on the first-person hands.

| Raises grime | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| A game day spent mostly outdoors | +8 |
| Butchering big game | +15 |
| Cleaning a small animal or a fish | +5 |
| A day in indoor smoke over 50% | +10 |
| A fall into mud or bog | +20 |

| Lowers grime | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Washing in water at 10 °C or warmer, 5 game minutes | −50 |
| A bucket of warm water, 10 game minutes | −60 |
| Either with soap | To 0 |
| The banya | To 0 |

Washing in water below 10 °C uses the cold-water rules in Swimming and working in water, so a winter wash happens indoors.

**What grime does.**

- **60 and above (Grimy).** The dirty-dressing infection roll rises from 15% to 20%, and handling Turning meat has a 15% gut-illness chance instead of 10%. Scent +10%.
- **80 and above (Filthy).** Spirits −1 a day and mosquito bites ×1.2.
- **20 and below (Clean).** Cuts heal 10% faster, and Spirits +1 on the first clean day, at most every 3 days. This answers a reply that asked for an upside, not only a chore.

**On screen.** Hands darken at 60 and blacken at 80; fingernails show dirt first. A wash shows water running grey off the hands.

**Ties.** Infection and dressings; soap and the banya; scent in the hunt; Spirits; bugs; edge and the hone.

**Studio detail (Body and survival).**

**Heated tools.** A heated item carries a data component `yearofice:heat_c`. Glowing means 700 °C or more for iron; "spits" means 200 °C or more for stone. In air it cools below use heat in 1 game minute (iron) or 3 (stone). In snow or water it cools at once, with a hiss. A glowing knife held to pick up any other item burns the hand (minor burn).

**What can be cauterized.** Only a deep cut (stage 2). The prompt never shows on a minor cut, so it is never the lazy fix. It can be done on oneself only, until the co-op decision.

**Costs and helpers.**

| Factor | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| A painkiller taken within 30 game minutes before | Spirits −1 instead of −3; blackout chance halved |
| Usnea or yarrow on the burn after | Infection roll halved for 4 days |
| Spruce-pitch salve on the burn after | Takes 1 day off the 4 |
| Grime 60 or more | Infection roll 20% instead of 15% on the burn's dressing |
| A Bonded wolf near | It whines and lies down beside the player during any blackout; no animal can approach unseen |

**Grime, edge rules.** Day grime (+8) is added at 00:00 if 12 of the last 24 game hours were outdoors. Fish cleaning adds at most +15 a game hour, however many fish. Rain on an uncovered player lowers grime by 5 a game hour, never below 30. Rubbing with snow lowers it by 20 and costs hand warmth 10. Swimming in water at 10 °C or warmer for 5 game minutes counts as a wash.

**Feel.** The hot blade glows orange in first person and smokes faintly. The press plays a hiss and a held breath, the screen flashes white, and the hand trembles for 30 real seconds. The journal writes "Burned it shut." Grimy hands darken under the nails first; a wash plays a splash and grey water drips.

**Sounds.** `yearofice:body.sear`, `body.sharp_breath`, `item.hot_quench`, `body.wash`.

**Build.** `com.yearofice.body.Cauterize` (action `data/yearofice/action/cauterize.json`); `com.yearofice.body.Grime` writes `grime` into `yearofice:body`, which replaces the separate `yearofice:grime` attachment. The hand overlay is three textures in `client.body.HandGrimeLayer`. GameTest `yearofice:body/cauterize_stops_bleeding` (batch `m4`): a deep cut stops on the press, a bad burn starts, and the knife loses 10 edge.

**Risk.** Grime could become a chore. The guard: it shows only as a word and on the hands, rain and swimming wash it, and Clean pays a bonus.

### The pocket stone

**What it is.** A heated stone carried in the coat, as one viewer suggested. It joins the Warm item state from the items tab and the hot-water bottle, which stays the sleeping warmer.

**Making it.** A fist-sized dry stone of 0.4 kg heats beside a fire, not in it, for 20 game minutes. It must be wrapped in a hide scrap or cloth before carrying; an unwrapped hot stone gives a minor burn.

**Carrying.** It goes in one of the two inner-pocket slots. At most 2 stones count.

| Use | Effect | Lasts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Carried | Torso feels-like +2 °C | 90 game minutes, fading evenly to nothing |
| Hands in pockets (walking with the main hand and off-hand both empty for 10 real seconds) | Hand zone +6 °C | Same stone, same clock |
| In the bed at night | +2 °C asleep | 3 game hours, below the hot-water bottle's +3 °C for 4 |

**Why it matters.** It is the cheapest warmth on a winter trapline: two stones from the morning fire buy 90 minutes of safe hands below −25 °C. It costs 0.8 kg and a fire stop to reheat.

**On screen and sound.** The stone's icon glows orange while Warm and fades. A soft clack plays when two stones touch in the pocket.

**Ties.** Warm items and the inner pocket; Clothing zones and safe-to temperatures; frostbite; fire.

**Studio detail (Body and survival).**

**How it fades.** The stone fades from Warm to the inner pocket's +20 °C over 90 game minutes; the effect falls linearly with it. Two stones give the torso +4 °C and the hands +12 °C at the start (both stones are in the pockets, so both count). A stone in the pack or on the hotbar cools in 20 game minutes and gives nothing.

**Making it, edge rules.**

- Any fist-sized stone (`yearofice:pocket_stone`, 0.4 kg) from a gravel bar or rock face. A wet stone can crack by the hot-stones rule (Blueprint › Fire, water and light), so it is dried 10 game minutes at the fire edge first.
- Too close (inside the ring) heats it to "spitting hot": unwrapped it burns, wrapped it scorches the wrap (a hide scrap lasts 20 heatings, cloth 8).
- Reheating needs a fire at Steady or Hot, or a stove; coals take 30 game minutes instead of 20.

**Hands in pockets.** The player must walk with both hands empty for 10 real seconds; the hands slide out of view. Any action takes them out in 0.5 s. A walking stick in the off-hand blocks it, so the trapline walker chooses between stick and warm hands.

**Numbers on a trapline.** At −30 °C feels-like in the starting mittens, the hands' frostnip comes in about 30 game minutes. Two fresh stones in the pockets raise the hand zone by 12 °C, which pushes frostnip past 90 game minutes for most of the stones' life (Every concept from the video › Exposure time shows it).

**Presets.** The same in all presets.

**Feel.** The icon glows orange and dims. Pulling hands in plays a rustle of coat cloth; two stones clack. The first warm stone of winter writes "Two stones in the coat. Warm hands for an hour."

**Ties.** Warm items and the inner pocket (Items tab); Clothing zones; frostbite; fire settings; the walking stick (Ideas from viewers).

**Build.** `yearofice:pocket_stone` with the existing temperature component; the Curios slot `yearofice:inner_pocket` reads it; the hands-in-pockets check is in `com.yearofice.body.Warmth`. GameTest `yearofice:body/pocket_stone_fade` (batch `m4`): one stone gives +2.0 °C torso at minute 0, +1.0 ±0.1 at minute 45 and 0 at minute 90.

**Risk.** Players may feel forced to carry stones every winter day. The guard: they cost both inner-pocket slots, where the messenger and batteries also want to be.

## Travel, light and the world

Four smaller ideas, each with few likes but a clear place in the systems: a walking stick, lights from spruce pitch, the glow viewers wanted to keep, and finding an old camp.

### The walking stick

**What it is.** A peeled sapling carried in the off-hand. One viewer called walking sticks overpowered in real life; here they help on slopes, in water, on ice and with a bad leg, and they cost the off-hand.

**Making it.** Start tier: a straight willow, birch or alder sapling 1.5 m long, cut and peeled with the knife in 10 game minutes, 0.6 kg. A carved staff (Woodcraft, 1 game hour) lasts 3 times as long. A tin-can ferrule on the tip (5 game minutes) bites into ice.

**Effects while it is in the off-hand.**

| Situation | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Slopes over 15° | Walking kcal −5%; slip chance halved, including the low-energy slip rule |
| Wading | Current push −30%; RMB tap ahead reads the depth 2 m away: "ankle", "knee", "thigh", "too deep" |
| Ice | RMB tap on ice within 2 m: under 5 cm a hollow tock and a crack line; 5–10 cm a dull dok; over 10 cm a solid thud. With the ferrule, the reading is also shown as text |
| A sprain or blister | The walk-speed penalty is halved: a sprain's half speed becomes 75% |
| A splinted leg | Walking 60% becomes 70% |
| Deep snow on snowshoes | +10% speed, from balance |
| Step-up | Walking (not running, load under 30 kg), the player steps up 1 full block instead of 0.6, for 1 kcal a step |
| A Curious bear | Raised high (RMB hold) adds 10% to the existing "stand on something high" chance |

**What it costs.** The off-hand holds the stick, so the torch, lantern and bear spray must sit on the hotbar. Spray from the hotbar readies in 1 s, as written, so the trade is light: no lantern in the left hand.

**Wear.** A sapling stick breaks after 60 game hours of use, or on a fall over 3 m. The staff lasts 180.

**On screen and sound.** The stick swings into view at each step and plants with a tap. On ice, the three sounds are distinct enough to learn by ear. In water, a ripple shows where the tip probes.

**Ties.** Travel kcal and slopes; thin ice in Danger in detail; wading and current; injuries; snowshoes; bear encounters; the off-hand slot.

**Studio detail (World and weather).**

**Conflict fixed: step height.** The engine already sets the player's step height to 1.0 on terrain. So the stick's step-up applies to blocks that are not terrain steps: felled logs, deadfall, boulders and low pieces. Without the stick, those need a jump (5 kcal). With it, they are a step (1 kcal). The step-up uses the `minecraft:step_height` attribute only while the target block is not in `yearofice:terrain_steps`.

**Two-handed actions.** Drawing a bow, chopping, paddling, hold-to-work and hand-carrying tuck the stick under the arm. Its effects pause and nothing drops. Effects return 0.5 s after the action ends.

**Exact readings.**

| Probe | Reading |
| --- | --- |
| Water under 0.2 m | "Ankle" |
| 0.2–0.5 m | "Knee" |
| 0.5–0.9 m | "Thigh" |
| Over 0.9 m | "Too deep": the crossing rule's limit |
| Ice under snow over 10 cm | Muffled; clearing the snow takes 2 s, then a normal tap |

**Wear counts only while walking with it** in the off-hand, not while it sits in the pack.

**Seasons make the choice.** In summer, the stick costs almost nothing. In December, with 20 hours of dark, the off-hand is where the lantern goes. Players switch at the equinox, and the journal notices once: "Swapped the stick for the lantern."

**Not a weapon.** LMB with the stick parts brush (sight through 1 block of willow for 2 s). It deals no damage and gives no knockback.

**First time.** The first tap on ice shows the caption "\[Ice: solid thud\]" with the three tones listed. After 5 taps, captions drop to the sound alone.

### Pitch lights

**What it is.** Lights made from spruce resin, after a reply that iron lanterns and glass are out of reach in the bush. Tallow candles, fat lamps, birch-bark torches, fatwood splints and the mining camp's tin lantern stay as written. Pitch lights burn smoky and carry no food scent.

**Gathering pitch.** Hardened pitch scrapes off old spruce wounds at 50 g per 10 game minutes, once per tree each season. Blazing a living spruce with the hatchet (1 game minute) makes it weep 150 g over 14 days above 10 °C. Blazed trees show a pale scar and are pinned on the map like patches. The canoe's 3 kg and the salve's 50 g come from the same trees.

| Light | Made from | Make time | Burns | Light radius | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pitch candle | 100 g pitch, a twisted fiber or moss wick, a folded birch-bark cup or a tin | 10 game minutes at a fire | 3 game hours | 2 m | Indoors adds 0.5% smoke a game minute |
| Pitch torch | A stick, a birch-bark wrap, 80 g pitch | 5 game minutes | 30 game minutes | 4 m | Holds in wind to 12 m/s; wolves keep their distance, as for the bark torch |
| Pitch in the tin lantern | A pitch candle | — | 3 game hours | 4 m | Smoke vents; carried in the left hand |

**Why choose pitch.** Tallow needs fat, which winter is short of, and it smells of food. Pitch costs only a walk to the blazed trees. Its smoke makes it a porch, cache and meat-shed light, and a cabin light only with a chimney or an open door: at 0.5% a minute, smoke passes 30% in an hour.

**On screen and sound.** Pitch flames burn yellow with a black smoke thread and crackle and spit. Blazed trunks glisten with fresh resin in summer.

**Ties.** Fire and light sources; smoke indoors; the salve and canoe; trees and pinning; scent; wolves and fire.

**Studio detail (World and weather).**

**Weather and cold.**

| --- | --- | --- |

**Fire danger.** A carried pitch torch drips burning pitch. At High and above, walking on dry fuel with it gives a 0.5% spark per game minute, under the Fire danger rules (Video tab).

**Blazing limits.** A tree can be blazed once a year. The third blaze kills it in 2 years: it dies standing and becomes dry firewood at 18%, like girdling.

**Feel.** The blazed scar glistens and slowly turns amber over 14 days. The scrape takes a 10-game-minute hold with the knife and a soft scratch sound (`yearofice:work.scrape_resin`). The candle gutters and spits (`fire.pitch_spit`) every 20–40 real seconds.

### Foxfire and holdover fires

**What it is.** The real version of the glowing leaves viewers liked, plus the glow-and-fire link one comment asked for. Foxfire is fungus that glows faintly in rotting wood. Holdover fires are burns that smolder in peat under the snow all winter and relight in spring, which interior Alaska sees after big fire years.

**Foxfire.** From August 1 to September 30, after 2 rainy days in 5, 15% of rotten birch and aspen logs in moist birch slopes and riparian forest glow.

- **The glow.** Cosmetic, seen within 30 m on nights with no moon. In Minecraft it is an emissive texture with block light 1, so it marks a spot without lighting the way.
- **Chips.** Splitting a glowing log (hatchet, 2 game minutes) gives 6 foxfire chips that glow for 2 nights while damp. Chips set on the ground mark a night trail for players whose messenger battery is flat.
- **Grubs.** A glowing log holds ×2 grubs (Grubs and ant brood).
- **Spirits.** +2 the first glowing log each year. Arvid Lund's logbook calls it "the trapper's lantern."

**Holdover fires.** After a year with the wildfire event, each burned region rolls 1 in 3 for 1–3 smoldering peat cells.

1. **Winter signs.** From November to April, a melt hole in the snow with a thin smoke thread, seen 500 m away in still air, and a faint orange glow in it at night. Snow around it turns grey.
2. **Warm ground.** Within 1 block of the hole, feels-like is +10 °C. A wolf curls there; a player can thaw a frozen item there.
3. **Spring.** When the 5-day mean passes 10 °C after 7 dry days, each cell relights the burn edge with a 20% chance, by the forest fire spread rules.
4. **Putting it out.** Digging it out takes 1 game hour with a shovel, or 20 L of water poured in. The messenger warns once in April: "Smoke in the old burn."

**On screen and sound.** Foxfire shows as soft green-white streaks along a log's cracks. A holdover cell hisses when snow falls into it.

**Ties.** Rotten wood and grubs; night navigation and the messenger battery; the wildfire event and fire spread; morels and berries after a burn; Spirits; the wolf.

**Studio detail (World and weather).**

**When foxfire shows.** "No moon" means the moon below the horizon or within 3 days of new moon. From August 1 the nights get dark again after the midnight sun, so foxfire has about 4–6 dark weeks a year. Each log rolls once per season. A log that glows keeps glowing until September 30, or until it freezes below −5 °C for 2 nights.

**Chips.**

### Needle tea and inner bark

**Steep, don't boil.** The vitamin C table gives spruce-tip tea +1 and old-needle tea half. Needles must be chopped and steeped off the boil: pull the pot from the fire for 2 game minutes, then steep 10. Needles boiled hard for 10 game minutes give 0, and the tea card says "boiled too long."

**Interior trees.** The valley has white and black spruce, and subalpine fir in the mountain regions; fir needle tea counts as old spruce needles. There are no pines, which answers the pine-tea comments.

**Inner bark.** From May 15 to June 30, the inner bark of spruce and birch peels in long strips: 250 g in 10 game minutes, 150 kcal, +0.3 vitamin C raw. Dried strips keep 1 year and are pounded to flour in the mortar. Peeling a full ring girdles the tree: it dies standing in 2 years and becomes dry firewood. A player can plant their own firewood that way.

**Studio detail (Body and survival).**

**Steeping, exact.** The pot's water temperature is tracked: off the boil it falls about 5 °C a game minute. Needles give full value when steeped between 70 and 90 °C. Each game minute above 95 °C after the needles go in removes 10% of the value, so 10 minutes on a hard boil gives 0. The tea card reads "Steeped well," "A little stewed" or "Boiled too long."

| Tea | Best | Value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spruce tips (May–June) | Steeped 10 min at 70–90 °C | +1 vitamin C |
| Old spruce needles | Same | +0.5 |
| Subalpine fir needles | Same | +0.5 |
| Any of them boiled 10 min | — | 0 |

**Inner bark, per face.** A trunk block has 4 faces. Peeling one face (250 g, 10 game minutes) leaves the tree alive; it shows a pale scar for 5 years. Peeling all 4 faces of any trunk block girdles it: it stands dead after 2 years, its logs dry to under 20% moisture (Every concept from the video › Firewood drying) and it is a snag for the felling rules. Peeling outside May 15 – June 30 gives 50 g of stringy bark, no food.

**Edge cases.** Birch inner bark is sweeter (+1 Spirits the first strip each year). Inner bark flour bakes with lard into a dense bannock, 300 kcal per 100 g. Peeling with grime 60 or more makes no difference; it is cooked or dried before most uses.

**Feel.** The knife scores a ring, the bark lifts in a wet, slapping strip, and the cambium shines white. A girdled tree browns over its second summer. The first girdling writes "Ringed a spruce. Firewood in two years."

**Ties.** Vitamin C reserve and scurvy; firewood drying; felling (Blueprint › Danger in detail); the stone mortar.

**Build.** Peeled faces as a block state `peeled` 0–4 on `yearofice:spruce_log` and `birch_log` trunks; girdled trees in the region SavedData by base position, converted on `ClockEvent.Day` after 730 days. Tea temperature in the pot's block entity. GameTest `yearofice:forage/needle_tea_boil` (batch `m5`): steeped at 80 °C, +1; boiled 10 game minutes, 0.

### Bears as viewers asked

**Swimming.** Bears swim at 1.5 m/s, faster than the player's 1.0 crawl. A river is no escape from a Chasing bear, and the onboarding never suggests it.

**The falls gathering.** In the 7-day peak of the salmon run, 2–4 grizzlies gather at the canyon falls. The largest takes the lip of the falls; younger bears are pushed to the tail-outs below. The zone within 150 m is Defensive for every bear in it. The messenger warns once: "Bears gathering at the falls."

**Dens.** Bear dens lie on north-facing slopes and are dug into banks. From November to March a den shows as a small frosted hole where breath escapes. Within 20 m, a player wakes the bear 5% of the time a game hour in December and January and 25% in March, and a woken bear is Defensive. Dens hold no loot; the viewer's "dead person in a cave" is declined.

**Studio detail (Ecosystem and hunting).** Bears as viewers asked.

**Swimming.** One table now serves both tabs: grizzly 1.5 m/s, black bear 1.4 m/s (Every concept from the video › Bears in water).

**The falls gathering.**

| Spot | Fish a game hour at peak | Who holds it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The lip | 6 | The heaviest bear |
| The plunge pool | 4 | The second |
| Tail-outs | 2 | Younger bears, sows with cubs |

- The gathering is 2–4 grizzlies drawn from the region's count, so a player who has killed grizzlies sees fewer.
- An arriving bear that outweighs a holder takes its spot. 10% of these moves are fights: roars heard 300 m away, and the loser leaves with a shoulder wound (Wounds that last).
- Black bears come only at night, while the grizzlies bed.
- From 12:00 to 14:00 the bears bed in alders within 300 m. A bedded bear surprised within 50 m is Defensive.
- Watching from the canyon rim at 150–250 m, downwind, is safe. The first watch gives Spirits +5 and a journal line.

**Dens.**

- Grizzly dens are dug into north banks steeper than 20°. Black bears also den under root wads and windfalls on flat ground (Wildlife and fish).
- Bears go in around November 1 (±10 days, on the first lasting snow) and come out April 15 – May 15.
- Wake chance doubles for loud work within 20 m (axe, sled team, a fire) and halves for a crouched player.
- A woken bear returns to the den within 1 game hour if left alone. A bear woken twice leaves the den for good. It becomes a winter bear: it roams, is Predatory 30% of the time and dies by March 50% of the time.
- A Bonded wolf raises its hackles at a den within 50 m.

**Minecraft build.** `yearofice:bear_den` is a generated 2 × 2 opening into a 3 × 3 × 2 space, placed per bear in the region file. While occupied, the bear is a record, not an entity, until woken. Breath particles `yearofice:den_breath` puff every 6 s from November to March. A player stepping into an occupied opening wakes the bear every time. Falls spots come from `worldgen.Falls` as ranked slots. GameTest `yearofice:ecosystem/den_wake_rate` (batch `m7`): over 10,000 simulated December hours at 15 m, the wake rate is 5% ±0.5%; at 15 m crouched, 2.5% ±0.4%.

### Trap catch shock

**A catch spoils the spot for a while.** Local refill in Populations already empties a range after a catch. Added: a snared or trapped animal's struggle and scent make same-species catch chance ×0.5 at every trap within 50 m for 2 nights. A dense cluster of snares catches less than a spaced line, so the trapline walk stays.

**Studio detail (Ecosystem and hunting).** Trap catch shock.

**Strength by trap.**

| Catch | Shock (same species within the radius) | Radius |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cordage snare holding a live animal | ×0.5 | 50 m |
| Wire snare (instant kill) | ×0.6 | 50 m |
| Spring pole | ×0.6 | 30 m |
| Deadfall | ×0.7 | 30 m |

- Shocks from several catches multiply, floored at ×0.25.
- They last 4 roll periods, 2 dawns and 2 dusks. Fresh snow over 5 cm or rain over 5 mm clears them early, because it buries the scent.
- Applies to snares, deadfalls, cubbies and noose poles; never to nets, fish traps or tip-ups.

**The line it teaches.** At 100 m spacing (Running the line) shock almost never overlaps. At 30 m spacing a 20-trap cluster should catch about a third less in a middle hare year; the balance sim confirms the figure.

**Readable, not a leak.** The placement ghost says "Catch scent nearby" only for a catch the player has already checked, so the rule never reveals an unseen catch. The journal notes it after the third case: "Two snares side by side, and the second one went quiet after the first caught."

**Minecraft build.** Shock records (species, position, radius, multiplier, periods left) live in `yearofice_region_<cell>`, written on `TrapEvent.Caught` and read by each roll as `m_shock` in the catch formula. Ships M6. GameTest `yearofice:traps/catch_shock` (batch `m6`): with one hare in range of two snares 20 m apart, a catch in one cuts the other's rate ×0.5 ±0.03 for the next 4 rolls only.

### Raw mushrooms

**Cook them.** Any edible mushroom eaten raw gives 25% of its kcal. Birch bolete and shaggy mane eaten raw add a 10% chance of 1 day of gut illness; the orange-cap bolete's undercooked rule stays. Cooking never makes fly agaric, false morel or poison pax safe, and the inspect card's cut check never says otherwise.

**Studio detail (Body and survival).**

**Every mushroom, raw and cooked.**

| Mushroom | Season | Raw | Cooked | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Morel | Spring after a fire | 25% kcal; 30% gut illness | Full; +3 Spirits | Raw morels are mildly toxic in life too |
| Birch bolete | July–September | 25% kcal; 10% gut illness 1 day | Full | Under birch |

**Cooked means.** Roasted on a stick or stone 5 game minutes, fried, or stewed. Half-cooked (stopped early) counts as raw.

### High-graded salmon

**Bears eat the best part.** During the salmon run's peak, a feeding bear eats only the skin, brain and roe of 70% of its fish. Each leftover drops on the bank as 1.5 kg of fillet meat, fresh for 6 game hours. Gulls, eagles and ravens take a leftover within 2 game hours, and each is a scent point of strength 1. Collecting them out of the bear's sight is the existing "Grizzly at the falls" choice, now with numbers.

**Studio detail (Ecosystem and hunting).** High-graded salmon.

**Numbers.** A feeding bear at peak takes 6 fish a game hour at the lip, so it leaves about 4 leftovers an hour. Once birds have found the falls, each leftover has a 50% chance a game hour of being taken, so 75% are gone within 2 hours. With 3 bears the bank holds about 10–15 leftovers at any time.

**Where they lie.** Leftovers drop 5–30 m from each feeding spot, inside the 150 m Defensive zone. The safe windows are the midday bedding (12:00–14:00, with bedded bears in the alders) and the minutes after a bear moves to a new spot.

**What a leftover is.** Skinless fillet of that run's species, with the kcal from Waters and fish. With no skin it cannot hang skin-on: it dries as strips, 0.8 times the usual rack time. It spoils at summer rates, so 6 game hours is the limit.

**Ties.** Spawned-out salmon feed the valley; the eagles and ravens in the Bestiary; scent points draw more bears.

**Minecraft build.** `yearofice:salmon_leftover` is a thin ground block with a block entity holding species, weight and drop minute, at most 20 per falls. A bird flock entity removes them by the hourly roll. Loot `data/yearofice/loot_table/gameplay/high_graded_salmon.json`. Ships M7. GameTest `yearofice:ecosystem/high_grade_rate` (batch `m7`): over 1,000 bear meals at peak, 70% ±3% leave a leftover.

**Risk.** Turning the falls into a fish shop. Guard: only the 7-day peak, inside the Defensive zone, with a 6-hour shelf life.

### One blow for a fish

**The club.** A fish priest is a birch club of 0.3 kg, carved with the knife in 5 game minutes. One blow to a landed fish's head kills it, so it cannot flip back into the water. It also starts the kill-and-bleed step in Handling the catch. Without a club, a rock takes 2 blows. A hand-held fish that is not killed counts as alive for the 4-second flip-back rule.

**Studio detail (Ecosystem and hunting).** One blow for a fish.

**Blows by size.**

| Fish weight | Club | Rock |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Under 5 kg | 1 | 2 |
| 5–15 kg (big pike, king salmon) | 2 | 4 |
| Over 15 kg (trophy pike, sheefish) | 3 | 6 |

- A flopping fish dodges 20% of swings at practice 0 and 5% at Practiced ("Club", N = 30).
- A pike handled alive bites 10% of the time: −2 Condition and a cut hand.
- On ice more than 2 m from the hole the flip-back rule does not apply, and below −20 °C a landed fish freezes still within 3 game minutes.
- The club also kills small game held alive in a cordage snare with one blow.
- It lasts 300 blows. Any 0.3 kg hardwood stick works as a rough club with 2 blows for every size.

**Minecraft build.** `yearofice:fish_priest` is a hand-carving recipe (`data/yearofice/recipe/carving/fish_priest.json`). A landed fish entity in the `flop` state counts 80 ticks to flip back; an attack with the club counts blows against a size threshold from the species file. Noise 5 m. Ships M5. GameTest `yearofice:fish/one_blow` (batch `m5`): a 1 kg grayling dies on the first landed club hit in 100 of 100 trials; a 10 kg pike needs exactly 2.

### Snow sounds by temperature

**Loose snow, by the thermometer.** The crusted-snow rule stays (2.5, every step squeaks below −15 °C). Loose snow now follows the cold too.

| Air | Sound | Noise factor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Above −5 °C | Soft crunch | 1.0 |
| −5 to −15 °C | Crunch | 1.5 |
| −15 to −30 °C | Squeak | 2.0 |
| Below −30 °C | Loud squeak; voices and axe blows carry 30% farther | 2.5 |

The player learns the temperature by ear, and a hunter learns when not to stalk.

**Studio detail (World and weather).**

**More modifiers on top of the band.**

| Condition | Factor |
| --- | --- |
| Fresh snow within 6 game hours of a snowfall | ×0.8: new snow muffles |
| Snowshoes | ×0.8 |
| Sneaking (crouch walk) | ×0.5 |
| Leaf litter under 1 layer of snow (fall) | ×0.9 |
| Wind over 6 m/s | Animal hearing range ×0.7 |

**Hysteresis.** A band changes only when the air passes its edge by 1 °C, so steps do not flicker between crunch and squeak at −15 °C.

**The inversion matters.** Air temperature is read at the player's feet. On a clear calm winter night the river flats can be 8 °C colder than a bench 100 m up (Blueprint › Calendar, daylight and weather). The flats squeak while the bench crunches, and a hunter hears the difference walking down.

**Carrying in deep cold.** Below −30 °C, the 30% extra range applies through the distant-sound path to the player's sounds and to animals' and wolves' howls. It applies both ways.

**Sound set.** `yearofice:step.snow_soft`, `snow_crunch`, `snow_squeak`, `snow_squeak_loud`, each with 6 variants and ±8% pitch, and `step.crust_break`. A pack over 30 kg plays the next-louder variant.

**First time.** The first squeak step gives the caption "\[Snow squeaks\]" and the journal: "The snow squeaks. It's below −15." The player learns the band by ear.

**Ties.** Senses (Minecraft engine › Senses, scent and wind) reads the factor into `noise_m`. Winter stalking of moose and caribou. Walking stick (above).

**Build.** `client.sound.Footsteps` picks the sound; the server's `world.SnowNoise` gives the factor from `env_sync` air temperature. Ships in M7.

**GameTest (m7).** `senses/snow_noise_bands`: a walking player is heard by a moose at 1.0×, 1.5×, 2.0× and 2.5× of base range at −2, −10, −20 and −35 °C.

**Risk.** No stalking all winter. Guard: snowshoes, sneaking, fresh snow and wind give 4 ways to cut the noise.

### Snag knock-down

**A falling tree can take a dead one with it.** A felled tree whose fall line strikes a dead standing snag within its length knocks the snag down 60% of the time. Living trees still only hang it up, as Practice says. A knocked snag falls in its own direction, ±30° from the push, and a chain stops at 2 trees. It is a widowmaker: anyone in its path takes the falling-tree rule.

**Studio detail (World and weather).**

**What counts as a snag.** Any dead standing tree: `yearofice:snag_log` (beaver ponds), burn-ridge snags, girdled trees, `struck_spruce` and trees killed by repeated blazing.

**The fall preview warns.** The felling preview arc turns red over any snag in the fall line, with the line "Dead tree in the fall line." Practice "felling" at level 2 shows the knocked snag's likely fall cone of ±30°.

**Snags break.** 30% of knocked snags snap at a third of their height. Only the top falls, which is the classic widowmaker, and the stub keeps standing. A pop sound (`yearofice:tree.snag_pop`) plays 0.5 s before a knocked snag moves: the last chance to step back.

**Wind brings them down.** In wind over 12 m/s, each snag within 64 blocks of a player falls with 1% chance per Severe day, in the downwind direction.

**Camp warning.** Placing a bed, tent or lean-to within one tree length of a snag gives the prompt "Dead tree over camp." The player can still place it.

**Damage.** A snag uses the falling-tree table (Video tab › Logs, falls and jams) by its diameter. A snapped top uses the crown row.

**Ties.** Firewood drying (Video tab): snags are dry standing wood at 18%. Thunderstorms (struck spruce). Beavers, ponds and thaw (Water, calls and encounters).

**Build.** The `falling_tree` entity sweeps its arc each tick against blocks in tag `yearofice:snags_standing`. A hit spawns a second `falling_tree` with the rolled yaw; the chain depth is held on the entity. Wind blowdown runs on `ClockEvent.Day` for loaded chunks and in closed form on load. Ships in M8.

**GameTests (m8).** `world/snag_knock_rate`: 1,000 felled trees whose fall lines cross a snag knock it 60% ±3%; living trees are never knocked. `world/snag_chain_cap`: a line of 5 snags stops after 2.

**Risk.** Unfair deaths. Guard: the red preview, the pop sound and the camp prompt.

## Raising animals

Viewer idea 26, "husbandry of wild animals", is adapted, not declined. Animals come to the homestead in two ways: domestic stock flown in by supply drop, and orphaned wild young the player raises. Wolves stay the only animal that becomes a true working companion. Everything else is livestock or a camp animal that stays half wild.

### Livestock by supply drop

Livestock is ordered over the radio like any other drop, at the prices in the radio supply catalogue (Open world in depth › The radio), which is the single source for prices, paid in research points (100 a season, credit capped at 200; see The radio). It is flown in only once the player has the shelter it needs, because Pete Lund won't land animals at a lean-to.

| Order | Points | Needs before it is flown in | What it gives | Daily care | Main risks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3 hens and a rooster | 100 | A hen house with a roost, a nest box and a door that shuts | 2–3 eggs a day from May to August, 70 kcal each; none in the dark months unless a fat lamp gives 14 hours of light and the coop stays above 0 °C; a broody hen hatches 6–8 chicks in 21 days | 150 g of grain or 200 g of fish meal per bird; water that isn't frozen | Foxes, lynx, marten, owls and wolves take loose birds; frostbitten combs below −20 °C in an unheated coop |
| 2 goats, a doe and a buck | 150 | A goat pen with a three-sided shed and 200 kg of hay or browse stored | 1.5–2 L of milk a day for 8 months after kidding (650 kcal per litre, some vitamin C fresh); 1–2 kids each May; hide, meat and fat | 2 kg of willow, birch browse or hay each; 4 L of water; let out to browse 4 hours a day in summer | Wolves and bears at an open pen; hay running out in March; frozen water |
| 2 reindeer (Homestead tier only) | 180 | A 20 × 20 block fenced lichen paddock and a winter shelter | Pack animal for 35 kg, or a small sled for 90 kg on snow; antler and hide; a calf most springs | 3 kg of lichen or hay each; let out to graze daily | Wolves single them out; a loose reindeer walks off with wild caribou in the fall migration |

Reindeer herding is real Alaskan history, so the radio calls them "Pete's last favour": they arrive only after 2 years of steady research logs.

**Care rules.** Each animal has three needs, fed, watered and warm, each from 0 to 100. Below 30 on any of them it stops producing; below 10 it loses Condition; at 0 for 2 game days it dies. Pens and coops count as warm rooms, so the same heat model decides if a coop stays above freezing. A full feed trough holds 3 days of feed and a water trough 2 days, so the player can leave for a short trip. A long trip needs a full hay store and an outpost close enough to come back.

**Predators know.** A pen is a scent point. Wolves test it on winter nights and bears visit in spring. Each night with predators in the region rolls an attack chance from the fence: 25% for brush, 8% for poles, 2% for a log stockade, and ×0.3 with a Bonded wolf sleeping near the pen. The attack shows up the next morning as tracks, torn fence and a missing animal.

**Studio detail (Ecosystem and hunting).** Livestock care loops.

**The daily round.** A full yard takes about 30 game minutes a day.

1. **Dawn.** Open the coop door and the goat gate (E). Collect eggs from the nest box. Milk each goat (hold use with a pail or bottle, 3 game minutes). Fill the troughs and break ice on the water.
2. **Day.** Hens free-range within 16 blocks. Goats browse when the gate is open. A goat loose in the garden eats 1 m² of crop a game hour.
3. **Dusk.** Hens roost on their own; the player shuts the door. Goats come to the pen 90% of the time; the rest must be led.

**Production, reconciled.** Crafting and building in depth says hens lay above 5 °C with 12 hours of light; this entry says 0 °C and 14 hours. Both fit one curve:

```latex
P_{egg} = 0.8 \cdot f_{light} \cdot f_{temp} \cdot f_{fed}, \quad f_{light} = \mathrm{clamp}\left(\frac{h - 10}{4}, 0, 1\right), \quad f_{temp} = \begin{cases} 0 & T < 0 \\ 0.5 & 0 \le T < 5 \\ 1 & T \ge 5 \end{cases}
```

Milk is 2.0 L × a lactation factor falling linearly from 1.0 at kidding to 0.5 on day 240, then dry, ×0.75 on hay alone. Kidding falls in May, so milk runs May to December. f\_fed is 1 above fed 60, 0.5 from 30 to 60, and 0 below 30.

**Feed.** A hen eats 0.1 kg of grain when the coop is at 5 °C or warmer, and 0.15 kg below that. Fish meal counts at 0.75 kg of grain per kg. Free-ranging from May to September covers 70% of it. A goat eats 2 kg of hay a day, about 400 kg over a winter (Survival tuning), so the 200 kg delivery minimum is only the start.

**Troughs.** Each animal takes its ration from a trough within 8 blocks at dawn. A water trough freezes in a day below −10 °C unless the room holds above 0 °C.

**Breeding.** Hens: with the rooster present, 6 or more eggs left in the nest from May to July make a hen broody 10% of the time a week. Chicks need the brooder for 3 weeks. Goats rut in October and November and kid in May. Reindeer rut in September and October and calve in May.

**Butchering stock.** E hold with the knife on a penned, calm animal: no hunt, the carcass from Bestiary › Carcasses and bodies, and Spirits −2 the first time for each species.

**Escapes.** A goat gets out of a brush fence 5% of days and a pole fence 1%. A hen left out at dusk has a 40% chance of being taken that night. An open coop door counts as a brush fence in the predator roll.

**Being away.** On an Endless SOS, Pete's neighbour keeps the yard: needs are held where they were. Presets multiply need decay (Gentle ×0.7, Brutal ×1.3) and predator rolls (Gentle ×0.5, Brutal ×1.5).

**Minecraft build.** `yearofice:hen` (with a `sex` field; the rooster is a male hen), `yearofice:goat` and `yearofice:reindeer` are our own entities. `minecraft:goat` and `minecraft:chicken` never spawn. Vanilla breeding is cancelled with `BabyEntitySpawnEvent`, and vanilla feeding interactions are cancelled too. Block entities: `yearofice:feed_trough`, `water_trough`, `nest_box`, `hay_rack`, `brooder`. The attachment `yearofice:livestock` holds fed, watered, warm, lactation day and last ration day. Data goes in `data/yearofice/livestock/<id>.json`, with `com.yearofice.food.livestock.LivestockCare` running once a game hour and catching up analytically on chunk load, as traps do. Pieces ship M8 and animals M9.

**GameTests (batch `m9`).**

- `yearofice:homestead/egg_curve`: 4 hens at 12 hours of light and 6 °C lay 0.40 ±0.04 eggs a hen a day.
- `yearofice:homestead/goat_dry`: a goat is dry on day 241 after kidding.
- `yearofice:homestead/trough_days`: a full feed trough keeps 2 goats fed above 30 for 3 days and no longer.

### Raising orphaned young

The player can raise a wild animal only when it is truly orphaned: its mother is dead (shot, killed by a predator or drowned) or has abandoned it, which the game rolls once a season for a few young in each region. Taking young from a living mother always fails: she charges or follows the player home, and the young die within 2 days.

| Orphan | When found | How it is raised | What it becomes | When it leaves |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Moose calf | June–July, by a dead cow | Goat's milk or a fish-and-birch-sap gruel 3 times a day for 8 weeks, then browse; must be kept in a pen from wolves and bears | A camp moose: alarms at bears; its scent and calls draw wild moose to the pen in the rut | In its second September, during the rut; it may visit the camp each fall after that |
| Caribou calf | June, after a migration loss | Milk, then lichen; stays with goats or reindeer if there are any | Carries a 20 kg pack on a lead; follows the player on the trail | Joins the fall migration in its second year unless it is kept with reindeer |
| Goslings (Canada goose) | Late June, an abandoned clutch hatched in an incubator box by the stove | They imprint on the first thing they see, so the player is "mother" | Follow the player in a line; honk at predators within 30 m; a pair lays 5–8 eggs a spring | Fly south in their first September; 50% come back to the pond each May |
| Red fox kit | May–June, a den with a dead vixen | Meat scraps and voles 4 times a day | A camp fox: kills 1–2 voles a night around the cache, which cuts vole losses by 60%; steals unguarded fish | Leaves for good when 8 months old (January), and may raid the chickens on the way out |
| Snowshoe hare leveret | Any time in summer | Greens and willow twigs; dies 30% of the time in the first week | A pet that lives in the cabin; Spirits +1 a day while it lives (the video's Peeper, made permanent) | Never; lives about 4 years |

**Raised animals stay wild.** A raised animal has a bond value, not obedience. Above 60 it stays near camp, comes when the player calls, and stays calm when handled. Below 30 it wanders and may turn wild for good. Bond rises with feeding on schedule (+3 a day) and calm handling (+1), and falls with missed meals (−5), being struck (−30) or a stranger animal in the pen (−2 a day). The game never adds a leash command or a combat role: raised animals are company, alarms, food or loads, not followers.

**Hunting pressure and the population.** Raising an orphan removes it from the wild count while it is in camp and returns it when it leaves, so the population model stays honest.

**Studio detail (Ecosystem and hunting).** Orphan rules.

**How an orphan is made.** Every young entity carries its `mother_id`. When the mother dies by the player, a predator, drowning or winter, every young within 200 m gets `orphan = true`. Abandonment is rolled on June 20 in each region: 0–2 young among the species present that year, weighted by litter size.

**The window.** An orphan stays within 50 m of its mother's body or the nest for 3 game days, calling (calf bleat, kit yips) that carries 150 m. Each day, a predator takes it 30% of the time. Calves die of hunger on day 3, kits and leverets on day 2. The inspect card reads the case: "Alone. No cow tracks for a day." With the mother alive it says "Fresh cow tracks. She is close." That is the warning.

**Taking it.**

- **Small young** (kit, leveret, chicks) ride in a `yearofice:carry_basket` in the off-hand.
- **Calves** follow: the player crouches within 10 m with a filled nursing bottle for 1 game minute, and the calf follows at 3 m for 6 game hours. Each feeding renews the follow.
- **Eggs** go into the incubator box, which must sit within 1.5 m of a lit stove at 35–39 °C for 28 days and be turned twice a day (E). Turned eggs hatch 80% of the time; unturned ones 40%. The goslings imprint on the first player within 3 m at hatching.

**Feeding calves.** A `yearofice:nursing_bottle` is made from a birch-bark bottle and a rawhide teat. A moose calf needs 2 L a day in 3 feeds, in the windows 06–09, 12–15 and 18–21. Calves survive 8 weeks 90% of the time on goat's milk and 60% on gruel (0.5 kg fish broth, 0.2 kg fat, 1 L water; birch sap in May–June adds ×1.2). Each missed feed costs −5 bond and +5% death chance. A moose calf grows from 15 kg to about 150 kg by October and needs a 10 × 10 block pen with a 2-block fence.

**Hard edges.**

- **A raised bull in the rut.** In its second September a raised bull with bond under 60 charges a player within 10 m 20% of the time. Above 60 it never does. A raised cow comes back with her own calf 30% of springs.
- **The fox.** It cuts vole losses only with bond over 30 and free nights. It takes a hen on leaving 30% of the time when one is outside or the door is open.
- **Pets and Spirits.** The leveret's Spirits +1 counts once a day, however many pets there are.
- **Killing a raised animal** costs Spirits −10 and a journal line.
- **One at a time.** One raised animal per species at a time, and the year card lists "Young taken". Killing mothers to farm orphans gains nothing: the cow's loss already costs about three future moose (Populations).

**Bond, read not shown.** The inspect card shows a word: Shy (under 30), Settled (30–60), At home (over 60).

**Minecraft build.** `com.yearofice.food.livestock.Orphans` sets the flag on `AnimalKilledEvent` and runs the June roll. `food.livestock.BondTracker` keeps the `yearofice:bond` attachment (value, feeds today, last feed minute). Taking young from a living mother starts `MotherPursuitGoal` on her, which follows by scent up to 2 km. GameTests (batch `m9`): `yearofice:homestead/orphan_window`, an untended moose orphan is gone by day 3 in 100 of 100 runs; `yearofice:homestead/incubator_turning`, hatch rates of 80% ±4% turned and 40% ±4% unturned over 1,000 clutches.

## Taming a wolf without bones

In vanilla Minecraft a wolf is tamed by right-clicking it with bones until hearts appear. Year of Ice removes that completely: bones do nothing to a wolf except give it something to chew. A wolf is tamed only through the trust system in Open world in depth (the four stages Wary, Tolerant, Bonded, Companion), and trust is built by behaving like something a wolf can learn to accept.

**Who can be tamed.** Only three kinds of wolf: a den pup taken while the pack is away hunting, an orphan whose pack was killed, or a lone dispersing yearling between September and February. A wolf from a healthy pack in its territory never tames; it only learns to avoid the player.

**What builds trust.** Each action has a daily cap, so trust can't be ground out in one sitting; Companion takes at least 32 game days, as the existing trust table sets.

| Action | How the player does it | Trust | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Leave food and step back | Drop meat or fish on the ground, then back away; the wolf eats only if the player stays farther away than 15 m minus 1 m for every 5 trust | +2 | 3 times a day |
| Sit still nearby | Crouch and look away (the crosshair more than 30° off the wolf) within 20 m for 10 game minutes | +1 | Twice a day |
| Keep a routine | Feed within 1 game hour of yesterday's feeding time | +1 bonus | Once a day |
| Talk | Hold V near the wolf while looking past it; the player hums or speaks low (the V voice key, RECONCILE 31, 60) | +1 | Once a day |
| Hand-feed | Hold meat out with use, from Tolerant (25) up; the wolf takes it from the hand | +3 | Once a day |
| Share the fire | Sleep within 8 m of a wolf at Bonded or higher | +2 | Once a night |

**What breaks it.** Looking straight at a Wary wolf within 10 m for more than 5 seconds counts as a stare: −2, and it backs off. Running at it costs −5. Striking it costs −30, as the existing table says. Feeding spoiled meat costs −3 and makes the wolf sick for a day. Killing wolves in its sight costs −10.

**Reading the wolf instead of a meter.** No trust number is shown until Bonded. The player reads the stages from the wolf itself:

- **Wary:** tail low, ears back, circles at the edge of sight.
- **Tolerant:** ears up, sits and watches, eats while the player is in view.
- **Bonded:** follows at 10 m, play-bows, sleeps near the fire.
- **Companion:** comes on the whistle and answers the commands in Open world in depth.

The field notebook logs each change in plain words, for example "Day 12. She ate at 9 m today." Naming becomes possible at Tolerant.

**Minecraft build.** The mod's wolf is its own entity, `yearofice:wolf`. Vanilla wolves don't spawn, and the vanilla bone-taming interaction is cancelled in the entity-interact event. Trust and bond values live in data attachments (`yearofice:trust` on the wolf, `yearofice:bond` on raised animals). Feeding is handled by our own interaction and item-pickup goals; a dropped food item is a target for the wolf only when the player is outside the distance above. Livestock are `yearofice:hen`, `yearofice:goat` and `yearofice:reindeer`, with troughs as block entities. Orphans spawn as young of the existing species with an `orphan` flag. All of it ships in M9, with the hen house and goat pen in M8.

**Tests.**

- Right-clicking any wolf with a bone 100 times changes nothing.
- A lone yearling or den pup fed by the rules above reaches Companion in 32–60 game days and never sooner; an orphan, which starts at 10 trust, in 29–60.
- A coop at −30 °C with a fat lamp and a small stove holds above 0 °C and keeps hens laying.
- A raised fox kit cuts cache vole losses by 55–65% over a winter.
- Taking a calf from a living cow moose always ends with the calf dead within 2 game days.

**Studio detail (Ecosystem and hunting).** Trust interactions in Minecraft.

**Two tables, one cap.** This entry's limits (food 3 times a day, sitting within 20 m) replace the ones in Open world in depth › Taming a wolf. The daily cap there still clamps the total: +3 below 50, +2 from 50 to 80, +1 above. Pups and yearlings starting at 0 still need at least 32 game days (17 to reach 50, 15 more to reach 80). An orphan starting at 10 needs 29.

**Each action, as built.**

| Action | Input | Server check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Leave food | Drop a `#yearofice:wolf_food` item (Q) | The item's thrower is the player. The wolf eats only while the nearest player is beyond 15 − trust ÷ 5 m. Spoiled (`fresh` component) gives −3 |
| Sit still | Crouch | Sampled every 20 ticks: crouched, crosshair over 30° off the wolf, within 20 m, for 10 game minutes in a row |
| Keep a routine | — | The feed time is stored as a minute of the day; within ±60 game minutes it gives the bonus |
| Talk | V hold, 3 s (the voice key from Danger in detail) | Look 30–90° off the wolf, within 20 m. Empty-hand use is an alias sent as payload `yearofice:talk`, because a right-click on air never reaches the server |
| Hand-feed | Use with food on the wolf within 2 m | Tolerant or higher. The player must not move over 0.5 m during the 2 s take, or the wolf backs off with no gain |
| Share the fire | Sleep | On wake: a Bonded or higher wolf within 8 m |

**What breaks it, as built.** A stare is the look vector within 10° of the wolf's eyes, within 10 m, for 100 ticks: −2, at most once a game hour. Running at it is sprinting with over 3 m/s toward the wolf within 20 m: −5, once per approach. A strike is any damage from a player source.

**Body language.** Only the stage (a byte) and a mood are synced to the client, so no tooltip mod or debug screen shows the trust number before Bonded. GeckoLib controllers drive the tail, ears and body by stage. At Bonded, a crouch-and-stand by the player is a play invite: the wolf play-bows back 50% of the time.

**Edge rules.**

- **Staying in range.** A candidate wolf follows at 50–100 m, so it stays Active and outside the entity caps. If the player travels over 2 km in a day, it loses the trail 50% of the time and turns up at the last feeding spot next day.
- **Being away.** On an Endless SOS, Bonded and Companion wolves fly out with the player and their trust is held. Below Bonded, they lose 2 a day while the player is gone.
- **Presets.** The no-food loss is Gentle −3, Standard −5, Brutal −7 a day. The 32-day floor never changes.
- **Several wolves.** Each keeps its own counters. Food taken by one gives the others nothing.
- **Bones.** A bone dropped or handed over is carried off and chewed for 1 game hour: no trust and no penalty. The rawhide-strip wolf stays capped at 25.

**Minecraft build.** `yearofice:trust` holds value, stage, today's counts by action, today's gain, feed minute and day, and resets on `ClockEvent.Day`. `minecraft:wolf` is removed from every biome's spawns, and any interaction with one while holding a bone is cancelled in `PlayerInteractEvent.EntityInteract` (for wolves from commands or spawn eggs). Brain memory `yearofice:offered_food` and activity `take_offered_food` sit in `com.yearofice.ecosystem.species.WolfEntity`, with `com.yearofice.ecosystem.taming.TrustService`. Ships M9.

**More GameTests (batch `m9`).**

- `yearofice:taming/distance_gate`: food dropped with the player 12 m away is not eaten by a wolf at trust 0, and is eaten at 16 m.
- `yearofice:taming/stare_once_an_hour`: a 60 s stare costs −2 once, not 12 times.
- `yearofice:taming/daily_cap`: every action done at its limit in one day adds exactly +3 at trust 10.
