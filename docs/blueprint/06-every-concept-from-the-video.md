# Every concept from the video

This tab lists every concept in Forge Labs' "1 Year in Alaska in ULTRA REALISTIC Minecraft", chapter by chapter, and says where each one lives in the blueprint. Concepts that were missing get a full rule for the Minecraft mod; concepts covered in only a line or two get a "Deepened" entry with the missing mechanics. Environmental concepts come first, because the owner asked for them by name.

## How to read this tab

**Three statuses.** "Covered" names the tab and section that already specify the concept. "Deepened" means it exists but thinly, and this tab adds the missing rules without restating them. "Added" means nothing covered it, and this tab gives the full rule.

**Counts.** The video holds 322 distinct concepts across its 19 chapters. 241 were already covered, 43 are deepened here and 38 are added here. Six more moments were left out on purpose; they are listed under Not carried over.

**Scope.** This tab keeps to what the creator says and shows in the video. Suggestions from viewers' comments belong to the "Ideas from viewers" tab.

**Units.** The video's temperatures are in °F with °C on hover. This tab gives °C first and the video's °F only where it quotes a reading.

### Two temperature units (Added)

**What it is.** The messenger shows temperature in both scales, as the video's device did. °C is the default primary unit; a setting swaps it so °F is primary.

**Rules.**

- Primary unit in large type, secondary in small type beside it: "−27 °C (−17 °F)".
- Hovering the reading, or holding the messenger button (M) for 1 s, swaps them for 5 s, like the video's mouse-over.
- Forecast lows and highs, the feels-like and the year card all follow the setting. Body weight follows a matching kg or lb setting.
- Rounding: whole degrees. Conversion is exact before rounding, so −40 shows as −40 in both.

**Why it matters.** Players quote readings to friends; the video's jokes about the two scales show they read both. Nothing in gameplay changes: all rules run in °C.

**Ties.** Messenger HUD (Minecraft engine › The messenger), the weather card below, the year card (Blueprint › Gameplay ideas from the video).

**Build.** A client config value `temp_unit` (C or F) and `mass_unit` (kg or lb) in `yearofice-client.toml`. `com.yearofice.client.ui.Units.format(double celsius)` is the only formatter; every screen calls it.

**Studio detail (World and weather).**

**Exact conversion rules.** Each scale is rounded on its own from the exact value, half away from zero. Absolute readings use F = C × 1.8 + 32; differences use ΔF = ΔC × 1.8 with no offset.

| Input | Shown (°C primary) | Shown (°F primary) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| −40.0 °C air | −40 °C (−40 °F) | −40 °F (−40 °C) |
| −17.5 °C air | −18 °C (1 °F) | 1 °F (−18 °C) |
| 0.2 °C air | 0 °C (32 °F) | 32 °F (0 °C) |
| Clothing warmth +8 °C | +8 °C (+14 °F) | +14 °F (+8 °C) |
| Forecast range −18 to −26 °C | −18 to −26 °C (0 to −15 °F) | 0 to −15 °F (−18 to −26 °C) |

- "−0" is never shown. The minus sign is U+2212 in every string.
- Body weight and load follow `mass_unit`: the load meter reads "48 / 66 lb" for 22 / 30 kg, rounded to whole pounds. Limits stay kg inside.
- Wind stays m/s; with °F primary the card adds mph in small type. Snow depth, distance and fuel stay metric in every setting (Metric first).

**Edge cases.**

1. Journal lines and year cards store numbers in °C. Switching the setting rewrites them on screen, so old pages match the new unit.
2. Feels-like below −60 °C shows "below −60 °C (−76 °F)", the readout limit of the device.
3. Multiplayer: the setting is per client. The server sends only °C.
4. Battery at 0: no readings at all, so no unit question.

**First time.** On the first messenger open, a one-time hint sits under the reading: "Hold M to swap °C and °F." It never shows again.

**Data rule for writers.** Lang strings never hold a literal unit. Messenger lines carry `{temp}`, `{temp_delta}` or `{mass}` slots filled by `Units.format`, `formatDelta` and `formatMass`. A data-validation JUnit test fails any value in `assets/yearofice/lang/*.json` matching `[−-]?\d+ ?°[CF]`.

**Build.** Client only, in `com.yearofice.client.ui.Units`; no server cost. JUnit `UnitsTest` covers the five rows above. Ships in M4 with the messenger HUD.

**Risk.** Two formatters drifting apart. Guard: one class, and the lang lint above.

### The weather card (Deepened)

The forecast rules live in Blueprint › Calendar, daylight and weather. The video's device also showed the parts below, which the blueprint did not list.

| Line | Rule | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Precipitation chance | From the blurred forecast: true Wet or Severe tomorrow shows 60–90%; otherwise 0–30% | "Rain 70%" |
| Cloud cover | Clear 0–20%, Cloudy 60–100%, Wet 80–100% | "Cloud 85%" |
| Wind | Mean and direction from the state table | "Wind NE 4 m/s" |
| Snowfall amount | The year table's fresh snow, rounded to 0.5 cm and blurred ±30% for tomorrow | "Snowfall 3.5 cm" |
| Running snow depth | Today's depth for the class under the player's feet | "On the ground: 42 cm" |
| High and low | Tomorrow's, with the ±2 °C error | "High 15 °C, low 7 °C" |

**Alerts the card pushes.** One line each, at the 06:00 morning prompt, never repeated the same day:

- "Thunderstorms tomorrow afternoon." (Storm in summer)
- "Snowfall coming tomorrow: 3–5 cm." (Snow, with the blurred range)
- "Freezing overnight: low −4 °C." (first night below 0 °C after August 15, then any night below −10 °C until the first snow that stays)
- "Blizzard tomorrow. Visibility 20 m." (existing)

**Build.** `com.yearofice.story.Forecast` already blurs the year table. Add `cloud_pct`, `precip_pct`, `snow_cm` fields to the `yearofice:env_sync` payload's forecast block.

**Studio detail (World and weather).**

**Keyed to the shown state.** The precipitation and cloud percentages follow the forecast state the card shows, not the true one. Otherwise "Cloudy, rain 70%" would leak the truth and lift the 80% accuracy. Each number is a seeded roll `hash(seed, day, lead, field)`, so reopening never changes it.

```latex
P_{precip} = 10 \cdot \mathrm{round}\left(\frac{p_0 + 30u}{10}\right), \quad p_0 = \begin{cases} 60 & \text{shown Wet or Severe} \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}
```

u is the seeded roll in \[0, 1). Percentages are in steps of 10, so the card never looks more exact than it is.

**What each lead shows.**

| Lead | Lines shown |
| --- | --- |
| Tomorrow | All six lines and up to 2 alerts |
| Day 2 outlook | State, high and low (±4 °C), wind direction only |
| Day 3 | Flags only (cold snap, blizzard, storm) |

**Edge cases.**

- **Snow depth indoors or on ice.** Inside a room, the card reads the open-ground class outside. On lake or river ice, it reads the open class.
- **Rain or snow by mean.** "Rain 70%" becomes "Snow 70%" when tomorrow's shown mean is below 1 °C; at 0–2 °C the line reads "Rain or snow 70%".
- **Overslept.** If the player sleeps past 06:00, the alerts show on waking, once.
- **Alert order.** At most 2 alerts a morning, in this order: blizzard, cold snap, thunderstorms, snowfall, freezing. The rest show only on the card.
- **Presets.** Forecast alerts are information, not warnings, so they show every day on every preset. Only the first "Freezing overnight" of the season is a teaching line, and on Brutal it shows once per run.

**Feel.** The card is one messenger page with six rows and small icons. The first time a row with 60% or more comes true, the journal writes: "The forecast said rain. It rained." The first time one fails: "Forecast said clear. Soaked anyway."

**Ties.** Fire danger (below) adds its level as a seventh row from snow-off to August 31. Sky signs (Blueprint › Calendar, daylight and weather) stay the no-battery fallback. Firewood drying (below) reads the same rain days.

**Build.** `com.yearofice.story.Forecast` fills `cloud_pct`, `precip_pct`, `snow_cm` for 3 leads: 9 bytes added to `yearofice:env_sync`, sent only when they change. Lang keys `messenger.yearofice.card.*`.

**GameTests (batch m7).** `story/forecast_precip_keyed`: over 10,000 seeded days, shown "60% or more" days are truly Wet or Severe 80% ±2%. `story/snowfall_range`: the true fresh snow falls inside the alert's range on 85–95% of snow days.

**Risk.** A card so exact that weather stops surprising. Guard: 10% steps, the ±30% snowfall blur and the 80% state accuracy stay.

### Thunderstorms (Added)

**What it is.** The video's storms were alerts that changed his plans: a day to dig, craft or wait. Storm days get their own dangers and their own uses. This entry keeps the video's storm moments; the full storm model is in Weather and storms.

**Gameplay loop.** The alert comes a day ahead. The player sets nets and traps in the morning, then spends 14:00–20:00 under cover on queued jobs. A player caught out on a ridge or open water faces lightning.

**Rules.**

1. A summer Storm day builds a cell between 14:00 and 20:00 (existing). It lasts 1–3 game hours over the player's region.
2. **Thunder distance.** Each flash plays a sound delayed by distance ÷ 340 m/s in game seconds. The messenger's lightning counter reads "Strikes 4 km away" once the player has seen 3 flashes.
3. **Strikes.** During the cell, 1 strike every 4–10 game minutes lands within 2 km. The target is the highest exposed point in a 32 × 32 block cell: a lone tree, a mast, a tower, a person on a ridge or on open water.
4. **Player risk.** Standing on the highest ground within 50 m, or on open water, gives a 2% chance per strike within 500 m of being struck. A strike takes 60 Condition and knocks the player out for 1 game hour. Crouching in a low spot or under forest canopy of trees taller than the player sets the chance to 0.05%.
5. **Fire.** A strike on a dead spruce in dry taiga lights it 25% of the time once 5 days have passed without rain. That fire follows Fire danger below and the wildfire rules (Open world in depth › Rare events).
6. **Rain.** 10–30 mm in 1–3 hours puts out any uncovered fire within 20 game minutes (existing rate) and doubles worm yield for 2 days.
7. **Animals.** Bites ×0.5 during the cell; the 12 hours before it give ×1.4 (existing). Moose and bears bed down; hares stay in cover, so snare catches that night are ×0.7.

**The storm day as a tool.** Queued jobs (Crafting and building in depth › Queued jobs) run at ×1.2 speed while the player is under a roof during a storm: weaving net mesh, twisting cordage, carving, sewing. The messenger offers them in the morning prompt: "Storm from 15:00. Under-roof jobs: 3 queued."

**What the player sees and hears.** The sky goes dark green-grey 30 game minutes before; wind rises; the first thunder is a low roll from 10 km. Flashes lift the lightmap for 3 ticks, capped below full white, and the client smoke's flash scan checks the cap. A struck tree splits with a crack, sheds bark and smoulders.

**Counterplay.** Read the alert, stay off ridges and the river, set queued jobs, cover the woodpile and the fire.

**Ties.** Weather (Blueprint › Calendar, daylight and weather), fire and wildfire (Open world in depth › Rare events), Condition (Blueprint › Survival tuning), fishing (Blueprint › Conditions), queued jobs.

**Build.** A server-side `com.yearofice.env.phenomena.Lightning` picks targets with a heightmap query per 32-block cell. A strike sends the `yearofice:strike` payload, which `BoltRenderer` draws, with thunder through `distant_sound` and its distance delay (Weather and storms); no vanilla `LightningBolt` is spawned, and our own code does the damage and fire. Struck trees become `yearofice:struck_spruce` (a log block with a `smoulder` state).

**Studio detail (World and weather).**

**Lightning needs heat.** A Severe day draws lightning only if its high is 18 °C or more; a cooler Severe day is wind and rain with no strikes. With the summer chain that gives about 4–8 lightning days a year, most from June 20 to July 31. No strikes after September 15.

**Target score.** Each strike picks the top of one 32 × 32 block cell:

```latex
S = h_{above} + 4 \cdot m + 2 \cdot l
```

h\_above is metres above the cell's mean ground, m is 1 for metal (mast, stovepipe, lightning rod), l is 1 for a lone point with nothing taller within 8 blocks. A standing player counts 1.8 m; a raised axe or rod adds 0.3. A lightning rod, such as the lookout's (Items), wins every strike within 30 m and grounds it with no fire.

**Player risk by preset.**

| Preset | Strike chance on high ground or open water | Under canopy, crouched | Damage |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gentle | 0.5% | 0% | 30 Condition, 20 min down |
| Standard | 2% | 0.05% | 60 Condition, 1 h down |
| Brutal | 3% | 0.05% | 60 Condition, 1 h down |

A strike can bring Condition to 5 but never to 0 on its own. Animals within 50 m flee for 10 game minutes, so the knocked-out player is not eaten. In Hardcore that rule is the only mercy.

**Near misses.** A strike within 100 m rings the ears: hearing range ×0.5 for 2 game minutes, a high tone, captions muted. Hair stands up 5 game seconds before a strike within 50 m: a prickle sound and the HUD frost edge flashing white. That is the warning to crouch.

**The counter.** After 3 flashes the messenger shows distance to 0.5 km and a trend: "Strikes 3.5 km, closer." The journal teaches the rule after the first storm: "Thunder 30 seconds after the flash: 10 km. Under 10 seconds: get low."

**Struck trees.** `struck_spruce` smoulders 30–90 game minutes in rain. A struck spruce becomes dead standing wood at 18% moisture (Firewood drying), with a spiral scar the player can find for years.

**Sounds.** `yearofice:weather.thunder_far`, `thunder_near`, `strike_crack`, `tree_split`, `hair_prickle`, `ear_ring`, `rain_heavy_roof`, `rain_heavy_canopy`.

**Ties.** Fire danger (rain of 10 mm or more resets d\_dry to 0); Worms as food (×2 yield); Tok and tamed wolves refuse to leave cover during the cell; queued jobs.

**Build.** `env.phenomena.Lightning` runs only while a player is within 2 km of the cell. It reads the `MOTION_BLOCKING` heightmap: 1,024 column reads per strike, under 0.2 ms. Off-screen, strikes are rolled on 32 m cells for the fire check only. Ships in M7.

**GameTests (m7).** `story/lightning_highest_point`: in a template with a 20 m mast among 12 m spruces, at least 90 of 100 strikes hit the mast. `story/canopy_chance`: 100,000 rolls for a crouched player under canopy give 0.05% ±0.02.

**Risks.** Lightning as a fire-starting tool: a player who builds a tall spruce tower near camp gets fires near camp, which is its own lesson. Tedium: storms are 4–8 a year and always forecast.

### Leaf fall (Deepened)

**Missing so far.** The blueprint dates "Leaves turn" (August 25 – September 10) and colours the leaves. It has no alert, no falling leaves and no effect on play.

**Stages.** Each birch, aspen, willow and alder stand goes through four stages by its own date inside the window, south slopes 3 days later than north slopes.

| Stage | Days after the stand's turn date | Leaf block | Falling-leaf particles per tree per game minute | Effects |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Green | before 0 | Green tint | 0 | — |
| Yellow | 0–6 | Yellow tint | 0.2 | Spirits +1 the first day the player sees a yellow slope |
| Falling | 7–13 | Yellow-brown, 50% of leaf blocks gone | 2, and 10 in wind over 6 m/s | Leaf litter forms |
| Bare | 14 on | No leaf quads | 0 | Sight range in the stand +30% |

**Leaf litter.** From the Falling stage, ground under broadleaf trees gets a `yearofice:leaf_litter` layer. Walking on it is louder (noise ×1.3, as dry brush) until rain soaks it (noise ×1.0) or snow covers it. It hides tracks: sign on litter is read at half the usual distance.

**Bare trees change hunting.** Squirrels and grouse in bare birch are seen 30% farther, so blunt-arrow hunting gets easier in late September, as in the video's run of squirrel kills. Moose browse is easier to read: clipped twigs show on bare willow.

**Messenger.** "Leaves are falling. About {n} days until freeze-up." The day count reads the year table's freeze-up date with ±5 days of blur.

**Ties.** Season stages (Minecraft engine › Seasons on screen), senses and noise (Blueprint › Senses by species), tracks (Blueprint › Tracks and sign), Spirits.

**Build.** Leaf stage is a field in the chunk's `season_stamp` attachment per stand id. Falling leaves are client particles `yearofice:leaf_fall` spawned from leaf blocks in loaded chunks within 32 blocks. Litter is a carpet-like block placed in the season pass, removed by the snow pass.

**Studio detail (World and weather).**

**Each stand's date.** A stand's turn date is `hash(seed, stand_id, year)` inside August 25 – September 10. North slopes take the early third, south slopes the late third (the 3-day lag), and each 100 m above the valley floor moves the date 2 days earlier.

**Weather moves the stages.**

| Event | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| First night below −2 °C | Every stand still Green turns Yellow the next day |
| A Severe day during Falling | Remaining leaves drop: the stand is Bare the next day |
| Wind over 10 m/s during Yellow | 20% of leaves go early; the stand enters Falling 2 days sooner |
| A wet fall (5 Wet days in the window) | Yellow lasts 2 days longer, colours duller |

**Species differences.** Aspen turns gold and flickers in any wind over 2 m/s (a shimmer on the leaf texture). Birch goes yellow. Willow and alder go brown-yellow and drop last, 4 days after birch in the same stand.

**Litter rules.** Litter lies 1 layer deep and is removed by the first snow pass that leaves 2 layers or more. Litter counts as dry ground for fire sparks only after 5 dry days. In spring, the same columns show a flattened brown litter tint until green-up plus 5 days.

**Feel, step by step.**

1. The first yellow slope seen from 500 m or more gives Spirits +1 and the journal: "Yellow on the north slopes."
2. Walking a falling stand, leaves drift past at eye level; a gust sends a swirl of 20–40 particles.
3. Footsteps change to `yearofice:step.leaf_litter`; the summer leaf rustle bed swaps for `ambient.leaves_dry` (sparser, higher).
4. Bare stands open the view: grouse budding in aspen tops at dusk show at 30% more range.

**Ties.** Moose rut (Bestiary): bulls thrashing bare willow are seen farther, so calling from the edge of a bare stand pays. Fire danger (above). Snow sounds by temperature (Viewers tab): litter under fresh snow muffles, not crunches.

**Build.** Stand ids come from the worldgen stand layer on 64 m tiles; each leaf block maps to its stand with one lookup. The server owns litter blocks; the client tints from the synced season stamp. Each client changes at most 1 stand's stage per 5 real seconds, which spreads the re-mesh. A client option "Falling leaves: Full, Low, Off" caps particles at 200, 60 or 0. Ships in M7.

**GameTest (m7).** `story/leaf_stage_dates`: over 100 seeds, no broadleaf stand is Yellow before August 25, and all are Bare by September 26.

**Risk.** Re-mesh hitches on a big turning day. Guard: the per-stand stagger above, plus the engine's 50 ms hitch budget measured in the client smoke.

### Daylight you can plan by (Deepened)

**Missing so far.** Daylight comes from the sun at 64°N, but the player is never told how fast it is changing.

**The daylight line.** The messenger's Forecast screen shows sunrise, sunset and the change since yesterday: "Sun 08:41–17:02. −6 min a day."

| Date | Daylight at 64°N | Change a day |
| --- | --- | --- |
| June 21 | about 21 h 50 min | 0 |
| September 22 | about 12 h 10 min | −6 to −7 min |
| December 21 | about 3 h 40 min | 0 |
| March 20 | about 12 h 10 min | +6 to +7 min |

**Last light warning.** When the player is more than 1 km from their bed or a lit fire, the messenger warns 60 game minutes before civil dusk: "Dark in 1 hour. Camp is 1.8 km." This is the fall evening the video was caught out by.

**Work by the light.** Hold-to-work jobs outdoors after civil dusk need a light (existing: crafting in the dark takes twice as long). The daylight line lets the player plan the day's outdoor work against it.

**Ties.** Calendar (Minecraft engine › Calendar, daylight and moon), light (Items › Light), travel time (Blueprint › Scope and pillars, travel rate).

**Build.** `com.yearofice.sim.time.Sun.sunrise(day)` and `sunset(day)` already exist for lighting (TimeService.sun()); the Forecast screen reads them.

**Studio detail (World and weather).**

**Two kinds of light.** The line shows sunrise to sunset and civil twilight (sun at −6°): "Sun 10:59–14:40. Light 09:45–15:55. +1 min a day." From about May 15 to July 28 civil dusk never comes, and the line reads "Light all night. Sun 03:10–00:40."

**When the warning fires.** It accounts for the walk home:

```latex
t_{warn} = t_{dusk} - \max\left(60,\ t_{walk} + 15\right) \quad \text{game minutes}
```

t\_walk is the straight-line distance × 1.3, at the player's current travel rate (on foot, snowshoes, canoe). "Camp" is the nearest bed the player has slept in, or a fire they lit that is still burning. At 3 km out the warning comes at dusk − 4 h: "Dark in 4 hours. Camp is 3.0 km, about 4 hours."

**Weather moves dusk.** Cloudy days count dusk 20 game minutes early, Wet days 30, a blizzard 60. On a clear full-moon night with snow cover, the warning adds "Full moon tonight", since sight reaches 40 m.

**Edge cases.**

- No warning during the midnight-sun window, in a room, or within 1 km of camp.
- Once per day. On Gentle it repeats at dusk − 15 min; on Brutal it is once per run, like other warnings.
- Battery at 0: no line. The sky's colour and Arvid Lund's logbook page "Light by the month" (a `yearofice:note`) stand in.

**Turning points.** Journal lines fire once a year:

| Day | Line | Spirits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| June 21 | "Longest day. It only gets shorter from here." | 0 |
| September 22 | "Day and night are even." | 0 |
| December 21 | "Shortest day: 3 hours 40 minutes of sun." | 0 |
| December 22 | "The light is coming back." | +3 |
| March 20 | "Even again. The light is winning." | +2 |

**Ties.** Animal activity windows read civil dawn and dusk (Minecraft engine › Animals). Headlamp in deep cold (Blueprint › Video checklist). Daylight hours set how many outdoor hold-to-work hours a winter day holds: about 6 in December.

**Build.** `sim.time.Sun.civilDawn(day)` and `civilDusk(day)` interpolate the 365-row table to the minute. The warning runs on the server each game minute for players over 1 km out: one path estimate, no pathfinding. Lang keys `messenger.yearofice.last_light`, `journal.yearofice.solstice_*`. Ships in M7.

**GameTest (m7).** `story/sun_table_64n`: June 21 sun 21:40–22:00 long; December 21 3:30–3:50; September 22 change −6.0 to −7.5 min.

**Risk.** Nagging. Guard: once a day, and silent within 1 km.

### Freeze-up you can see (Deepened)

**Missing so far.** Ice thickness and states are fully specified (Minecraft engine › Ice, snow and permafrost). The video's moment is the first look: shore ice forming at −6 °C while the middle still flows.

**Shore ice first.** From the first night below −5 °C, `skim_ice` forms along still edges, eddies and behind rocks each night, reaching 1–3 blocks from the bank. It melts by noon on days above 0 °C. Slush (`yearofice:frazil_slush`, a translucent floating layer) drifts down the channel in the 3–7 days before freeze-up (the slush-running stage in Blueprint › Calendar, daylight and weather).

**The messenger's first ice note.** "The river is starting to freeze. Shore ice only: do not cross." Then each day until the channel is closed: "River: open channel, shore ice 2 m."

**Testing the ice.** Striking ice with an axe or a pole (use key on the ice) gives a readout: "Ice about 4 cm. Not safe." The reading is ±1 cm. It costs 30 game seconds and is how the player learns the 10 cm rule.

**Ties.** Ice (Minecraft engine › Ice, snow and permafrost), the thin-ice danger (Blueprint › Danger in detail), travel on ice (Open world in depth › Traversal).

**Studio detail (World and weather).**

**The order of freeze-up.** Each body walks the same steps, ponds first:

| Step | Trigger | What shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ponds skin over | First night below −5 °C; frozen over at a 3-day mean below −3 °C | Black ice, singing when stoned (Creative play) |
| Shore ice | Same, on rivers | `skim_ice` 1–3 blocks out from still edges |
| Frazil | Water at 0 °C and a night below −8 °C | Slush drifting, a hiss along the banks |
| Pans | 5-day mean below −8 °C | Round pans of slush with raised rims, drifting and grinding |
| Bridging | Pans jam at a narrow or slow reach | Ice grows upstream from the jam, 50–200 m a day |
| Closed | Bridge reaches the next riffle | Only riffles, springs and below-falls leads stay open |

Lakes close about a week before the river, as written. Pans are particles riding a `frazil_slush` layer, not entities, so they cost nothing on the server.

**Snow on new ice lies.** Snow on ice under 10 cm draws as grey "sugar" snow, never the white of safe ice. Fresh snow on any ice slows its growth by half for 3 days (insulation), so the first snowfall can leave thin ice longer.

**Testing, exact.** The axe, a pole or the walking stick reads the ice. Practice "ice reading" cuts the error from ±1 cm to ±0.5 cm after 20 tests. On Brutal the error is ±2 cm. A chiselled hole reads exactly. A tested spot keeps its reading for that day within 10 m, so the player is not asked to tap every step.

**The canoe season ends.** In shore ice the hull wears ×3; in slush paddling speed is ×0.5. A canoe or raft left in the water on a freeze night is frozen in by morning and takes 20 game minutes with the hatchet to free.

**Messenger, honestly.** The daily river line reports what can be seen from shore. Thickness shows only for spots the player has tested: "Lake: frozen. Tested 6 cm at the outlet."

**Sounds.** `yearofice:ice.frazil_hiss`, `ice.pan_grind`, `ice.test_tock` (under 5 cm), `test_dok` (5–10 cm), `test_thud` (over 10 cm), `ice.skim_tinkle` as skim breaks against a hull.

**Ties.** Walking stick (Viewers tab): the same three tones. Gear left on the ice (below). Wolves cross from 10 cm, moose later (Bestiary). Travel on ice (Open world in depth › Traversal).

**Build.** Skim ice is placed in the freeze pass at 03:00 on edge columns whose current speed is under 0.3 m/s, 4 chunks a tick. `IceChangedEvent` fires per step, which the messenger and the beaver food-pile goal read. Ships in M7.

**GameTests (m7).** `ice/skim_melts_by_noon`; `ice/test_reading_error`: 1,000 taps read within ±1 cm.

**Risk.** Tapping every block. Guard: the 10 m daily memory and the visible tracks of earlier crossings.

### Fire danger (Deepened)

**Missing so far.** Wildfire is a rare event, and an overpacked hearth may spread. There is no daily fire danger the player can read.

**The fire danger index.** The messenger shows one of four levels from snow-off (the thaw's Bare ground day) to August 31:

```latex
D = d_{dry} \cdot \left(1 + \frac{T_{max} - 15}{10}\right) \cdot \left(1 + \frac{w}{8}\right)
```

- d\_dry: days since 5 mm or more of rain, capped at 14.
- T\_max: today's high in °C, floor 15.
- w: mean wind in m/s.
- Low below 5, Moderate 5–12, High 12–25, Extreme above 25.

**What the level changes.**

| Level | Spark from an open fire to dry ground within 2 m, per game hour | Hearth "packed too full" spread chance per game hour |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Low | 0% | 2% |
| Moderate | 0.5% | 4% |
| High | 2% | 8% |
| Extreme | 6% | 15% |

A spark makes a ground fire 1 block wide that grows 1 block per 2 game minutes in dry moss, litter or grass. Stamping (use key, 3 s per block), a shovel of dirt or a pot of water puts out one block. Past 12 blocks a fire the player lit becomes the wildfire event; a lightning fire follows the rule in the studio detail below.

**Counterplay.** A fire ring of stones cuts the spark chance to a tenth; clearing 2 m of ground to bare mineral soil (shovel, 10 game minutes) to zero. A Dakota hole never sparks.

**Ties.** Fire states (Blueprint › Fire, water and light), wildfire (Open world in depth › Rare events), thunderstorms above, the land after fire (Land and seasons in play › Controlled burns).

**Build.** `com.yearofice.fire.FireDanger` computes D once per game day; spark checks run in the fire block entity's per-minute step. Ground fire is our own `yearofice:ground_fire` block, so vanilla fire spread stays off.

**Studio detail (World and weather).**

**The window starts at snow-off.** Interior fires often start in May on dead grass. The index runs from the "Bare ground" day of the thaw to August 31. Until green-up, dead grass and litter multiply D by 1.3. From September 1 the level shows "Low" and sparks follow the Low row.

**Rain and the counter.** 5 mm or more sets d\_dry to 0; 2–5 mm halves it; a dew-heavy misty morning does nothing.

**Worked values.**

| Days dry | High | Wind | D | Level |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | 18 °C | 2 m/s | 3.2 | Low |
| 5 | 20 °C | 3 m/s | 10.3 | Moderate |
| 7 | 25 °C | 4 m/s | 21 | High |
| 14 | 30 °C | 8 m/s | 70 | Extreme |

**Fuels.** Sparks catch on columns with dry moss, reindeer lichen, litter or grass. Gravel, wet bog moss, mineral soil and snow never catch.

**The spark gives a chance.** A spark first shows a 1-block smoulder with a smoke wisp and a hiss for 30 game seconds, then becomes ground fire. Stamping it in that window is free.

**Ground fire spread.** It grows 1 block per 2 game minutes, twice as fast downwind as upwind. More than 12 blocks hands over to the wildfire event.

**Lightning fires and the rare-event draw.** A lightning fire past 12 blocks becomes the wildfire event only if the year drew it, or the level is Extreme. Otherwise it burns out as a spot fire of one 32 m cell. A player's fire past 12 blocks always escalates. That keeps the rare draw rare, but lets the player cause a fire.

**Preset sparks.** Gentle ×0.5, Standard ×1, Brutal ×1.5.

**Readable without the messenger.** At High and above, dry moss crunches (`yearofice:step.dry_moss`), smoke haze from far fires lies in the valley, and leaves hang limp. At Extreme, Dana Reyes on the radio says: "Burn ban in the valley. No open fires."

**Ties.** Thunderstorms (above). Pitch lights (Viewers tab): a pitch torch drips at High and above. Controlled burns (Land and seasons in play) need Low. Coal seams (below) can catch from a wildfire.

**Build.** `fire.FireDanger` stores today's D in `yearofice_clock`. `ground_fire` uses scheduled ticks every 2 game minutes, since random ticks are off. At most 64 ground-fire blocks may exist near players; the 65th hands over to the wildfire event. Ships in M7.

**GameTests (m7).** `fire/danger_examples` checks the table. `fire/ring_cuts_spark`: 10,000 game hours at High with a stone ring give 0.2% ±0.05% per hour.

**Risk.** Campfires that feel like a trap. Guard: no sparks below Moderate, the 30-second smoulder window, and the ring or cleared ground fix.

### Misty mornings (Added)

**What it is.** River and lake mist on calm mornings in spring and fall, like the video's mist on April 24. Ice fog in deep winter is a separate rule (Land and seasons in play › Ice fog).

**Trigger.** Mist forms at dawn over a water body when all hold:

- open water (not iced),
- wind under 2 m/s,
- water at least 5 °C warmer than the air at 05:00,
- a clear or cloudy night (not Wet).

In practice that is late April to early June over the first open leads, and late August to freeze-up over rivers and lakes. Over 365 days it gives about 25–40 mist mornings.

**What it does.**

| Effect | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Extent | The water body plus 30–80 m inland on each side, thickest in the river corridor |
| Visibility inside | 40–80 m |
| Lifts | At 09:00–11:00, or as soon as wind passes 3 m/s |
| Fishing | Bites ×1.2 until it lifts: low light, calm surface |
| Animals | Moose and beaver stay out at the water 1 game hour later than usual |
| Scent | Carries along the corridor, not up the slopes: the scent plume (Blueprint › Senses by species) bends to follow the river |
| Spirits | +2 the first mist of each season |

**What the player sees and hears.** A white layer on the water at sunrise, thinning in bands. Sounds carry: a beaver slap or a loon call is heard 1.5× as far. A moose shape at 60 m shows as a dark outline before it can be identified.

**Risk.** In mist, an animal is identified at half the usual distance. A hunter can mistake a cow moose for a bull, or walk into a bear on a gravel bar.

**Counterplay.** Wait for it to lift, or use it: approach game on the river in cover of mist, downwind along the corridor.

**Ties.** Weather (Blueprint › Calendar, daylight and weather), fishing conditions, senses, Spirits.

**Build.** `com.yearofice.story.Mist` sets a per-water-body flag at 05:00 from the day's numbers. The client draws it as a low-lying fog volume using the `ViewportEvent.RenderFog` hook only when the camera is inside the mist zone, plus `yearofice:mist` particles over the water.

**Studio detail (World and weather).**

**Mist has height.** The layer is 2–6 m thick and covers only columns within 5 m of the water surface's height. From a bluff 20 m up, the river is a white ribbon with spruce tops above it: one of the valley's best views. Spirits +2 the first time per season it is seen from above.

**How it lifts.**

| Day | Lifts |
| --- | --- |
| Clear | Thins from 08:30, gone by 10:00 |
| Cloudy | Thins from 09:30, gone by 11:00 |
| Wind passes 3 m/s | Gone in 10 game minutes |

It thins in bands, 25% of thickness each 30 game minutes, so the far bank comes out of it first.

**Sound carries both ways.** The 1.5× range applies to everything: the player's paddle knock and axe carry as far to animals as their calls do to the player.

**Air drains downstream.** Inside the mist zone, cold air slides down the valley at 0.5–1 m/s, whatever the hourly wind. The player's scent goes downstream along the water, not upslope.

**Winter steam.** Over open leads and springs in winter, the same trigger is always true. It makes thin steam only: 10 m wide, visibility 150 m, no fishing bonus. Below −35 °C the ice fog rule takes over.

**Identification.** Inspect inside mist names only the shape: "Moose. Can't tell bull or cow." With binoculars (Items) it is named at 60 m instead of 30 m.

**Canoe danger.** Rocks and sweepers show at 20 m. The riffle sound comes first, at 80 m, as the warning.

**Year-1 example.** About 8–12 mist mornings in May on the first open leads, 15–25 from late August to freeze-up.

**Ties.** Fishing conditions (bites ×1.2). Senses (Minecraft engine › Senses, scent and wind). Ice fog (Land and seasons in play). Leaf fall: on a turning slope, yellow stands above the white is the screenshot day.

**Build.** `story.Mist` writes a per-body flag and band at 05:00. `env_sync` carries the list of mist chunks within 8 chunks of the player, up to 64 positions. The client fog runs only when the camera is in a mist column. `yearofice:mist` particles are capped at 120. Ships in M7.

**GameTests (m7).** `story/mist_count`: over 100 seeds, a mean of 25–40 mist mornings a year. `story/mist_needs_calm`: no morning with 05:00 wind of 2 m/s or more.

**Risk.** Lost players on a misty river. Guard: it is gone by 11:00, and the bank always leads home.

### Feathers on the water (Deepened)

**Missing so far.** Floating items drift and catch on snags. Nothing says where drifting feathers come from.

**Source.** Birds that live on or by water drop molt feathers into the current: loons, ducks, geese and gulls in July and August, about 1 feather per bird per 2 game days. Within 200 m upstream of a snag, that gives 1–4 feathers a week on a busy stretch.

**Catching them.** A felled tree lying across 30% or more of a channel's width is a snag. Feathers arriving there stop 90% of the time (existing snag rule), so a player who fells trees into the river, as the video did, builds a feather trap by accident.

**Value.** Each is a `yearofice:game_feather`, the fletching for blunt arrows. Three make one arrow.

**Ties.** Items in the river, one arrow per craft (Blueprint › Video checklist), loons (Bestiary › Common loon).

**Studio detail (World and weather).**

**A budget per reach.** Feathers come from a per-reach budget, not per snag: 1–4 a week for each 200 m of water with birds on it. More felled trees spread the same feathers over more snags, so they do not make more of them.

**Seasons.**

| Months | Source | Feathers a week per busy reach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| June | Ducks and loons begin molt | 1–2 |
| July – August | Geese flightless in molt; ducks, gulls | 2–4 |
| September | Migrants resting on lakes | 1–2 |
| October – May | None: ice | 0 |

**Goose primaries.** 1 drop in 5 in July and August is a `yearofice:goose_primary`. It splits with the knife (1 game minute) into 3 game feathers.

**Wind on still water.** On lakes and ponds, floating feathers move downwind at 0.2 m/s. Lines of feathers collect along the lee shore after a windy day. A player who walks the downwind beach finds them.

**Minecraft edge.** Vanilla item entities despawn after 5 real minutes. Drift feathers are item entities with `lifespan` set to 7 game days (3.5 real hours), after which they waterlog and vanish. Unloaded reaches do not tick. On load, each snag gets the feathers the reach's budget made since the last visit, capped at 12 per snag.

**Feel.** White specks sit against the trunk of a felled tree. The prompt says "Pick up feathers (3)". The first time, the journal writes: "The river brings feathers to the trees I dropped in it."

**Ties.** Blunt arrows and one arrow per craft (Blueprint › Video checklist). Loons and waterfowl (Bestiary). Logs, falls and jams (below): jams count as snags.

**Build.** `world.DriftFinds` owns the reach budget, keyed by 200 m reach id from the stage-4 river graph. Ships in M7.

**GameTest (m7).** `world/feather_budget`: one reach with 1 snag and the same reach with 6 snags each collect 1–4 feathers a week in July over 20 seeds.

**Risk.** Feather farming with sweepers on every bend. Guard: the reach budget.

### Logs, falls and jams (Deepened)

**Missing so far.** Trees fall with physics, logs float and jam, and a trunk can be a bridge. The engine tab allows log bridges but does not say how a felled tree becomes a moving log.

**Rules.**

1. **A falling tree is an entity.** On the last chop, the tree becomes `yearofice:falling_tree`, rotating about its base toward the notch side (Blueprint › Gathering materials). It lands in 1.5–3 s.
2. **Landing.** On ground, it becomes a `yearofice:felled_log` entity (one per 4 m length) that can be bucked into logs. Landing on a player is a falling-tree direct hit, −40 Condition, by the rule in Body, injury and sickness (RECONCILE 126), as in the video's ice-smash.
3. **On water.** A trunk landing in water becomes a `yearofice:drift_log` entity moving at the surface current. On ice under 15 cm it breaks the ice (blocks within 1 block of the trunk become water), as in chapter 18.
4. **Over a falls.** A drift log reaching a falls drops, spins and lands in the pool. 20% of the time it pins against rocks below and starts a jam.
5. **Jams.** Two or more logs pinned within 4 m form a `yearofice:log_jam` block structure. A jam catches drift items, slows the current behind it by 30%, and grows by each new log. Breakup clears jams under 4 logs; larger ones stay a year.
6. **Bridges.** A felled trunk resting on both banks with at least 1 m on each is walkable. Crossing it is a balance check: on wet bark or frost, a misstep chance of 2% per metre, ×3 when the player's energy is 3 or below. A misstep drops the player into the water below.
7. **Rolling rocks.** A loose boulder (`yearofice:loose_boulder`, 1 per 2 scree slopes) can be pushed with 3 s of holding use. It rolls as an entity down slopes over 20° and stops in water or on flat ground. It has no reward beyond the sound, the splash and Spirits +1 once a day.

**What the player sees and hears.** A rising creak before the fall, a whump on landing, a hollow knock as drift logs hit rocks. Logs going over a falls spin end over end.

**Ties.** Items in the river, crossings (Structures and buildables › Water power, crossings and craft), ice, the creative pull of play (Blueprint › Gameplay ideas from the video).

**Build.** All three are custom entities in `com.yearofice.world.logs`, sharing the current field from the chunk's `current` attachment. A jam is placed as blocks once two logs come to rest, so it saves with the chunk.

**Studio detail (World and weather).**

**Falling tree numbers.**

| Trunk | Height | Fall time | Hit by trunk | Hit by crown |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10 cm | 8 m | 1.5 s | 10 Condition | 3, knock-down |
| 25 cm | 16 m | 2.2 s | 25 | 5, knock-down |
| 40 cm | 25 m | 3.0 s | 40 | 8, knock-down |

The trunk line is 1 block wide; the crown covers 3 blocks either side for its top third. The creak plays 1 s before the last chop lands, and the fall arrow from the notch shows during the chop.

**Drift log motion.**

- Moves at the current V; on still water at 0.1 m/s downwind.
- Swings to the outside of bends and beaches where V drops under 0.3 m/s.
- A log pinned against a sweeper becomes a new snag (tag `yearofice:snags`).
- At freeze-up a drift log freezes in place as a `frozen_log` block and is freed at breakup.

**Jams in the year.** A jam of 8 logs or more raises the spring flood line 1 m for 200 m upstream at breakup. A pool forms below every jam: fishing structure, bites ×1.2 (Waters › reading the water).

**Breaking a jam.** Prying the key log free takes 1 game hour with a pole or a rope. The jam lets go 100% of the time when the key log goes, and a player standing on it falls in 30% of the time. Standing on the bank to pry is safe but takes ×2 as long.

**Rolling rocks.** A boulder in motion hits anything in its path for 30 Condition. Animals in the path dodge 95% of the time and flee, and give no meat. Pieces hit take 100 HP. Spirits +1 once per day.

**Minecraft build.**

- `falling_tree` lives 3 s at most, then becomes `felled_log` entities.
- `drift_log` entities persist and are capped at 40 per region; past the cap, the oldest beaches.
- Physics runs only within 128 blocks of a player. Past that, a log steps along the flow field once a real second.
- On chunk load, a log walks the flow field for the elapsed minutes × V, up to 2 km, stopping at the first snag, jam, beach or ice.

**Ties.** Feathers on the water (above). Firewood drying: beached logs are driftwood, 25%. Snag knock-down (Viewers tab). Spring floods (Minecraft engine › Rivers, current and water).

**GameTests (m8).** `world/log_jam_two_logs`: two logs pinned within 4 m become a jam within 1 game minute. `world/log_catchup`: a log in a chunk unloaded for 2 game hours on a 1 m/s reach is found at the next snag or 2 km downstream.

**Risk.** Entity creep. Guard: the region cap and jams turning into blocks.

### Lily pads (Added)

**What it is.** Yellow pond-lily pads on still, shallow water. In the video they stopped the raft during the beaver chase.

**Where and when.** On ponds, oxbows, beaver ponds and lake shallows 0.5–2 m deep, with no current. They cover 10–40% of such water from June 10 to the first frost below −2 °C, when they blacken and sink over 5 days.

**Rules.**

| Effect | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Raft or canoe speed through pads | ×0.4 |
| Paddle snag | 10% per paddle stroke in thick pads; the stroke is lost |
| Casting into pads | Hook snags 30% of casts; pike bite ×1.3 at the edge |
| Moose | Wade in to feed on pads and roots, 05:00–09:00 and 19:00–23:00 in July |
| Beaver | Eat the rootstocks; a colony keeps a channel through the pads to its lodge |
| Food | Rootstock (dig with a stick from a canoe, thigh-deep water): 400 kcal per kg, mostly carbs; bitter until boiled twice |

**What the player sees.** Flat green pads with yellow cup flowers in July. A clear lane through them points to a lodge.

**Counterplay.** Pole around them along the beaver lane, or approach from the open side.

**Ties.** Rafts and canoes (Blueprint › Video checklist), beavers, moose feeding, fishing structure (Blueprint › Reading the water), carbs.

**Build.** `yearofice:lily_pad` is a waterlogged surface block with an `age` state, placed by a worldgen feature in still water and removed by the season pass. Raft drag reads a tag `yearofice:slows_boats`.

**Studio detail (World and weather).**

**The year of a pad bed.** The block's `age` state follows the calendar:

| Age | Dates | Look | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 Bud | June 1–10 | Red-brown leaves just under the surface | None |
| 1 Pad | June 10 – July 1 | Flat green pads | Drag, snags |
| 2 Flower | July | Yellow cup flowers | Moose feeding peak |
| 3 Pod | August 10 – September 15 | Ribbed green pods | Seeds |
| 4 Black | First frost below −2 °C, for 5 days | Black, sinking | Drag halves daily |

**Seeds.** Ripe pods give 100 g of seeds per 10 pods, picked from a canoe at 10 pods per 2 game minutes. Parched at a fire for 5 game minutes, they pop open and give 350 kcal per 100 g, keeping 1 year dry. Item `yearofice:lily_seeds`.

**Rootstock, exactly.** Rootstock digging works only in open water. It takes thigh-deep wading or a canoe and a stick. Each 10 game minutes gives 1–2 kg and wets the legs zone, with water temperature from the cold-water rules. Boiling twice takes 20 game minutes each, and water must be changed between. Eaten once boiled it causes cramps 30% of the time.

**Lanes.** Each canoe pass clears a 1-block lane that stays open 7 days. A beaver lane stays open all summer and always points to the lodge or a feeding spot.

**Not the vanilla pad.** `minecraft:lily_pad` lets the player walk on water, so it is hidden and banned from worldgen. Ours has no collision for players or animals. Boats read the drag tag.

**Moose.** A pond with at least 25% pads draws moose 2× as often in July in the listed hours. A moose belly-deep is slow to leave water, which makes it the safest close look at a moose in summer and a real shot.

**Pike tackle.** A weedless hook (a bent-wire guard, Iron tier) cuts snags from 30% to 10% of casts.

**Sounds.** `yearofice:water.paddle_in_pads` (soft drag), and wood frogs chorusing from pad edges in late May.

### Dams you can watch grow (Deepened)

### Firewood drying (Deepened)

**Missing so far.** Seasoned, green and wet wood burn differently, and a woodshed dries wood 1.3 times as fast (B4, Proposed; was twice as fast). The video's last complaint was that it was unclear how wood gets dry. This makes it readable.

**Moisture.** Every firewood stack carries a moisture percentage, shown on hover: "Birch, split: moisture 34%. Smoky, 60% heat."

| Wood | Moisture when cut |
| --- | --- |
| Green spruce or birch | 50% |
| Dead standing spruce | 18% |
| Driftwood on a bar | 25% |
| Ground deadfall | 35%, 60% after rain |

**Heat by moisture.**

```latex
H = H_0 \cdot \mathrm{clamp}\left(1 - \frac{m - 20}{40},\ 0.2,\ 1\right)
```

Below 20% moisture, wood burns at full heat; at 50% it gives 25%; at 60% or more it smoulders at 20% with twice the smoke.

**Drying per game day.**

| Month (B4, Proposed) | Split, covered rack | Split, woodshed (×1.3) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| April, after melt-out | −0.10 | −0.13 |
| May | −0.45 | −0.59 |
| June | −0.45 | −0.59 |
| July | −0.35 | −0.46 |
| August | −0.30 | −0.39 |
| September | −0.15 | −0.20 |
| October, while the daily mean is above 0 °C | −0.05 | −0.07 |
| November–March | 0 | 0 |

| Where (B4, Proposed) | Rate |
| --- | --- |
| Split, stack with no top cover | ×0.7 of the month's rate, and +1.5 per rainy day |
| Unsplit rounds | ×0.4 of the split rate |
| Whole logs, birch rounds in their bark | ×0.25 |
| Split, inside a warm room within 3 m of a stove (unchanged) | −4.0 in any month |
| Any, snowed on without cover (unchanged) | +2 per snow day |

**Before B4** (restore on a veto): per game day, scattered unsplit logs −0.8 summer, −0.3 fall, −0.1 winter; split on a covered rack −1.5, −0.6, −0.2; split in the woodshed −3.0, −1.2, −0.4; rained on without cover +5 per wet day. The calendar and its sources are in Crafting and building in depth › Trees, felling and firewood › B4 Seasoning by the calendar.

So green split birch put in the woodshed on June 1 is seasoned by about July 29 (B4, Proposed; was early July), while scattered logs in October will not be dry before the next summer (B4, Proposed; was spring). Bringing tomorrow's armloads inside next to the stove dries them overnight, at the cost of space.

**Ties.** Fire (Blueprint › Fire, water and light), woodshed and woodpile (Blueprint › Making camp), warm rooms (Crafting and building in depth › Warm rooms), smoke indoors.

**Build.** Moisture is a data component `yearofice:moisture` on firewood items and a block-entity field on woodpile and woodshed blocks, stepped on `ClockEvent.Day` in closed form for unloaded chunks.

**Softwood and hardwood.** The fuel numbers below expand the existing rule that birch holds a fire through a −40 °C night. They are relative to one armload of seasoned spruce and derive from the Blueprint's Wood table (Fire, water and light), where spruce burns ×0.7 at heat ×1.0 and birch ×1.4 at heat ×1.1: birch therefore burns 2.0 times as long and gives about 2.2 times the heat per armload (RECONCILE 126). The Blueprint table wins if the two ever differ.

**Studio detail (World and weather).**

**Moisture in bands.** Moisture is stored in 5-point steps (10, 15 … 60), clamped to 10–60. Stacks with the same band merge, so firewood stacks work like freshness stacks. A stack merged from two bands takes the weighted mean, rounded up.

**More drying rules.**

- An unsplit round dries ×0.4 of the split rate (B4, Proposed; was ×0.5). Whole logs and birch rounds in their bark dry ×0.25.
- Wood above 35% that lies on bare ground for 1 year turns punky: heat ×0.6 for good. Punky rounds hold grubs (Viewers tab › Grubs and ant brood).
- Below −15 °C, frozen rounds split in half the time.
- Wood within 1 m of a stove on Hot catches 1% of game hours. The 3 m drying spot holds 4 armloads at most.

**Wet wood has uses.**

| Use | Best moisture | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Smudge against mosquitoes | 40–60% | Smoke ×2, bites ×0.5 within 5 m (Blueprint › Bugs) |
| Signal column | 50%+ | The 8 km green-wood smoke column |
| Smoking meat | 25–35% willow or alder | Even smoke, low flame |
| Night fire | Under 20% | Full heat, few sparks |

**Hover lines.**

**Worked year.** Birch cut green on June 1 and split into the woodshed is under 20% by about July 29 (B4, Proposed; was June 11). Scattered rounds cut on October 1 are still at about 50% on April 1, because nothing dries from November to March (B4, Proposed; was 25%).

### Coal seams (Added)

**What it is.** Exposed coal in the rock of the north range and in one creek's cut banks, as on the video's winter trip to the mountain for coal, iron and mud. Interior Alaska has real surface coal, so it fits the valley and the pillar Explore, don't dig.

**Where.** Generated by stage 5 (resources) beside the iron rules:

- 3–6 seams per world, each a band 1 block high and 4–10 blocks long in the outer shell of face rock or a cut bank.
- At least 1 seam between 1.5 and 3 km from the start, none within 600 m.
- One creek per world is marked Coal Creek: its gravel bars below a seam hold 2–6 pieces of float coal after each breakup.
- Never below the generated surface. The engine's no-nodes-below test applies to coal unchanged.

**What the player sees.** A black, shiny band in grey rock, often with rust stain below. Looking at it within 20 m: "A black seam in the rock: coal." Float coal on a bar is black, light pebbles that the rock-pick-up prompt names.

**Taking it.**

| Source | Tool | Time | Yield | Renewal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Seam block | Pick | 3 game minutes | 1 lump, 2 kg | 6 lumps per block, then it turns to face rock |
| Float coal | Hands | 2 s | 1 piece, 0.3 kg | 2–6 pieces per bar each breakup |
| Rockfall | Hands | — | 1–3 lumps | A rockfall on a seam slope exposes 1 new seam block (Land and seasons in play › Rockfall) |

**Uses.**

| Use | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Stove fuel | 1 lump = 2.5 armloads of spruce in heat, burns 3× as long; needs an existing bed of coals to catch |
| Open campfire | Burns at 0.6× without a draft; not worth hauling for camp |
| Forge | 1 lump replaces 1.5 charcoal at the forge, saving a charcoal burn |
| Bloomery | Not used: sulfur makes the bloom brittle (bar quality −15%); the messenger says so the first time |
| Smoke | ×2 smoke indoors; needs a chimney or stovepipe |

**The danger.** A coal fire in a closed room without a working chimney builds bad air. After 2 game hours the player gets "Headache"; after 4, Condition −5 an hour and sleep cannot start. A player asleep in such a room is woken by the headache at the 2-hour mark: the rule warns before it hurts.

**Why carry it.** A winter cabin burns 2–5 armloads a day (Crafting and building in depth › Warm rooms). Ten lumps (20 kg, one pack trip) replace about 25 armloads, so a coal trip before freeze-up buys about a week of woodcutting in January.

**Ties.** Resources and the iron trips (Blueprint › Biomes and resources), fuel above, forge (Open world in depth › Outposts), warm rooms, weight limits.

**Build.** `yearofice:coal_seam` is a face-rock-shell block with a `remaining` property (0–6). Float coal is a ground item placed by the breakup pass. Data: `data/yearofice/worldgen/placed_feature/coal_seam.json` with a custom placement modifier `yearofice:shell_only`.

**Studio detail (World and weather).**

**What kind of coal.** Valley coal is low-grade, like the real interior coal near Healy: soft, brown-black and crumbly. Lumps left outdoors in a dry summer slake to coal fines in 60 game days: 50% heat, usable only in a stove. Under a roof they keep.

**Wet coal.** Coal that is rained on or hauled through water burns at 0.8 until it has spent 2 dry days.

**Seam fires.** A wildfire that reaches a seam block lights it 10% of the time. A burning seam smokes from a crack all year, keeps snow off 3 blocks around it, and holds feels-like +8 °C in a 2-block radius. It burns out after 2–5 years and leaves red baked rock (`yearofice:clinker`, a hard stone for hearths). Holdover fires (Viewers tab) use the same smoke and warm-ground code.

**A story tie.** Kessler's ledger (1908) records "the black seam on the creek, good for the forge." That is how Coal Creek got its name on the old map. Finding the ledger pins the creek on the map.

**Supply over a world.**

| Source | Lumps |
| --- | --- |
| Seams: 3–6 × 4–10 blocks × 6 | 72–360 in all, once |
| Float coal, Coal Creek | 2–6 pieces per breakup, about 0.3–0.9 lumps' worth |
| Rockfall | About 1 new seam block every few years |

Coal is finite in practice: a stove fuel to stretch woodcutting, not to replace it.

**Bad air, exact.** It applies only to coal in a closed room without a working chimney or stovepipe. An open door or a 1 × 1 vent counts as working. Headache shows on the vitals strip; the messenger line is "Headache. Bad air in here?" Leaving the room clears it in 30 game minutes.

**Tools.** Only an iron pick or rock pick takes a seam block. The hatchet and a hand stone cannot.

**Ties.** Firewood drying (above). Forge (Open world in depth › Outposts). The north-range iron trips. Kessler and Doane (Blueprint › story).

**Build.** `worldgen/placed_feature/coal_seam.json` with `yearofice:shell_only`. Seam fires are a `burning=true` state on `coal_seam`, stepped daily. `body.BadAir` reads the room model's chimney flag. Ships in M8.

**GameTests (m8).** `worldgen/coal_seam_count`: 200 seeds, 3–6 seams each, at least 1 at 1.5–3 km, none within 600 m. `body/bad_air_wakes`: a sleeper in a closed coal room wakes at the 2-hour mark.

**Risk.** Coal as a solved fuel. Guard: finite seams, slaking outdoors and the bad-air rule.

### Mud and packed mud (Added)

**What it is.** Wet earth dug from banks and seeps, used raw or stomped into packed mud blocks. The video gathered mud blocks on the mountain and liked packed mud for building.

**Where to dig.** Mud comes only from the top soil block (the 2-block dig rule holds):

- river and pond banks within 3 m of water,
- bog edges,
- any soil during mud season (Land and seasons in play › Mud season),
- spring seeps and the hot-spring outflow, which stay unfrozen all winter, so mud can be had in December as in the video.

From October to breakup, other ground is frozen and cannot be dug (Minecraft engine › Ice, snow and permafrost).

**Items and blocks.**

| Thing | Made from | Time | Weight | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mud clod `yearofice:mud_clod` | Shovel on a mud source | 30 game seconds | 5 kg | 4 per block dug |
| Packed mud `yearofice:packed_mud` | 4 clods and 1 bundle of dry grass, stomped on flat ground | Hold 20 s (practice cuts to 10 s) | 22 kg | Soft for 2 dry days, then hard |
| Mud plaster | 2 clods and 1 grass bundle, smeared on a wall face | 2 game minutes per m² | — | A face state on log or dugout walls |
| Mud brick | Existing (Blueprint › Gathering materials) | — | — | Still the chimney material |

**What packed mud does.**

- **Floors and berms.** A packed-mud floor or an earth berm against an outside wall adds insulation like 10 cm of moss (Crafting and building in depth › Warm rooms). A berm 1 block thick against a log wall cuts that wall's heat loss by 30%.
- **Plaster.** Mud plaster on log walls inside a cabin stops flame damage to the logs behind it while it is intact. It cracks at 10% per year and needs patching.
- **Dugout lining.** Plastered dugout walls stop the drip that wets a dugout in a thaw (see Dugouts and rock shelters).

**Failure.** Rain on soft packed mud turns half of it back to clods. Packed mud in a wall below the flood line washes out at breakup.

**Ties.** Building pieces (Crafting and building in depth › Building pieces), warm rooms, fire risk (Structures and buildables › Fire risk), mud season, dugouts.

**Build.** Two blocks and one item; the stomp is a hold-to-work job in `com.yearofice.build.EarthWork`. Plaster is a block-state `plaster=true` on wall blocks, so it saves with the block.

**Studio detail (World and weather).**

**Mud and frost.**

- A clod below 0 °C for 6 game hours freezes. A frozen clod cannot be stomped or smeared until it has spent 1 game hour within 3 m of a fire.
- Packed mud placed below 5 °C cures at half rate. Placed below 0 °C, it stays soft until the spring thaw.
- A rain day resets the 2-dry-day cure count.
- Frost heave cracks outdoor plaster at 10% per winter, on top of the 10% a year.

**Handling.** Soft packed mud cannot be picked up. A cured block is a 22 kg hand-carried load in the carry slot, 1 at a time. A packed-mud wall stands 3 blocks high without a log frame; a 4th block needs posts.

**Fire.** Packed mud never burns and is tagged `yearofice:room_walls` and non-burnable. A mud-plastered wall is the safe wall behind a stove (Structures and buildables › Fire risk).

**Mud on skin.** Smearing mud on face and hands (use a clod on yourself, 10 game seconds) cuts bug bites ×0.5 for 2 game hours. It dries, itches (1 itch tick an hour) and adds grime +10 (Viewers tab › Cauterizing and grime).

**Tells for a mud source.** A source block shows a wet, darker top texture and leaves footprints that hold water. The prompt names it: "Dig mud (shovel)." On a frozen block, the prompt says "Frozen ground", as the engine rule says.

**Numbers for heat.**

| Block | U (W/m²K) | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Packed mud, 1 m | 0.9 | Like a log wall with moss chinking |
| Berm against a log wall | Wall loss −30% | As written |
| Plastered log wall | Seam loss 0 | Plaster also seals chinking |

**Not vanilla mud.** `minecraft:mud`, `packed_mud` and `mud_bricks` are hidden and their recipes filtered. Our ids carry the cure state and weight.

**Ties.** Dugouts and rock shelters (Survival, in this tab). Warm rooms (Crafting and building in depth). The thaw: any soil is a source from Bare ground. Late mosquitoes (below).

**Build.** Tag `yearofice:mud_sources`; within-3-m-of-water is a 7 × 3 × 7 scan, cached per block for 1 game day. `packed_mud` has `cured` 0–2. Heat values go in `data_maps/block/heat.json`. Ships in M8.

**GameTests (m8).** `build/packed_mud_cures`: cured after 2 dry days at 10 °C, and rain on day 1 resets it. `build/frozen_ground_no_mud`: no clod from a bank on November 15 unless it is a spring or seep.

**Risk.** Mud walls as a cheap cabin. Guard: 22 kg blocks, curing time and washout below the flood line.

### Winter's dead (Added)

**What it is.** At the thaw, the bodies of animals that died over winter appear, like the video's dead bird on the ice and dead wolf in spring. They are the visible part of winter losses the population model already counts.

**Where they come from.** Each region's population step records winter deaths: starvation, cold, drowning through ice, predator kills not finished. At snowmelt, 30% of those deaths in loaded or visited regions become carcasses, at most 6 per region.

| Carcass | Where | What the player can take | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Small bird (redpoll, grouse, jay) | On lake ice or snow under spruce | 3–10 feathers | Ravens find it within 2 days |
| Hare | Under willow, often half-eaten | Nothing edible; bones | Tells a lynx or fox works here |
| Wolf | Open snow on a ridge | Poor pelt (quality 30%), teeth, bone | Usually starved or killed by its pack |
| Moose | In a creek bed or deep snow | Antlers if a bull, bone; meat only if found before April 10 | Draws bears out of dens within 3 days |
| Beaver | Below a failed lodge | Pelt at 40% | Rare |

**Reading them.** Looking at a carcass gives the cause in one line: "Starved: no fat on it." "Killed and left: wolf tracks round it." The messenger never lists them; the player finds them on foot.

**Why it matters.** A spring moose carcass is a bear magnet: bears out of dens go to it first. Finding one near camp in April is a warning; finding one far away is a place to hunt a bear from cover.

**Ties.** Populations (Minecraft engine › Populations), bears in spring (Blueprint › Who hunts whom), tracks and sign, ravens and eagles (Bestiary).

**Build.** A carcass is the carcass entity of its species' size class (`carcass_tiny` to `carcass_huge`, all in the tag `#yearofice:carcasses` over the base class `ecosystem.carcass.CarcassEntity`; RECONCILE 41) with a species and `cause` field, the same entity used for kills, created by the snowmelt pass from the region's death log.

**Studio detail (World and weather).**

**Where each carcass goes.** The death log stores cause and a 16 m habitat cell for each death. At snowmelt, each carcass appears when its own column's snow class melts out: open ridges in early April, under spruce in early May.

| Cause | Placed |
| --- | --- |
| Starved, cold | Under spruce or willow in the species' winter habitat |
| Drowned through ice | On the shore or at the first eddy below the hole, after breakup |
| Killed and left | In the open, with old tracks if the sign attachment still holds them |
| Avalanche or fall (mountain regions) | At the foot of a slope over 30° |

**Edible or not.** Carcass meat is edible only if it has had fewer than 48 game hours above 0 °C, which usually means before April 10. Inspect says "Still frozen through" or "Thawed. Rotten."

**Decay.**

| Days after melt-out | State | Takeable |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–2 | Whole or half-eaten | As the table above |
| 3–30 | Scavenged by ravens, foxes and bears | Bones, antlers, teeth |
| 30–730 | Bones bleaching | Bones, antlers |
| Over 2 years (bone piles last 2 years, as in the Bestiary) | Removed | — |

**Inspect lines.** "Starved: no fat on it." "Killed and left: wolf tracks round it." "Drowned: it came up with the ice." "Old: the ravens have been." "Fell: the slope above is bare rock."

**Counts.** Each region shows 2–4 carcasses after a normal winter and 5–6 after a hard winter. Regions not visited by June 30 lose that year's carcasses.

**Bear guard.** No moose carcass is placed within 150 m of a bed the player has slept in. It moves to the nearest valid cell. A bear magnet near camp is a warning, not an ambush.

**First time.** The first carcass found writes the journal: "Winter took this one. It could have been me." Spirits −1 once, the only loss here.

**Ties.** Populations (Minecraft engine). Bears in spring and scent points (strength 3). Ravens (Bestiary). The thaw (above).

**GameTest (m7).** `ecosystem/winter_dead_cap`: after a hard winter with 40 logged deaths in one region, 6 carcasses appear and none is within 150 m of the bed.

**Risk.** Bone farming. Guard: the cap of 6, and bones being common anyway.

## Survival

### Dugouts and rock shelters (Added)

**What it is.** A shelter dug into a hillside or cut bank, or built into a natural rock overhang, closed with a log front, a door and a fireplace. The video's winter home was exactly this: dug into a slope by a rock face, with a stone-brick fireplace and a mud-brick chimney.

**Reconciling it with Explore, don't dig.** Pillar 3 is about resources: nothing of value is found by digging down. A dugout gives living space, never resources, so it keeps the pillar. It is built the way the root cellar already is (Minecraft engine › Explore, don't dig, rule 4): a building piece that clears its own footprint and nothing else.

**Three kinds.**

| Kind | Where it can go | Interior, largest | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hillside dugout | A soil or till slope of 30° or more, with a face at least 3 blocks high | 4 wide × 2 high × 6 deep | The common one; north range foothills, river terraces |
| Cut-bank dugout | A cut bank at least 2 m high, above the flood line | 3 wide × 2 high × 4 deep | Close to water; the placement check flags the flood line |
| Natural rock shelter | A generated overhang or alcove in face rock | 3–5 wide × 2–3 high × 2–4 deep, as generated | No digging; may be enlarged by 1 block of face rock only |

**Where rock shelters come from.** Stage 5 places 1–3 overhangs per region with face rock, and 4–8 in the canyon archetype ("dig-in rock shelters", Blueprint › Valley archetypes). At least one sits within 1 km of the start on every seed. They show as a dark recess with a dry floor; the site check says "Rock shelter: dry, out of the wind."

**The gameplay loop.**

1. Find a site: a slope or bank the right height, facing away from the north wind, above the flood line, wood within 100 m.
2. Place the dugout ghost. The site check runs its six tests (Blueprint › Making camp), plus three for dugouts: soil depth, permafrost, and slope above the mouth.
3. Dig in stages, 1 m³ at a time, as a queued job. Each 2 m of depth needs a shoring frame before the next stage.
4. Close the front with a log or stone wall, a door and a window.
5. Build a hearth and chimney inside (next section), then move the pot and bed in.

**Digging numbers.**

| Ground | Time per m³ with a shovel | Without a shovel (digging stick) | kcal per m³ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Soil | 20 game minutes | 50 game minutes | 180 |
| Gravel or till | 40 game minutes | Not possible | 360 |
| Frozen (October to breakup) | Not possible | Not possible | — |

A first 3 × 2 × 4 m dugout is 24 m³ in soil: 8 game hours of digging and about 4,300 kcal, plus 2 shoring frames (2 game hours). That is two or three summer days at the video's pace. Spoil goes to an earth berm at the mouth automatically; each m³ of spoil adds 1 m² of berm.

**Shoring.** A frame is 2 posts and a cap log (3 poles, 1 log, 10 game minutes each). An unshored span longer than 2 m from the face or the last frame risks a cave-in on wet days.

| Condition | Cave-in chance per unshored metre per game day |
| --- | --- |
| Dry day | 0% |
| Rain day, or a thaw day in winter | 5% |
| Mud season or breakup flood | 10% |

A cave-in drops 1 m³ of soil. A player under it takes 20–50 Condition and is pinned: hold use for 10 s to dig out. The messenger names the cause: "The roof came down where it was not shored."

**Heat.** Earth walls hold the room near ground temperature, so a dugout is the cheapest warm room in the game. The room-finding rules (Crafting and building in depth › Warm rooms) accept earth as a boundary. Each earth wall, roof or floor face loses heat to the ground, not the air:

```latex
L_{earth} = 0.5 \cdot A_{earth}, \qquad T_{ground} = \mathrm{clamp}(T_{5day},\ -3,\ 8)
```

Earth holds 4 Wh/°C per m², so a dugout cools slowly after the fire dies. Only the front wall, door and window lose heat to outside air.

Worked through: a 3 × 4 m dugout 2 m high has about 46 m² of earth faces (L\_earth 23 W/°C) and a log front with L\_air near 10 W/°C. On a −35 °C day with only the player inside, it holds about −10 °C. A fireplace on Low (about 4 armloads a day) keeps it near 0 °C; a barrel stove on Low keeps it near +20 °C. The same fuel in an unchinked lean-to gives almost nothing.

**Dry and damp.** Bare earth walls drip on thaw days and in mud season: bedding inside gets 10% wet a game hour unless the walls are plastered with mud (see Mud and packed mud) or lined with bark. A dugout below a slope steeper than 35° needs a retaining wall over the mouth (see Camp finishing pieces) or rockfall and slumping can block the door.

**Permafrost.** On permafrost columns (1 diggable block over frozen soil) a dugout cannot be dug. The placement check says "Permafrost: no dugout here." A rock shelter on permafrost ground is fine.

**Safety.** Bears and wolves cannot dig through earth or rock; the door is the only way in, so the existing door and siege rules apply (Blueprint › Video checklist, wolves). Earth and stone do not burn: in a house fire only the wooden front, door, shoring and awning take damage, as in the video.

**Failure and counterplay.**

| Mistake | Cost | Counterplay |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Digging deep without shoring | Cave-in on the first wet day | Frame every 2 m |
| A cut-bank dugout below the flood line | Floods at breakup; stored food spoils | Heed the amber flood warning |
| Starting in September | Ground freezes in October; the dugout is half done | Dig in June–August |
| A fire with no chimney | Smoke rule: no sleep, Condition loss | Build the chimney first |

**Ties.** Warm rooms, building pieces and queued jobs (Crafting and building in depth), the site check (Blueprint › Making camp), permafrost and flood lines (Structures and buildables), energy and calories, fire risk.

**Minecraft build.** `yearofice:dugout` is a multiblock building piece. Its ghost is validated by `com.yearofice.build.DugoutPlacement`: face height, slope, soil depth from the column data, permafrost flag, flood line. Each dug m³ replaces the piece's footprint blocks with `yearofice:dugout_air` (an air-like block that marks the space as part of the piece) even where those blocks were deep ground. Nothing outside the footprint changes. Shoring frames are `yearofice:shoring_post` and `yearofice:shoring_cap`. The heat model adds an `earth` face type in `data/yearofice/data_maps/block/heat.json`.

**Studio detail (Building and crafting).** Dugouts and rock shelters.

**Build sequence in blocks.** This is the first hillside dugout, 3 × 2 × 4 blocks inside, in soil, with a shovel.

1. **Ghost.** Place `yearofice:dugout` against a slope face. The ghost shows the 24 interior cells, the berm cells and the frame positions. A red ghost names the first check that failed.
2. **Mouth frame (frame 0).** Two `shoring_post` blocks a side and a 3-block `shoring_cap` at the face. It carries the front wall header and the portal roof.
3. **Stages 1–2.** Dig the first 2 blocks of depth: 12 cells, each one queued dig job of 20 game minutes, clock ×8.
4. **Frame 1 at 2 m.** Stage 3 stays locked until it stands: "Shore it before you dig on."
5. **Stages 3–4.** The last 12 cells. The back wall is earth and counts as a support, so 4 deep needs 2 frames and 6 deep needs 3.
6. **Portal roof.** Wherever earth cover over a cell is under 1 block, usually the first 1–2 m on a 30° slope, the ghost adds a sod roof on the mouth cap. It is an ordinary piece: heavy span (40 per block) in SupportGraph, SnowLoad at the 60 cm cap, and rot.
7. **Front.** A sill, 3 log wall blocks, a door and a hide window fill the 3 × 2 opening.
8. **Inside.** Hearth and chimney (next entry), a bunk, then lining.

| Step | Game hours | kcal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dig 24 m³ of soil | 8 | 4,320 |
| 2 shoring frames | 2 | 900 |
| Sod portal roof, 3 blocks | 2.5 | 1,130 |
| Sill, 3 log wall blocks, hide flap, hide window | 6 | 2,700 |

| Bunk | 2 | 900 |
| **Total** | **about 27** | **about 12,600** |

**Why it does not replace the cabin.** The first cabin is 160 game hours and 70,000 kcal (Structures and buildables › Example homesteads). The dugout is a sixth of that, and it pays for it every year.

- **Dark.** One window makes the room card read Dark. A Dark sleeping room costs −1 Spirits a day from November to February; one lit lantern lifts it to Dim (see Placed lanterns).
- **Damp.** Unlined earth wets bedding on thaw and mud-season days, and stored hides and furs lose 1% condition a day then. Lining is `yearofice:lining`, a face block like glow lichen: mud (2 clods and 1 grass bundle per m², 2 game minutes) or bark (1 bark sheet per m², 3 game minutes). The 3 × 4 room has 34 m² of wall and ceiling.
- **No growth.** One room, 6 deep at most, no under-floor cellar: the floor is deep ground ("Deep ground. No pit here.").
- **Upkeep.** About 10–20 game hours a year: lining patches, re-shoring, slump digs. It goes on the upkeep page like chinking.

**Cave-in, exact rules.**

- One roll a game day at dawn for each unshored slice (1 block of depth across the full width), at the table's chance. Gentle ×0.5, Brutal ×1.5.
- A hit picks one hour of that day. One `yearofice:slump` block lands in a random cell of the slice. Digging it out is a normal 20-minute dig job that adds no size and gives nothing.
- **Tell.** A soil trickle and dust 5 s before (Gentle 10 s, Brutal 3 s), the earth version of the rockfall cue. Stepping out of the slice in time avoids the hit.
- A post at Rotten, a heaved post or a burned cap stops shoring; its slices roll again. Lining and retaining walls never shore. Only frames do.
- Missed days in unloaded chunks roll on load from `hash(seed, pos, day)`; reloading never rerolls. Pinned at 0 Condition ends a Hardcore run; in Endless it is Collapse and SOS.

| Shoring post | Rot a year |
| --- | --- |
| Unpeeled, on bare earth, unlined room | 30% (Rotten in under 3 years) |
| Peeled, on bare earth | 6% |
| Peeled, on a flat stone, lined room | 1% |

**Heat and smoke, edge cases.**

- Earth and rock round `dugout_air` carry the tag `yearofice:earth_faces`, which sits in `room_walls` and `room_roofs`. Posts and caps are thin blocks tagged `yearofice:room_passable`, so the room flood fill passes them.
- Earth cover under 1 block outside the sod portal is red: "Roof too thin. Needs 1 m of earth or a sod roof." SnowLoad skips natural ground, so only the portal and any awning carry snow.
- A mouth on a lee-drift column takes the drift after every storm over 10 m/s. The dugout door opens inward, so the player can always dig out from inside: 20 game seconds per 50 cm. "Snow against the door. Dig out from inside."
- A cold dugout is not a root cellar. Unheated with its front bermed, the heat model gives about +9 °C in July and −6 °C in a −30 °C January. The root cellar keeps its own 2–4 °C from deeper cover.
- **Winter digging from inside.** A face cell inside a room held above +5 °C for 5 days thaws. It digs at ×1.5 time, one stage per 5 days, within the 6-deep limit, and rolls cave-in as a thaw day until shored.

**Explore, don't dig, the root cellar and permafrost.**

- **Deep ground.** Minecraft engine › Explore, don't dig, rule 4 says dug features never clear deep ground, but a hillside dugout must. Proposed wording for rule 4: "Deep ground may be cleared only inside a validated dugout, root cellar or chimney footprint, level with or above the mouth floor, with no drops."
- **No tunnels.** The interior never goes below the mouth floor. A dugout must be 8 blocks or more from another dugout's interior, at most 3 per region, with its mouth on a generated face. Spoil goes to the berm as `yearofice:spoil`, which drops nothing and is never a mud source.
- **Root cellar.** It uses the same `EarthWork` dig job and numbers. It may open off a dugout's back wall through a plank door, with 1 block of earth between the two interiors.
- **Permafrost.** It is refused because a heated room there settles about 4 cm a year at 20 °C (PermafrostSettlement) and its walls would thaw and slump. The unheated permafrost cellar is that ground's dug piece (Structures and buildables › Food and storage).
- **Silt.** Cut banks on riverside silt are frost-susceptible. A post set on bare silt heaves 3 cm a winter and stops shoring after 3 winters; on a flat stone it does not move.

**Rock shelters.** Rock loses heat to the air at U 0.3 (Crafting and building in depth › Building pieces), not to the ground. A 3 × 3 × 2 shelter with a log front holds about −23 °C on a −35 °C day with only the player inside, and about −10 °C with a fireplace on Low. Build a rock shelter for speed, a dugout for warmth.

- The interior takes no rockfall hits. The front pieces and anyone in the fan do (Land and seasons in play › Rockfall).
- Enlarging by the 1 allowed face-rock block drops angular stone for the hearth. No chimney can pass rock, so a shelter uses a side flue.
- One generated shelter in 3 holds an old fire ring under a sooted ceiling. One per world has "K.D. 1908" scratched by the miners.

**Bears.** From October 20, an unheated dugout or rock shelter unvisited for 30 days, with no barred door, may be taken as a den. Each region rolls 10% a year for its black bears. The player finds it by breathing and smell: "Something is sleeping in the dugout." A barred plank door prevents it.

**Messenger lines.** "Shore it before you dig on." · "Roof too thin. Needs 1 m of earth or a sod roof." · "Permafrost: no dugout here." · "Frozen ground. Dig from inside a warm room." · "The shoring at 2 m is rotten. It no longer holds." · "Snow against the door. Dig out from inside." · "Something is sleeping in the dugout."

**Minecraft build.** The controller is a `yearofice:dugout` block entity in the mouth sill. It stores the kind, footprint, stage list, frames and each slice's shored flag. Everything runs on the server; the client draws `yearofice:soil_dust` and plays `yearofice:build.soil_trickle` and `build.cave_in`. Cost is one roll per dugout per game day, with no ticking. The dugout JSON adds `cave_in_tell_s`, `max_per_region` 3, `min_spacing_blocks` 8 and `warm_dig` {`room_c` 5, `days` 5, `time_mult` 1.5}. The `earth` face values belong in the data map `data/yearofice/data_maps/block/heat.json` (the engine convention), not a separate `heat/faces.json`.

**GameTests (batch `m8`).**

### Dugout hearth and chimney (Added)

**What it is.** The fireplace and chimney that make a dugout livable in winter, and the move of the cooking pot inside, as in chapters 17–19.

**The pieces.**

| Piece | Materials | Time | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Stone hearth | 12 dressed stones, 0.5 bucket clay | 2 game hours | A fireplace (40% of heat stays in the room); holds the pot on a stone shelf or an iron bar |
| Mud-brick chimney through earth | 12 mud bricks per metre, 0.5 bucket clay | 1 game hour per metre | Clears a 1 × 1 column of soil from the hearth to the surface, up to 3 blocks of cover |
| Side flue | 3 m stovepipe or 6 mud bricks and a roof collar | 1 game hour | For cover deeper than 3 blocks or rock overhead: the smoke runs out through the front wall |
| Pot crane | 1 iron bar or a green-wood crane | 30 game minutes | Swings the pot over the fire and back: cooking inside at full speed |

**Rules.**

- A hearth without a chimney or flue fills the room with smoke by the existing smoke rule.
- Wooden shoring within 1 block of the hearth takes flame damage when the fire is on Hot or packed too full. A stone or mud-plastered face beside the hearth stops it.
- A chimney through earth gives no creosote fire risk to a roof, because the roof is earth; it still needs cleaning to keep its draft (existing creosote rule, without the roof-fire result).
- Moving the pot inside makes water boiling and soup work possible in a blizzard. The pot at the hearth counts as a cooking station for all fire recipes.

**What the player sees.** A glow on the earth walls, smoke rising from a hole in the slope above, snow melting in a ring round it. From outside, the chimney's smoke shows the camp from 1 km on a calm day, to wolves as well.

**Ties.** Warm rooms, smoke and creosote (Crafting and building in depth › Warm rooms), mud bricks, dressed stone, cooking.

**Build.** `yearofice:hearth_stone` is a fire block entity sharing the fireplace numbers in `heat.json`. The chimney piece clears its column of `yearofice:dugout_air` like the dugout itself.

**Studio detail (Building and crafting).** Dugout hearth and chimney.

**Where it may stand.** `hearth_stone` needs earth or rock behind it and on both cheeks: a dugout wall or a rock shelter's face. Anywhere else the ghost is red: "Needs earth or rock behind it. Build a fireplace." The earth does the work of the missing masonry. That is why the hearth costs 12 dressed stones and 2 game hours, against a free-standing fireplace's 60 stones and 12 hours (Crafting and building in depth › Building pieces).

**Build sequence in blocks.**

1. **Firebox.** The piece clears 1 block of earth behind the wall face, its own footprint. It sets 12 dressed stones and 0.5 bucket of clay as floor, cheeks and lintel.
2. **Throat.** The block above the firebox becomes `yearofice:hearth_throat`, the smoke shelf.
3. **Chimney.** It rises straight from the throat through the cover, 1 block a metre at 12 mud bricks each. It ends in a cap 1 block above the surface. Under 2 blocks of cover that is 3 m: 36 bricks and 3 game hours.
4. **Side flue.** Use it instead where cover is over 3 blocks or rock is overhead.
5. **Pot crane.** Press E to swing it in or out.

**Numbers.** The hearth uses the fireplace's heat row: 40% stays in the room, and Low is 6 game hours an armload at 0.8 kW. Its C is 20 Wh/°C, half a fireplace's 40, because it holds fewer stones. Feels-like is +15 °C within 2 blocks. Light comes from the fire states: Low 9, Steady 12, Hot 15.

**EmberSpread rows to add** (Structures and buildables › Fire risk).

| Hearth state | Ember radius | Chance per game hour |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Low or Steady | — | 0%, the firebox is enclosed |
| Hot | 1 block | 2% |
| Packed too full | 2 blocks | 6% |

Indoors, dryness is always 1.0. Targets are dry spruce shoring (1.0), bark lining (1.5) and bough bedding (1.5, a new value). Mud lining and dressed stone are 0. A burning cap loses 50 HP a game minute. At 0 HP it drops and its slices become unshored, so a hearth fire can cause a cave-in days later: "The shoring by the hearth burned. That span is unshored."

**Smoke, edge cases.**

- **Cap height.** Ground upslope within 3 blocks and higher than the cap blows smoke back in winds over 6 m/s, as "Chimney too low" does.
- **Snow.** A fire at Low or higher melts a 1-block ring round the cap. A banked or dead fire lets snow bury it. Eight layers over the cap, from a drift or 1 m of snowfall, disconnect the chimney, and the smoke rule runs at once: "Chimney blocked by snow."
- **Side flue.** A wind over 6 m/s blowing into the mouth drives smoke back: +5% smoke a game minute above Low. Run the fire at Low on windy nights.
- **Creosote.** A chimney through earth is a cold flue, so creosote builds ×1.5. Above 70, a chimney fire on Hot cracks 1 m of brick (−400 HP) and puts 20% smoke into the room at once. No roof can catch.

**First time.** The first fire lit before the chimney fills the room with grey haze: "Smoke has nowhere to go. Build the chimney." The first meal cooked at the crane in a storm writes the journal line "The pot is inside now. Soup in a blizzard."

**Minecraft build.** `yearofice:hearth_stone` is a controller block entity running the fire state machine from `com.yearofice.fire`, with `hearth_throat` as its member. The chimney reuses the mud-brick chimney block with a `through_earth=true` state. `yearofice:side_flue` is a pipe block with a `collar` state. `yearofice:pot_crane` has `swung=in|out`, two blockstates and no GeckoLib. The melt ring is set by the existing snow pass, which reads lit chimneys, so it adds no ticking.

1. `yearofice:fire/hearth_snow_chimney`: with 1 m of snow on a dead hearth's cap, relighting reaches 50% room smoke in 5 game minutes. With a fire at Low through the snowfall, the cap stays clear.
2. `yearofice:fire/hearth_needs_backing`: a hearth ghost in a log cabin is red with its reason; in a dugout's back wall it is white.

### Stumbles, trips and falls (Added)

**What it is.** Small injuries from rough ground, like the video's stubbed toe and blisters, and the slide down the slope while fleeing the first bear.

**Chance per 100 m on rough ground** (tussock tundra, scree, deadfall, wet rock, ice):

```latex
p = 0.3\% \cdot m_{run} \cdot m_{wet} \cdot m_{load} \cdot m_{energy} \cdot m_{dark}
```

- m\_run 3 when running, else 1.
- m\_wet 2 on rain-wet rock, ice or in wet boots.
- m\_load 2 above 80% of the pack's weight limit.
- m\_energy 2 at 3 bolts or fewer.
- m\_dark 2 at night without a light.
- A walking pole halves p. Trails and paths set p to 0.

**Outcomes.**

| Roll | Result | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 70% | A stumble: camera dips, 1 s slow | None |
| 20% | "Foot injury": bruised toe or turned ankle, −10% speed for 12 game hours | A bandage, or a night's rest |
| 8% | A fall: on slopes over 25° the player slides 2–6 blocks; 5–20 Condition; the held item drops | Pick it up |
| 2% | A sprain, by the existing rules | Existing |

**Counterplay.** Walk, use trails, carry a pole, keep the pack light, keep a light. When fleeing a bear downhill, the running multiplier stacks with the slope: the player chooses between speed and footing.

**Ties.** Energy, weight limits, injuries (Blueprint › Injuries), light, travel.

**Build.** Rough ground is a block tag `yearofice:rough_ground`; the check runs in the player tick every 100 m of distance.

**Studio detail (Body and survival).**

**Rough ground, by block.** The tag `yearofice:rough_ground` holds tussock, scree, deadfall, wet rock, bog mat and bare ice. Snow 30 cm or deeper over tussock or deadfall evens it out and clears the tag's effect there. Snow over scree hides the rocks and keeps it rough.

**Edge rules.**

- **Ice.** Running on bare ice uses the existing 3% a second slip (Blueprint › Danger in detail), and the stumble roll is skipped there. Walking on ice uses the stumble roll with m\_wet 2. Ice creepers zero both.
- **The pole.** The walking stick (Ideas from viewers › The walking stick) is the pole: it halves p on any rough ground, at any slope.
- **No unfair deaths.** A slide stops at any edge with a drop over 2 blocks, with "You catch yourself at the edge." Only a player who is running gets no such stop; then the height table in Danger in detail applies.
- **One at a time.** At most 1 stumble outcome per 60 real seconds, so a bad night walk reads as a few events, not a stream.
- **Dropped item.** A held item dropped within 2 m of moving water falls in and drifts by the canoe capsize rule. A fall into bog or mud adds 20 grime (Ideas from viewers › Cauterizing and grime).

**Numbers in play.**

| Case | p per 100 m | Per km |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Walking a scree slope by day, dry | 0.3% | about 3% |
| Same with a full pack and 2 bolts | 1.2% | about 11% |
| Running from a bear at dusk on wet rock, no light | 3.6% | about 31% |
| Walking with the stick on a trail | 0 | 0 |

**Presets.** Gentle ×0.5, Standard ×1, Brutal ×1.25 on p. Endless and Hardcore use the same rolls.

**Feel.** A stumble dips the camera 6° and back in 0.4 s, with a scuff sound by surface and a short breath. A turned ankle adds a limp bob to the hands. A fall rolls the camera, drops the held item with a clatter, and slides the view down the slope. The first stumble writes the journal line "Caught a toe on a tussock" and shows the tip once: "Rough ground. Walk, take a trail or carry a stick."

**Sounds.** `yearofice:body.stumble_scuff` (tussock, scree, wood, ice variants), `body.fall_thud`, `body.grunt`.

**Build.** `com.yearofice.body.Stumbles` reads distance from the player tick, checks the block under the feet against the tag every 100 m of horizontal travel, and rolls with `hash(seed, player, "stumble", distanceIndex)`. The pure formula lives in `com.yearofice.sim.body.StumbleModel`. The slide is a server-side velocity push along the slope's down vector over 10 ticks. Cost: one tag lookup per 100 m, under 0.01 ms.

**Test.** JUnit on `StumbleModel`: 10,000 km of dry scree walked by day gives 250–350 outcomes, of which 7–9% are falls; the same distance on a trail gives none.

**Risk.** Players may come to hate rough ground. The guard: trails zero it, the stick halves it, and the 60-second cap keeps it rare.

### Fire mishaps and slips of the hand (Added)

The video's worst moments were small slips. These rules keep them possible but readable: each slip needs a deliberate hold, warns once, and costs hours, not the run.

- **Putting a fire out by accident.** Using a shovel or water pot on a burning fire smothers it. The first time each game day, it needs a 1 s hold and shows "Hold to put out the fire." Later the same day it is instant, because by then the player means it.
- **Drinking from the river.** With an empty hand, holding use on open water drinks unboiled water: 1 s hold, with the prompt "Unboiled: 30% chance of gut illness." Gear in the water within reach (a net, a trap, a tip-up) takes the use key first, so fixing a gill net never drinks.
- **Mislaying a key tool.** The messenger remembers where the knife, fire steel, axe, pick and rod were last set down: "Fire steel: set down at camp, 2 h ago." A set-down tool glints within 6 m. It costs no battery, because it is a note, not a GPS fix.
- **Leaving work on the fire.** Existing: staves and food burn. Add a single chime when anything on a fire reaches Done, heard within 30 m.

**Ties.** Fire states, water, controls (Blueprint › Controls and interaction), the messenger.

**Studio detail (Body and survival).**

**Hold guards, exactly.**

| Slip | Guard | Resets | Never guarded |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Smothering a burning fire | 1 s hold, prompt, first time each game day | 00:00 | A fire already at Coals or Banked |

### Hooking a bear (Added)

**What it is.** In chapter 8 the creator's cast hooked a bear fishing the same pool. In Year of Ice it is a real, rare danger of fishing below falls in a salmon run.

**Trigger.** A bear fishing a pool (Waters and fish › Salmon runs in detail) stands in the water. A cast whose hook lands within 2 m of it, or a line it walks through, hooks it 25% of the time.

**What happens.**

1. Line tension jumps to 100% in 1 s. The bear turns toward the line's source.
2. The player has 3 s to drop the rod (the drop key) or cut the line (hold use 0.5 s with a knife).
3. Holding on past 3 s pulls the rod into the water, where it drifts. The bear then charges 50% of the time.
4. Letting go at once: the bear charges 15% of the time, else it huffs, pops its jaws and goes back to fishing.

**Gut bait draws bears.** Fish guts on the hook, or a cleaning spot within 30 m, give a scent of a tenth of a carcass. From August 15 to October 15, a bear visits a gut-baited fishing spot 3% per game hour within its home range.

**What the player sees and hears.** A dark shape in the white water, a splash, the reel screaming. The messenger never warns.

**Counterplay.** Look before casting at a falls; fish with minnows or flies when bears are on the river; clean fish away from the water you fish.

**Ties.** Fishing methods, bears and salmon, scent (Blueprint › Senses by species), items in the river.

**Build.** On cast land, `com.yearofice.hunt.fishing.Cast` queries bear entities within 2 m; a hooked bear sets a `hooked` flag that the bear's goal selector reads.

**Studio detail (Body and survival).**

**When it can happen.** Only during a salmon run, from the first chum at the falls to 7 days after the last (Waters and fish › Salmon runs in detail). Black bears and grizzlies both fish. Night casting works the same; the bear is harder to see, so the 2 m check is the whole warning.

**Outcomes by choice.**

| Player action in the 3 s | Bear charges | Rod |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Drop the rod at once (under 1 s) | 15% | Lies on the bank or in the shallows; the bear may bite it (20%: tip broken) |
| Cut the line (knife, 0.5 s hold) | 15% | Kept; 10 m of line and the hook lost |
| Hold on past 3 s | 50% | Pulled in; drifts to the next snag by the capsize rule |
| Reel in | 80% | Pulled in after 1 s; the bear is hooked in the lip and stays Defensive for 3 game days |

A charge is a Defensive charge by the existing rules, with its warning. Spray works. On Gentle, predators are Shy: the bear huffs and leaves, and never charges.

**Gut bait, exact.** A hook baited with fish or game guts, or a cleaning spot within 30 m, is a scent point of strength 0.1 while it lasts. Bears path to it by the scent rules. The 3% an hour visit applies only when the spot is inside a bear's home range and between August 15 and October 15.

**The hooked bear.** A hooked bear keeps the flag `hooked` for 3 game days. It avoids that pool for 10 game days afterwards, so the player has lost the best spot, not only the rod. A bear hooked twice in a year gets the "remembers" flag that Legends read (Ideas from viewers › What viewers loved).

**Feel, step by step.**

1. The bobber lands near a dark shape. A heavy splash; the reel screams at a pitch higher than any fish.
2. The tension bar slams red. The bear's head turns. The close clock starts.
3. The prompt shows only the two keys: "Drop rod" and "Cut line."
4. After the choice, the bear either huffs and pops its jaws, or charges with the existing warning.

**Sounds.** `yearofice:fishing.reel_scream`, `animal.bear_huff`, `animal.bear_jaw_pop`, `fishing.line_snap`.

**Build.** On bobber land, `com.yearofice.hunt.fishing.Cast` queries bear entities within 2 m and rolls `hash(seed, cast, "hook_bear")`. The bear's Brain reads memory `yearofice:hooked_by` (player UUID, game minute) and a pool-avoidance memory. Cost: one entity query per cast.

**Test.** `yearofice:fishing/hook_bear_rates` (batch `m6`): over 1,000 casts landing within 2 m of a fishing bear, 22–28% hook it; with an instant drop, 12–18% of hooks lead to a charge; on Gentle, none do.

**Risk.** Players could farm bear charges for drama, or never fish the falls. The guard: the bear is visible, the outcome is rare, and the cost is the pool, not the run.

### Exposure time (Deepened)

**Missing so far.** Warmth falls by the warmth formula, but the player is not told how long they can stay out.

**The line.** When feels-like is below −20 °C, the messenger shows "Outside like this: about 40 min." It solves the warmth formula for the time until warmth reaches the frostbite threshold at the current clothing, wind and activity. A bare head below −25 °C feels-like halves the time, which is why the video's hat had to go on.

**Ties.** Warmth and clothing (Blueprint › Warmth; Blueprint › Clothing), frostbite.

**Studio detail (Body and survival).**

**What is solved.** The line shows the shorter of two times, at the current clothing, wind, wetness and activity:

```latex
t_{out} = \min\left( \frac{W - 30}{0.02\,(10 - T_{feel})\, m_{cold}},\ \min_{zone} \frac{300}{T_{limit,zone} - T_{feel,zone}} \right)
```

The first term is time to Slowed (warmth 30). The second is time to frostnip of the weakest zone. m\_cold is the preset's cold slider. A bare head below −25 °C feels-like doubles the warmth term's loss rate, so the time halves.

**Display.**

| Result | Line |
| --- | --- |
| Under 10 min | "Outside like this: under 10 min. Get to a fire." in red |
| 10–60 min | Rounded to 5 min |
| 1–6 h | Rounded to 15 min |
| Over 6 h | "Outside like this: hours." |
| Moving vs still | A second line when walking: "If you stop: about 25 min" |

**When it shows.** Outdoors, with air feels-like below −20 °C, and not within 8 m of a fire or in a room. It recomputes each game minute and only changes the shown number when it moves by a step, so it does not flicker. It costs no battery beyond the strip. With the battery at 0, the body cues remain.

**Edge cases.**

- Wet layers: the w term enters both terms, so falling in a creek collapses the time at once.
- Zones already past frostnip show "Hands: frostbite now" instead of a time.
- Sleeping outdoors uses the asleep feels-like (2 °C lower) when the player opens the sleep radial: "Sleeping here: about 3 h before cold wakes you."

**Feel.** The first time the line shows, the journal writes "−34 °C. The messenger says forty minutes." The number shrinks as wind rises, which teaches the wind term without a tutorial.

**Ties.** Warmth and Clothing (Blueprint › Warmth; Blueprint › Clothing); frostbite timers (Blueprint › Health, medicine and nutrition); the pocket stone (Ideas from viewers › The pocket stone), which lengthens the hand term.

**Build.** `com.yearofice.sim.body.ExposureSolver` (pure) runs in `BodyTicker` after the warmth step; the result goes in `yearofice:body_sync` as `exposure_min`. The client layer `yearofice:vitals_strip` draws it.

**Test.** `yearofice:body/exposure_honest` (JUnit sim): for 500 random clothing, wind and wet states below −20 °C, the predicted time is within ±10% of the time the body model actually takes to reach warmth 30 or frostnip.

### Rest days (Deepened)

**Missing so far.** The creator spent four days by the fire to heal frostbite and regain weight. Rest has no rules of its own.

**Rule.** A game day in which the player stays within 10 m of a warm fire or in a warm room, works under 1 game hour and sleeps 8 hours is a Rest day. On a Rest day: calorie burn is ×1.1 of BMR instead of the activity total; wounds, frostbite and sprains heal at 2× the usual rate; Spirits +2 the first day, then −1 a day after three in a row (cabin fever). Scurvy and parasites do not heal from rest.

**On screen.** The morning line says "Rest day: healing faster." The messenger offers "Rest today" when a severe condition is present.

**Studio detail (Body and survival).**

**Declaring a rest.** The sleep radial (Z) gains "Rest today." The messenger offers it once each morning when a severe or healing problem is present. A day also counts with no declaration if it meets the rule at 00:00.

**What breaks a rest.** More than 1 game hour of work in total, more than 30 game minutes in total outside the 10 m fire or room radius, or under 8 hours of sleep by the next 08:00. The line then reads "Rest broken." Light seated jobs (cordage, carving, sewing, tending a pot) count as work at half time, so 2 hours of sewing still fits.

**Healing, exact.**

| Problem | Rate on a Rest day |
| --- | --- |
| Cut, burn, frostnip and superficial frostbite, sprain | ×2 (a sprain day does not also count double for walking under 1 km; the larger applies) |
| Broken bone | ×1.5 |
| Condition back per game hour | ×2, after the underweight halving |
| Infection, gut illness, parasites, scurvy | ×1; their own treatments only |

**Spirits.** Day 1 of a run of Rest days +2; days 2 and 3 +0; day 4 on −1 a day ("cabin fever"). A day with work breaks the run. A Bonded wolf at the fire, a lamp evening or chaga still count, so a good winter cabin offsets the fever.

**Food.** See the conflict note below: a Rest day burns the ordinary resting rates plus a healing cost.

**Endless and Hardcore.** The same. In Hardcore a rest is often the only way to clear a stage 2 problem before it turns severe.

**Feel.** The morning body line names it: "Rest day: healing faster." The fire roster shows the wood needed to stay in the radius all day. On day 4 the line becomes "Fourth day indoors. Restless."

**Conflict and resolution.** The entry sets burn at ×1.1 of BMR (about 1,970 kcal at 77 kg). The ordinary rates already give about 1,680 kcal for 8 h asleep and 16 h resting, so ×1.1 BMR makes resting cost more than resting. Proposed: burn the ordinary rates, plus a healing cost of 10% of BMR (about 180 kcal, so about 1,860 kcal) only while a problem is healing. Rest still costs food, which is the video's lesson.

**Build.** `com.yearofice.body.RestDay` keeps `rest_run`, `rest_work_min` and `rest_away_min` in `yearofice:body`; it reads the heat index for the 10 m test. GameTest `yearofice:body/rest_heals_double` (batch `m4`): a mild sprain heals in 1 game day on a Rest day and in 2 without; scurvy stage does not change.

**Risk.** Rest could become the answer to everything. The guards are the cabin fever, the fuel it burns and the days it takes from the calendar.

**Missing so far.** A weak player gets one 10 s sprint when chased (Blueprint › Video checklist). The video also showed the crash after it.

**Studio detail (Body and survival).**

**Ties.** Energy and Condition bands (Blueprint › Energy; Blueprint › Health, medicine and nutrition); bear and moose charges (Blueprint › Danger in detail); stumbles above, since the burst counts as running (m\_run 3).

**Why it rarely pays.** Digging one worm takes about 1 game minute and costs about 8 kcal of work. After rain, worm yield doubles for a day (existing), so a worm then costs about 4 kcal and nets 1. A handful of worms is a few calories and lost bait.

### Stomach fill (Added)

**What it is.** A stomach meter shown as percent full, as the video's "36% full". It limits how much can be eaten at once, so a big kill feeds the player over several meals.

**Rules.**

- 100% is 1.8 kg of food by mass. Each item's mass is its weight, so dense foods (fat, pemmican, dried meat) carry more kcal per percent.
- It empties 7% a game hour awake and 4% asleep: a full stomach is empty in about 14 game hours awake.
- Above 100% the player cannot eat: "Too full."
- Below 15%: "Stomach empty," with the existing growl.
- Auto-eat from the food cache (Blueprint › Video checklist) fills the stomach in its two meal windows, morning 06:00–09:00 and evening 18:00–21:00 (Blueprint › Spoilage model, Auto-eat), only up to the ration's share, so Feast is limited by stomach space as well as stores.

**Why it matters.** It makes fat valuable: 100 g of fat gives 900 kcal for 5.5% of the stomach, while 1 kg of lean hare gives about 1,100 kcal for 55%. It also explains why the creator could not eat a moose in one sitting.

**On screen.** A small stomach icon beside the hunger line with the percent; it pulses when eating is blocked.

**Ties.** Calories (Blueprint › Calories and weight), macros, auto-eat, eating fat below.

**Build.** A float `stomach_kg` in the `yearofice:body` attachment, stepped in the body tick.

**Studio detail (Body and survival).**

**What counts as mass.** Solid food counts its weight. Stew and broth count 50% of their mass, since most of it is water. Water and tea count nothing. Pemmican, lard and dried meat count their weight, so they carry the most kcal per percent.

**Fill per meal, at a glance.**

| Food | Serving | Fill | kcal | kcal per 1% |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lard | 0.1 kg | 5.6% | 900 | 162 |
| Pemmican | 0.2 kg | 11% | 1,000 | 90 |
| Moose meat | 0.5 kg | 28% | 650 | 23 |
| Hare, lean | 1 kg | 56% | 1,000 | 18 |
| Berries | 1 cup (0.15 kg) | 8.3% | 85 | 10 |
| Fish-head soup | 1 L | 28% | 300 | 11 |

**A day's ceiling.** With 1.8 kg full at waking, 7% an hour awake for 16 hours and 4% an hour asleep for 8, a player can eat about 4.4 kg a day. That caps a berry-only day at about 2,500 kcal, below a working day's burn, and a moose-meat day at about 5,100. Fat and pemmican are the only way to Feast on a small stomach.

**Edge cases.**

- Vomiting (Ideas from viewers › Sick stomach) empties the stomach to 0.
- Gut illness halves absorption, not fill: a sick player fills up and still starves a little.
- A refeeding player (BMI under 18.5) can still fill to 100%; the nausea rule watches kcal, not mass.
- Asleep, eating is impossible, so auto-eat at 18:00 is the last cache meal of the day.

**Presets.** Gentle 2.2 kg, Standard 1.8 kg, Brutal 1.6 kg. A realism slider can turn the limit off.

**Feel.** The icon is a small sack shape that fills as a bar. At 90% the eat prompt reads "Nearly full." At 100% the bite animation stops and the player shakes their head; the icon pulses. A moose kill becomes a camp of meals over two days, which is the video's pacing.

**Ties.** Auto-eat and rations (Blueprint › Preserving in detail); macros (Blueprint › Macros and strength); eating fat below; Sick stomach.

**Build.** `com.yearofice.food.Stomach` steps `stomach_kg` in the body tick; `data/yearofice/food/stomach.json` holds `capacity_kg`, `empty_pct_per_h_awake`, `empty_pct_per_h_asleep` and the liquid share. The eat action checks fill on the server before the absorb step. Cost: one float per player.

**Risk.** Players may read it as a nag. The guard: the icon appears only above 70% or below 15%, the same "empty when all is well" rule as the strip.

### Berry patches (Added and Deepened)

**What it is.** The creator's design statement: berries are eaten where they grow, in season, and cannot be moved to camp. The blueprint has the berry calendar and berry swings, but no rule against moving bushes and no picking cycle.

**Rules.**

1. **No transplanting.** Berry bushes have no item form. Breaking one drops sticks only and the bush is gone for 3 years. Berries cannot be planted: the vanilla sweet berry bush is off.
2. **Ripeness you can see.** Each bush steps through green, pink and ripe. Green berries can be eaten for 0 kcal and a 30% chance of a stomach ache; the video's "tease berry".
3. **Picking.** A ripe bush gives 0.15–0.25 kg per pick, at 30 game minutes a cup. It then ripens again in 5–8 game days during its season (blueberry, cloudberry). Lingonberries and highbush cranberries ripen once and stay on the bush.
4. **Patches.** A patch is 20–60 bushes on a burn edge, a south slope or a bog margin, sized by the berry swing year (Land and seasons in play › Berry swings).
5. **Under snow.** Lingonberries keep under snow (existing). Clearing snow off a bush takes 1 game minute. Bushes that were picked or eaten by bears and grouse in fall have nothing left, which is what the video found.

**What the player sees.** Colour on the bush from 10 m; a picked bush shows bare stems for its regrowth time. The plant guide names the patch's season: "Blueberries: picked out. Ripe again in about 6 days."

**Ties.** Foraging calendar, bears sharing patches, grouse, carbs and vitamin C, Spirits.

**Build.** One block per species, `yearofice:<species>_bush`, all in the tag `yearofice:berry_bushes` and sharing the class `com.yearofice.forage.BerryBushBlock` (RECONCILE 15, 64, 130). Block states are `phase`, `taken` and `ripe` 0–2; the 0–8 regrow count lives in the chunk's `patches` ledger, not a block state, because it changes daily in unloaded chunks (Minecraft development › World and worldgen). No `BlockItem` is registered for any of them. Each species' numbers are in `data/yearofice/plant/<species>_bush.json`.

**Studio detail (Body and survival).**

**Pick rate stays the main tab's.** One bush block holds 0.15–0.25 kg (1–1.7 cups) per flush. Picking a bush clean is a hold-to-work time-lapse at the plant's pick time per cup: 30–50 game minutes for blueberries. The entry's "2 game minutes" is replaced: rule 3 now reads 30 game minutes a cup (RECONCILE 130).

**Flushes.** Repeat ripening (blueberry, bog blueberry, cloudberry) runs at 1.0, 0.6 and 0.36 of the first flush's yield, then stops for the season. A 40-bush blueberry patch gives about 60 cups in its first flush and about 120 in a season.

| --- | --- | --- |

**Night and weather.** Without a light after dusk, green and ripe look the same and every pick takes 50% longer. **Who else eats.** A bear feeding in a patch takes 2 cups a game minute; grouse take 5% a day after the peak; a patch picked by both shows torn branches and purple scat. **Ties.** Foraging and plant knowledge (Blueprint); bears' hyperphagia (Bestiary); carbs, vitamin C and stomach fill.

### Dressed stone (Added)

**What it is.** Squared stones for a hearth, a chimney base and foundations: the video's stone bricks for the cave fireplace.

**Making it.** A loose stone from a gravel bar or scree, or a block of face rock taken with a pick, is shaped with a hammer stone (any fist stone) or an iron chisel.

| Tool | Time per dressed stone | Practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hammer stone | 10 game minutes | −40% at full practice |
| Iron chisel and mallet | 4 game minutes | −40% |

Each weighs 15 kg, so 12 for a hearth is three trips from a bar 200 m away, or one with a sled on snow.

**What it does.** Dressed stone does not burn, holds 10 Wh/°C per m² like a rock face, and counts as a stone wall for fire risk. A hearth of dressed stone keeps radiating for 2–3 game hours after the fire dies.

**Ties.** Warm rooms (heat capacity), fire risk, the dugout hearth, weight and sleds.

**Build.** `yearofice:dressed_stone` is a building block and item; shaping is a hold-to-work job at any flat stone.

**Studio detail (Building and crafting).** Dressed stone.

**Sources.**

| Raw stone | Where | Time to dress | Loss |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Angular stone | Face rock taken with a pick, rockfall stones, scree | As in the table above | None |
| Rounded river stone | Gravel bars | ×2 | 25% split to rubble |

A rough stone of 20 kg dresses to 15 kg, so dressing at the source saves a quarter of the haul. A pack frame (45 kg) takes 3 dressed stones, and a fourth rides in the hands by the corner-stone rule. That gives the entry's 3 trips for a hearth.

**Tools and practice.**

- **Hammerstone.** It cracks after 40 dressings. Any fist stone makes a new one in 1 game minute.
- **Stone chisel.** New: `yearofice:stone_chisel`, 0.3 kg bar iron, Furnace, 1 game hour, 450 kcal. It wears 1 edge point per stone and is restored on the whetstone. It is driven with the wooden mallet (Items).
- **Stonework.** A new skill, N 30, 1 rep per dressed stone, giving the −40%. It also shortens quarrying millstones (Structures and buildables › Water power) and building fireplaces.
- **Limits.** Below −25 °C, 10% of dressings split the stone. Dressing needs light 7 at the stone.

**Uses.**

| Piece | Dressed stones | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dugout hearth | 12 | See Dugout hearth and chimney |
| Chimney base under a cabin's mud-brick chimney | 4 per block | Keeps the chimney off soil: no frost heave and no rot under it |
| Dressed-stone wall block `yearofice:dressed_stone_wall` | 8 and 0.25 bucket of clay | U 1.5, C 10, HP 1,500, flammability 0; the wall class in SupportGraph (7 per block up) |
| Retaining wall, 1 m | 4 dressed or 8 rough | Holds a cut slope |

**Why there is no stone house.** A wall block costs 80 game minutes of shaping and 120 kg of hauling. Its U of 1.5 against a log wall's 0.5 means its walls lose three times the heat of logs. Stone is for fire and footings, as in the video.

**Feel.** Place a rough stone on the ground and hold use with the tool. A progress ring, stone-chip particles and a tapping sound follow: dull for the hammerstone, ringing for the chisel. The block squares off by stages before the player's eyes.

**Minecraft build.** `yearofice:rough_stone` is a placeable block item and `yearofice:dressed_stone` its result, stacking to 4 by weight. `com.yearofice.build.pieces.Stonework` runs the job. Sound `yearofice:build.stone_tap`, particle `yearofice:stone_chip`. Heat values go in the data map: C 10; U 1.5 for the wall block.

**GameTest (batch `m8`).** `yearofice:build/dressing_time`: 12 angular stones with a hammerstone take 120 ±2 game minutes at no practice and 72 ±2 at full Stonework.

**Band height.** A wolverine reaches 1.2 m from the surface it stands on. The ghost sets bands from 2.0 to 2.6 m, which holds to 80 cm of snow. A deeper drift brings the band in reach, and the evening prompt says "Snow is up to the cache bands."

**Grizzly reach.** A grizzly reaches 3 m standing (Blueprint › Danger in detail). The test is the deck's underside at least 3 m above the surface the bear stands on. The table's 3.5 m is the deck top: 3 leg blocks plus a slab floor. A spring grizzly out after April 20, standing on 60 cm of old crust, reaches a standard 3 m cache. Late April is the cache's worst week.

**Peeling pays twice.** A drawknife peels a leg in 10 game minutes, a knife in 25. Peeling also cuts rot from 10% to 2% a year (Structures and buildables › Rot), ×3 where the leg stands in soil and ×0.5 more with a charred end. A live-tree leg cannot be peeled safely. A peeled live tree dies within a year, rots as dead wood and brings "Dead tree overhead" warnings.

**Rotten legs.** A leg at Soft or Rotten lets a grizzly push the cache: 20% a visit to topple it, whatever its height. The contents spill as a ground pile. A rotten leg makes a tall cache a short one.

**Ladder.** Press E on it to take it down (2 s). Left up overnight, martens and wolverines use it every time.

**Feel.** A peeled leg shows pale, slick wood. A band is a bright tin ring that catches low sun. A failed climb leaves claw scratches below the band for 3 days, and the player reads them.

**Minecraft build.** `yearofice:cache_leg` gets a third state, `band_y` 0–3, for the band's height step. `com.yearofice.ecosystem.goal.ClimbCacheGoal` reads `peeled`, `banded`, `band_y` and the snow layers at the leg once per visit. Chances live in the `climb` block of each climbing species' `data/yearofice/species/<id>.json`. Server only. Scratches are sign decals.

### Squirrels (Deepened)

**Missing so far.** The red squirrel climbs, scolds and takes blunt arrows. The video's squirrel scenes need its escape rules.

**Escape.**

1. Seen within 15 m, a squirrel runs to the nearest trunk and climbs to 6–12 m.
2. Pressed, it leaps to a tree up to 3 m away; with branches touching, it can cross a stand of 10 trees in a minute.
3. A felled tree with a squirrel in it: 70% it leaps to a neighbour before the fall; 30% it rides down and is stunned for 3 s, when a rock or a hand takes it.
4. It returns to its midden within 30 game minutes, so waiting beats chasing.

**Seeing them.** Still against spruce bark, a squirrel is seen at 60% of the normal sight distance; moving, at 100%. In bare birch after leaf fall, at 130%.

**The cost of the chase.** Felling a tree for a squirrel costs 20–40 game minutes of chopping and about 150 kcal, against the squirrel's 250 kcal. The messenger's journal notes it once: "Felled a tree for a squirrel: about even."

**Ties.** Throwing rocks and blunt arrows (Blueprint › Weapons and arrowheads), leaf fall, middens (Blueprint › Populations).

**Studio detail (Ecosystem and hunting).** Squirrels.

**Daily and seasonal rhythm.**

| Period | Where the squirrel is | Chase notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dawn to dusk, May–Aug 14 | Feeding in the canopy, on the midden 30% of the time | Normal escape rules |
| Aug 15 – Oct 15 (caching) | On the ground cutting and carrying cones 60% of the time | Easiest season: it is caught 8–15 m from a trunk |
| Winter, above −30 °C | Out 10:00–15:00 only | Tracks lead from midden tunnel to tunnel |
| Below −30 °C | In the midden tunnels (existing rule) | Cannot be hunted; a pole snare still rolls |
| Night | In a nest ball of grass high in a spruce | Not found except by sign |

**The trunk dodge.** On a trunk, a squirrel moves to the side away from the player every 2 s, keeping the trunk between them. A stick or rock thrown past the far side makes it shift back into view 50% of the time, as real hunters do it alone. Two-sided shots are impossible without that trick.

**Arrows in the canopy.** A blunt arrow that misses upward and passes through leaf blocks lodges in the branches 30% of the time. It falls when wind tops 6 m/s (10% a game hour), when the tree is felled, or when the player shakes a trunk under 15 cm (E hold, 3 s). A killed squirrel snags on the way down 20% of the time by the same rule.

**Pressure.** Red squirrel `respawn_days` is 10. A midden whose owner is taken stays empty that long, then a juvenile moves in, so the 2–4 middens within a short walk of camp make a slow, steady supply, not a farm. Every chase scolds, and each scold alerts game within 50 m (Senses by species), so a chase beside a moose bedding area spoils the stalk.

**Presets.** Return to the midden: Gentle 15, Standard 30, Brutal 45 game minutes.

**First time.** The first squirrel the player chases dodges the trunk twice, then a journal line appears: "It keeps the tree between us. Throw something past it."

**Minecraft build.** `yearofice:red_squirrel` (`com.yearofice.ecosystem.species.RedSquirrelEntity`, M5) uses a custom `TrunkClimbNavigation` on blocks tagged `yearofice:climbable_trunks` at 2 m/s and leaps along leaf blocks touching within 1 block, up to 3 blocks across and 2 down. The midden is a generated prop block `yearofice:cone_midden` holding the owner id. The squirrel rides a `yearofice:falling_tree` entity as a passenger for the 30% case and gets `stunned_until` (60 ticks). Data: an `escape` block in `data/yearofice/species/red_squirrel.json` (`sight_mult_still_bark` 0.6, `sight_mult_bare_birch` 1.3, `return_min` 30). Cost: climb pathing searches only the 3 nearest trunks; at most 6 Active squirrels near a player. GameTest `yearofice:ecosystem/squirrel_returns_to_midden` (batch `m5`): back within 30 game minutes in at least 95 of 100 trials with the player 40 m away and still.

**Risk.** A chase that never ends. Guard: after 3 escapes in one game hour the squirrel stays in its nest for 2 game hours and the scold stops.

### Bears in water (Deepened)

**Rule.** Grizzlies swim at 1.5 m/s and black bears at 1.4 m/s in still water (RECONCILE 33), both faster than the player's 1.0, and cross rivers to follow, as in chapter 14. They do not enter current above 2.5 m/s. Escaping a bear by water works only across fast current or onto thin ice that holds a player and not a bear.

**Studio detail (Ecosystem and hunting).** Bears in water.

**One swim table for big animals.** The species files carry `swim_speed_m_s`; this reconciles the 1.4 here with the 1.5 in Ideas from viewers › Bears as viewers asked.

| Animal | Swim speed | Enters current up to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Moose | 1.8 m/s | 2.5 m/s |
| Caribou | 1.6 m/s | 2.0 m/s |
| Grizzly | 1.5 m/s | 2.5 m/s |
| Black bear | 1.4 m/s | 2.0 m/s |
| Companion wolf | 1.2 m/s (Swimming and cold water) | 1.5 m/s |
| Player, crawl | 1.0 m/s | Pushed by any current |

**Edge rules.**

1. **No bites while swimming.** A bear attacks only once it stands in water under 1 m. A player in deep water beside a swimming bear is not bitten, but the bear follows to whichever bank they reach.
2. **The bank wait.** A bear that loses a swimmer waits on the bank for 30 game minutes, then leaves 70% of the time. Meanwhile the cold-water rules (Swimming and cold water) run on the player, so water buys time, never safety.
3. **The shake.** A bear climbing out stops 3 s to shake: the one window to ready spray or put a tree between.
4. **Thin ice.** Ice breaks under a grizzly below 15 cm and a black bear below 12 cm; it holds a player at 10 cm (Danger in detail). The safe band is 10–15 cm against a grizzly and 10–12 cm against a black bear. A bear that breaks through climbs out on the far side in 30–60 s or gives up the chase 80% of the time.
5. **Canoes.** A bear never chases a canoe in open water. One met at a bank within 3 m hooks the gunwale and capsizes it 50% of the time, so landing beside a feeding bear is the mistake.
6. **Seasons.** Bears are denned from November to April, so water escapes are a May–October question. At the falls in the run they stand in current under 1.5 m/s at the tail-outs.

**On screen and sound.** Only the head and hump show; a V-shaped wake; heavy breathing (`yearofice:animal.bear_swim`) audible at 40 m; spray from the shake.

**Minecraft build.** The bear's path navigation reads the current attachment (Minecraft engine › Rivers, current and water) and marks water nodes above its current limit as blocked. Swim animation `swim` in the GeckoLib file. Ice thickness comes from the ice block state; the break check is `weight_kg` against `ice_holds_kg` in `data/yearofice/ice/thickness.json`. GameTest `yearofice:ecosystem/bear_refuses_fast_current` (batch `m6`): a Chasing grizzly stops at a 3 m/s channel in 50 of 50 runs, and crosses a 1 m/s one in 50 of 50.

### Bears and tree stands (Added)

**What it is.** In chapter 18 a bear brought down the tree the creator was climbing. Tree stands get a rule for it.

**Rules.**

| Stand tree trunk | Grizzly | Black bear |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Under 20 cm | Shakes and pushes; 3 shoves topple it | Climbs |
| 20–30 cm | Shakes: the player must hold on (hold use) or fall | Climbs |
| Over 30 cm | Safe | Climbs |

- A fall from 3–5 m costs 10–30 Condition and may sprain (existing rules).

**Counterplay.** Build stands in thick trees; climb before the bait draws anything; against black bears, height does not help, so keep the bow ready.

**Ties.** Tree stands (Blueprint › Video checklist), bear species (Blueprint › Wildlife and fish), falls.

**Studio detail (Ecosystem and hunting).** Bears and tree stands.

**Where the diameter comes from.** Minecraft trunks are 1 block at every size, so the tree record stores `dbh_cm`, set by the tree builder from species and age. The stand card reads it ("White spruce, 34 cm, 5 m: safe from grizzlies").

| Tree | Usual trunk | Stand use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Black spruce | 8–15 cm | Never safe from a grizzly |
| Paper birch | 15–30 cm | Usually the hold-on band |
| White spruce | 20–50 cm | The stand tree; pick the big ones |
| Balsam poplar | 30–80 cm | Always safe; on river flats |

**The shove.** A grizzly at the trunk shoves every 3 s. The trunk sways and creaks 0.5 s ahead (`yearofice:tree.shove_creak`), and the player must hold RMB through the next 2 s. With Strength under 40 the grip fails 20% of the time even when held. A toppled tree uses the falling-tree rules: the player rides it down (a 4 m fall with branches halving the loss, 5–15 Condition) and lands at the bear's feet.

**Height.** A grizzly reaches 3 m standing (Danger in detail). A stand at 3 m or lower takes swipes: −8 Condition each, 30% deep cut. The card warns below 4 m.

**How long it stays.** With nothing below, a grizzly gives up after 60–120 real seconds. With bait or a carcass below, it feeds and stays 1–4 game hours: the player is treed, as hunters over bait are. A bear on a carcass is Defensive, so climbing down early is the wrong response.

**Black bears.** They climb at 0.5 m/s, so a player on a 5 m stand has about 10 s. Contact on a stand follows the black-bear rule: fight. A hatchet hit drives it down 50% of the time; a fall from the stand is likely either way. The journal notes it once: "It came up the tree after me."

**Seasons.** Bait sets draw bears mostly from August to October (fall feeding) and in May (lean spring bears). The ladder rule holds year-round.

**Minecraft build.** `yearofice:tree_stand` attaches to a log column and stores the tree id. The bear Brain gets a `ShakeTreeGoal` when its target is 2+ blocks above ground within 1.5 blocks of a trunk. Shoves are server events; the client gets camera sway. A topple converts the tree into `yearofice:falling_tree` pointed away from the bear. Data: the `climb` block of the bear's `data/yearofice/species/<id>.json` (`shove_interval_s` 3, `topple_shoves` 3, `grip_fail_under_strength` 40). GameTest `yearofice:ecosystem/stand_topple` (batch `m6`): a grizzly topples a 15 cm stand tree in exactly 3 unheld shoves and never moves a 35 cm one.

**Risk.** Stands as a safe room. Guard: black bears climb, and bait keeps grizzlies below.

### Wounds that last (Added)

**What it is.** A big animal hit but not killed keeps its wound. The creator thought the bear he finally killed might be the one he wounded weeks before.

**Rules.**

- A non-fatal hit stores the hit zone and date on the animal.
- Muscle wounds heal in 10–20 game days; while healing the animal has −25% health, −15% speed and leaves a limp in its tracks.
- A wounded bear's aggression rises one step: it charges at close range more often and comes to camp food and bait sooner.
- An arrow left in an animal is recovered when it is killed: "Your arrow, from August 28."
- A wounded animal met again is named on first look: "This bear has an old arrow wound." Three meetings make it Known (Water, calls and encounters › Known animals).

**Ties.** The hit-zone model, tracks and sign, Known animals, the encounter journal.

**Build.** A `wound` record (zone, day, arrowItem) in the animal entity's saved data and the region file, so it survives despawn and respawn.

**Studio detail (Ecosystem and hunting).** Wounds that last.

**Which hits wound.** Only hits the hit-zone model lets the animal survive: a leg hit, a neck hit that misses the spine, an arrow stopped by the shoulder blade (the front 30% of the heart zone) when draw weight is short of the species' need, and a light-bow body hit on a moose or bear. Gut and liver hits kill, as written. Wild sources add the rest: 10% of adult moose and caribou start the year with an old wolf bite on the hindquarters, and the loser of a falls fight leaves with a shoulder wound.

**Healing by zone.**

| Zone | Heals in (Standard) | Limp shows | Arrow shaft |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Leg | 20 game days | All 20 days | Breaks off in 3 days; the head stays |
| Shoulder | 15 | First 7 days | Same |
| Neck muscle | 10 | None | Same |
| Old bite | Healed; scar only | None | — |

Gentle ×0.7, Brutal ×1.3. After 3 days the player recovers only the arrowhead: "Your arrowhead, from August 28."

**Festering.** 10% of arrow wounds go bad. The animal dies 5–15 game days later inside its home range, and its body is placed through Bestiary › Carcasses and bodies with cause `wound` and the arrowhead inside. Ravens lead to it (Bestiary › Animals that help the player). Journal: "The bull from August 28. The wound went bad."

**What a wound changes.**

- Wolves pick wounded moose and caribou ×2 in the predation step (Who hunts whom).
- The animal's wariness of the player rises 20 for good, and a moose avoids the spot where it was hit for 30 game days.
- A wounded bear's scent-pathing range rises from 400 to 600 m while it heals.
- At most 3 wounds are kept per animal; healed ones leave a visible scar mark, which seeds the Known animal's mark (Water, calls and encounters › Known animals).

**Tracks.** Sign records get `gait: "limp"`; the decal drags one print. A learned reading: "Moose, cow, limping on the left hind, 1–6 hours."

**Minecraft build.** Big-game individuals already keep persistent ids (Populations); the `wound` list (zone, day, cause, arrowhead stack with a `yearofice:shot_day` component) lives in the individual record in `yearofice_region_<cell>` and in the entity's saved data while Active. The client draws the shaft at the GeckoLib locator `wound_<zone>` for 3 days, then a scab texture. Healing runs on `ClockEvent.Day` in the Abstract tier. Small game keeps no wounds. GameTest `yearofice:ecosystem/wound_survives_demotion` (batch `m6`): wound a moose, walk 1 km away and back, and the same id returns with the wound and speed ×0.85.

**Risk.** Guilt that nags. Guard: one journal line per wounded animal, no Spirits penalty, no lecture.

### Snare funnels (Added)

**What it is.** The creator laid a log beside a beaver snare to steer the animal into it.

**Rule.** Placing 2 blocks of brush, sticks or logs on both sides of a trail snare narrows the run: catch chance ×1.3. A log laid across a beaver run above a lodge snare forces a dive into it: ×1.5. Each snare takes one funnel. Setting it takes 2 game minutes and leaves human scent for 1 game day (catch chance ×0.7 that day).

**Ties.** Traps, scent, beavers.

**Studio detail (Ecosystem and hunting).** Snare funnels.

**What counts.** A funnel is 2 blocks from tag `yearofice:funnel_materials` on each side of the snare, within 1 block and across the trail's direction (from the trail node list). The tag holds brush piles, stick bundles, logs, rocks and, in winter, packed snow blocks. The trail snare's guide sticks are part of any valid set; the funnel is the extra wall.

**Too much.** A block over the loop or a full wall across the trail blocks the run: animals detour and catch chance falls ×0.3. The ghost preview says "Blocked: they will go around."

**Stacking.** Place multipliers multiply but are capped at ×6 in total: on a trail ×4, beside cover ×1.5 and a funnel ×1.3 give 7.8, cut to 6. Scent multipliers (setting ×0.7, checking ×0.5) floor at ×0.35. Any handwear halves the funnel's scent time to 12 game hours.

**Lifetime.** Brush and stick funnels are flattened by 20 cm of new snow and need 1 game minute to fix; logs and rocks last. Snow walls harden and last until the thaw.

**Beavers in winter.** The under-ice set takes a dive fence instead of a log: 6 dry poles pushed through the hole around the set in a V (10 game minutes, ×1.5). In summer the log across the run above a slide snare stays ×1.5.

**Other targets.** Lynx and wolf trail sets take funnels at ×1.3. Cubby sets already narrow the approach and take none.

**Ties.** Trap catch shock (Ideas from viewers › Deepened entries): a funnelled snare still loses to a catch within 50 m. Trap sounds.

**Minecraft build.** The snare block entity reads its 8 neighbours on set and on each roll; a cheap check. Data: `funnel_mult`, `blocked_mult` and `place_mult_cap` in `data/yearofice/trap/trail_snare.json`. GameTest `yearofice:traps/funnel_cap` (batch `m5`): a baited, funnelled snare on a trail beside cover rolls at exactly the ×6 cap over 10,000 rolls (±2%).

**Risk.** Funnels everywhere. Guard: setting costs 2 game minutes and a day of scent, and the cap stops stacking.

## UI

### Fire time and the fire roster (Deepened)

**Missing so far.** Fires have states and fuel. The video read "52 minutes of firewood" off a fire, and later kept three fires going without a way to see them all.

**Fire time.** Looking at any fire, stove or smoker shows its state and fuel left in game time: "Fire: Low. Fuel 52 min." It reads the armloads in it and the burn rate of its setting.

**The roster.** A messenger page lists every lit fire within 2 km that the player built: kind, state, fuel left, and what is on it ("Smoker: 6 moose ribs, 3 h left"). Its header totals the day's wood: "3 fires: about 9 armloads a day." A fire under 15 minutes of fuel with food or a pot on it turns amber.

**Why it matters.** The cost of many fires becomes visible, which is the choice the video made by accident: one fire for everything, or three and the woodcutting that goes with them.

**Ties.** Fire states, warm rooms, the smoker, firewood drying.

**Build.** Fire block entities register with a per-player `FireRoster` in SavedData; the page reads it with no battery cost beyond the normal page cost.

**Studio detail (Look, sound and UI).** Fire time is a look readout; the roster is a new messenger screen. Both read the same fire record, so they never disagree.

**The look readout.** Looking at a fire, stove, smoker or hearth within 6 m for 0.5 s adds a second row to `yearofice:prompt_line`. The format is "{state}, {setting}. Fuel {time}." State and setting stay separate words, because Low is a setting, not a state (Blueprint › Fire, water and light).

| Case | Readout |
| --- | --- |
| A fire on a setting | "Fire, Low. Fuel 52 min." |
| Coals | "Coals. Out in 40 min." |
| Banked | "Banked. Relights until 21:40." |
| Smoker | "Smoker, Steady. Fuel 1 h 10 min. Ribs: 3 h left." |
| Uncovered fire in rain | "Fire, Steady. Rain: out in about 20 min." |
| Low fire in snowfall | "Fire, Low. Snow will put it out." |
| Wet fuel (moisture over 35%) | "Fire, Steady. Fuel 1 h. Smoky: wet wood." |

Times under 1 h show minutes, then "h min", then "about {n} days". They are game time, so they hold under any clock rate.

```latex
t_{left} = \frac{n_{armloads} \cdot t_{armload} \cdot k_{fuel}}{u_{setting}}, \qquad u = 0.4,\ 1,\ 3 \text{ for Low, Steady, Hot}
```

t\_armload is the build's burn minutes per armload at Steady; k\_fuel is the burn-time factor in `fire/fuels.json` (birch 1.6, spruce 1.0).

**The Fires screen.** A new messenger screen after Stores, with no battery cost, as for Stores. It holds at most 16 player-built fires, sorted by fuel left. Holdover fires, ground fires and wildfire are never listed.

```latex
A_{day} = \sum_f \frac{1440 \cdot u_f}{t_{armload,f} \cdot k_{fuel,f}}
```

The header rounds A\_day to whole armloads; coals and banked fires count 0. An amber row also carries the words "Low fuel", so colour never carries the meaning alone. A fire that went out with food on it reads "Smoker out: 6 ribs cooling."

**Sound and first use.** No new sound for the readout: the existing crackle by state is the cue within 30 m. Beyond 30 m, a row turning amber plays the prompt chime once and the messenger line "Smoker: 15 min of fuel. Ribs on it." At most one chime per fire per game day. The screen teaches itself once, when a second fire is lit: "Two fires lit. Messenger, Fires page, shows both."

**Edge cases.**

- **Unloaded chunks.** The roster projects fuel in closed form from the last step, as the engine's catch-up does, so a fire 1.5 km away reads true.
- **Raids and accidents.** A fire put out by an animal, a cave-in or a falling tree keeps its projected reading until the player sees it. Like trap catches, the device never reports what happened out of sight.
- **Battery 0.** The screen and chime go; the look readout stays, because it is the player's eye, not the device.
- **Presets and modes.** Gentle pauses on the screen; Endless and Hardcore are the same. Multiplayer: per player, own fires only.

**Minecraft build.** Server `com.yearofice.fire.FireRosterData` (SavedData: player UUID to a list of `RosterEntry(pos, kind, state, setting, armloads, lastStepMinute, contents)`), written on each `FireStateEvent`. Client `com.yearofice.client.messenger.FiresPage`. Payloads `yearofice:fire_roster_request` and `yearofice:fire_roster`, sent on open and every 5 real seconds while open. About 40 bytes an entry and no ticking. Lang keys `messenger.yearofice.fires.*`. GameTest `yearofice:ui/fire_roster_projection` (batch `m5`): a Steady spruce fire with 3 armloads, its chunk unloaded for 2 game hours, reads within 1 game minute of its true burn-out time.

**Risk.** Remote watching could become babysitting. The one-chime-a-day cap and the 2 km range keep it a planning page, not a pager.

| 3 | A Rest day yesterday | "Rest day: healing faster." |
| 4 | Up 3 days running | "Gaining: 3 days up." |
| 5 | Otherwise | "Burned {kcal}, ate {kcal}." |

Rows 1, 2 and 4 follow the Warnings preset (Blueprint › Realism presets): Standard repeats after 24 h clear, Brutal once a run, Gentle every morning.

```latex
n = \frac{W - W_{17}}{\overline{\Delta W}_{3d}}, \qquad W_{17} = 17 \cdot h^2
```

**Numbers.** Δ is today's weight minus yesterday's morning weight, to 0.1 kg. kcal round to 100. Strength shows its target while under it: "Strength 61 (longbow 70)." The draft's example breaks the body model: 800 kcal over 2,400 kcal per kg is −0.3 kg, not −0.4. Use "Weight −0.3 kg (68.9 kg)" with "Burned 3,400, ate 2,600."

**Edge cases.** After an SOS flight: "Back at the outpost. 72.0 kg. BMI 21.5." Units follow `mass_unit`. Battery 0: no line, but the weight graph still records, because it lives in the `yearofice:journal` attachment. Hardcore and Endless show the same.

**Feel with no device.** The body cue exists already (thinner hands below BMI 18.5). Added: each 2 kg lost since the last notch, the first wake plays a 2 s first-person belt-tightening animation with a leather creak. A flat-battery player still reads the trend.

**Minecraft build.** Server `com.yearofice.body.MorningReport`, built on `PlayerWakeUpEvent` from the `yearofice:body` attachment, sent as a `yearofice:morning_report` payload. Client `com.yearofice.client.ui.MorningLine` formats through `Units`. Lang keys `messenger.yearofice.body.*`. GameTest `yearofice:ui/morning_line` (batch `m5`): a player who burned 3,400 and ate 2,600 reads Δ −0.3 kg; one at BMI 17.8 losing 0.2 kg a day reads "about 4 days" ±1 for a 183 cm body.

**Risk.** A daily number can turn into a nag. A client option, "Morning detail", hides line 2 on routine days; line 1 always shows.

### Tip-up flags (Deepened)

**Missing so far.** Tip-ups are pinned on the map. The video's were buried by snow and lost.

**Rules.** A tip-up's flag springs up when a fish takes it, seen from 150 m in daylight and 40 m by headlamp. Fresh snow over 5 cm since the last check buries the flag unless a marker pole is set beside it (1 stick, 10 game seconds). The hole skins over at 2 cm a night below −20 °C and must be chipped open (2 game minutes) before the next catch. An unbaited tip-up catches at ×0.3.

**Ties.** Ice fishing (Blueprint › Fishing methods), snowfall, trap pins on the map.

**Studio detail (Look, sound and UI).** The flag is a world object the player scans for across the ice; the map never reports it.

**Model.** `yearofice:tip_up` is a block with states `flag` (down, up), `buried` (true, false) and `skin` (0–3: open, glaze, grey, white). The flag is 0.3 × 0.3 m of saturated red on a 0.6 m spring, the only saturated red on winter ice. It passes the hand-made props readability check at 10 m.

**Seen from 150 m.** At 1080p and 75° field of view, the flag is about 2 pixels high at 150 m. It reads by colour against snow and by the spring's motion, so the spring plays a 0.5 s wobble when the flag rises. The flag is part of the block model, not a block-entity renderer, because block-entity renderers stop at 64 blocks; a block-state model draws to the render distance.

**Night: a clash and its fix.** The draft's 40 m by headlamp exceeds the headlamp's 25 m beam (Blueprint › Fire, water and light). Fix: 25 m in the beam, 8 m in wide mode, and 40 m under a full moon on snow, which matches the engine's night-light table. A tin-lid reflector on the flag (1 tin can, 2 game minutes) doubles each range.

**Snow and ice on screen.**

1. A buried tip-up shows as a low snow mound with the line stick's tip out. Prompt: "E  Dig out tip-up (20 s)."
2. A marker pole is a 2 m blazed stick model, seen at 150 m like a marker post.
3. Skin steps from clear glaze to grey to white. Chipping uses the existing chisel strike sound and readout "Hole skinned over: 2 cm."

**Sound.** Silent beyond 10 m, as the draft says. Within 10 m, `yearofice:trap.tipup_flag`: a spring twang and a spool whir as the fish runs. A sleeping player is never woken by it.

**Map.** Tip-up pins keep "time since checked" and never show the flag's state (the messenger rule). With the battery flat the pins vanish, and buried flags are lost exactly as in the video.

**Edge cases.** Blizzard caps sight at 20 m. Aurora adds the engine's +10 m at night. A tip-up on ice that turns rotten joins Gear left on the ice. Gentle, Standard and Brutal are the same.

**Minecraft build.** `com.yearofice.hunt.fishing.TipUp`, server side. The catch sets `flag=up` with one block update. The snow pass sets `buried`; the ice pass steps `skin` on `ClockEvent.Day`. Cost: no ticking block entity. GameTest `yearofice:fishing/tipup_buried` (batch `m7`): with 6 cm of fresh snow a raised flag becomes buried; with a marker pole beside it, it stays visible.

**Risk.** Scanning 10 holes from the shore is the point, not an exploit; the tackle's hook and line counts cap the number of tip-ups.

## Design

### Hand-made props (Added)

**What it is.** The creator modelled the small props himself (snare, hanging pot, bed, minnow trap, smoker, squirrel, tip-up) and paid artists for the big animals. The same split fits the studio.

**Rule for the studio.** Small props are blocky models made in Blockbench and exported for GeckoLib or as plain block models: snare, deadfall, hanging pot, cooking stand, minnow trap, smoker, tip-up, cache, moose call, lantern. Big animals (moose, bears, wolves, beaver, wolverine) get the art department's full GeckoLib models and animations. Each prop ships with its texture, its model and a one-line readability check: it must be recognisable from 10 m in first person.

**Ties.** Minecraft engine › Libraries; the art department in the studio chain.

**Studio detail (Building and crafting).** Hand-made props.

**Budget.** The engine allows 25 piece models (Minecraft engine › Look). The props below take 17, which leaves 8 for later tabs. Prefer plain block models and blockstates. Use GeckoLib only where motion carries information, such as a flag going up or a spring pole snapping.

| Prop | Form | States or animations | Must read at 10 m |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Snare | Block model | Set, sprung, caught | Sprung against set, from the trail, since the messenger never reports catches |
| Deadfall | Block model | Set, sprung | The fallen log |
| Hanging pot and cooking stand | Block models | Empty, water, food; steam particle | Steam means it is boiling |
| Minnow trap | Block model in water | Empty, holding | The float line |
| Smoker, smoke rack | Multiblock members | Fish hung 0–3 | The fish count |
| Tip-up | GeckoLib block | Flag down, flag up, spring wobble | The flag, from 50 m |
| Moose call | GeckoLib item | Raise to mouth | — |
| Lantern | Block model | Hanging or standing, lit or unlit | Lit |
| Shoring post and cap | Thin block models | Rot words as texture steps | Rotten wood |
| Pot crane | Block model | Swung in, swung out | Over the fire or not |
| Hearth and throat | Block models | Fire states | — |

| Awning | Blocks and slabs | Snow layers | Sagging at load 0.8 |
| Cache leg | Block model | Peeled, banded | The band |

Small game (hare, squirrel, grouse, ptarmigan) is built in-house on GeckoLib with 4 animations each: idle, move, alarm and dead. That is the creator's hand-made squirrel. The big animals stay with the art department.

**The readability check.**

1. The screenshot checker renders the prop at 10 blocks in first person in four lights: July noon, overcast, dusk by firelight at level 9, and snow cover in January.
2. A grayscale copy must still show the silhouette.
3. The prop's mean luminance must differ from its usual ground by 20% or more in every light.
4. Two people who have not seen the model name it within 3 s. Two out of two passes.
5. Every gameplay state must be told apart at 10 blocks.

**Rules.** Textures are 16 px per block in the shared palette. Block models have up to 24 elements; GeckoLib props up to 40 cubes and 3 animations. An animated prop freezes in its static pose beyond 32 blocks.

**Paths.** Sources go in `art/blockbench/<id>.bbmodel`. Exports go to `assets/yearofice/models/block/<id>.json` and `textures/block/<id>.png`, or for GeckoLib `assets/yearofice/geo/block/<id>.geo.json` and `animations/block/<id>.animation.json`.

**Workflow.** The building and crafting department writes a prop card: id, size in blocks, states and what each state must say. Art builds the model. It merges once the checker passes, in its system's milestone: snares M5, tip-ups M7, lanterns and shoring M8.

**Risk.** Hand-made props can drift from the artists' animals. The guard is one palette, one pixel density and a review of each prop beside the moose.

### The competition format (Deepened, proposed)

**Status.** Co-op is decided: solo first, co-op after 1.0 (Blueprint › Open decisions). This records what the video describes, as the proposal for the co-op build.

**The format the creator describes.**

- Teams of two, each pair in its own valley. One game day equals one calendar day, starting on the real date (the existing real-calendar mode).
- Last team standing wins. Nobody knows whether other teams are still in.
- Any player may tap out from the messenger; the pair continues until both are out or extracted.
- The settings are deliberately harder than solo, so people tap out.

**A preset for it: Expedition.** Brutal (Blueprint › Realism presets) plus:

| Setting | Expedition value |
| --- | --- |
| Big-game opportunities from the encounter director | ×0.6 |
| Infection and parasite chances | ×1.5 |
| Pack weight limits | −10% |
| Arrows | One per craft (as everywhere) and a 20% chance an arrow breaks on a miss |
| SOS | Ends the player's run at once; no return after 14 days |

**Partners.** A partner's body line is visible on the messenger only within 2 km, so the pair must meet to check on each other.

**Ties.** Real-calendar mode, presets, the encounter director, SOS (Blueprint › Collapse and SOS).

**Studio detail (Look, sound and UI, proposed).** These are the screens the format would need. None ships before co-op, which comes after 1.0 (Ruhan, 2026-10-19: solo first); until then the preset stays hidden behind the client option `expedition_preview = false`.

**What players never see.** No scoreboard, no team list, no "teams left" count. In an Expedition world the vanilla player list (Tab) and name tags are cancelled (`RenderGuiLayerEvent` for the player list, `RenderNameTagEvent`), so a partner is found by eye, by sound and by the partner line. Other teams play on other servers; nothing in the game connects them.

**The partner line.** A row at the top of the Vitals screen, refreshed once a game minute:

| Partner | Row |
| --- | --- |
| Within 2 km | "{name}: 63.8 kg, BMI 19.0. Condition 72. 1.4 km NE." |
| Beyond 2 km | "{name}: out of range since 14:10." |
| Tapped out | "{name}: flown out, day 84." |

Distance rounds to 0.1 km and bearing to 8 directions, so the line guides a meeting without being a GPS fix. It costs the Vitals screen's normal drain, and nothing when the battery is 0. A partner in trouble triggers no alert, because the messenger never reports what happens out of sight.

**Tapping out.** The existing SOS hold (3 real seconds, then confirm) is the tap-out. The Endless sequence's white noise and rotor sound play, then one screen: "Out. Day 84. {cause}." The tapped-out player becomes a spectator locked to the partner's first-person view. They have no HUD and no chat to the partner, so the remaining player is still alone with the choice.

**Chat.** In-game chat between partners works only within 2 km, so the game's own channel follows the same rule as the partner line. Outside voice tools are the players' business.

**The real calendar on screen.** The Forecast header reads "Expedition day 84. Real date, game date matched." A missed holiday gets one journal line, as the video's Christmas did.

**Sound.** No new events. The partner's axe, footsteps and voice use their normal ranges; below −30 °C they carry 30% farther, which is how a pair finds each other in deep cold.

**The record.** At each tap-out the server writes `expedition/<team>.json`: seed, start date, day out, cause, and a SHA-256 of the journal. An organiser compares the files; the game never names a winner.

**A clash for the co-op decision.** In multiplayer the engine's clock runs at the slowest rate any player asks for, so one partner's hold-to-work time-lapse would never run while the other walks. Proposal: in Expedition, work charges its game minutes to the worker's body (kcal, fatigue) while the world clock stays shared. That is a co-op rule, not a UI one, and goes to the Director with the decision.

**Minecraft build.** `com.yearofice.core.rules.Expedition` (server: partner pairs, chat range, spectator lock, the record file) and `com.yearofice.client.messenger.PartnerRow`. Payload `yearofice:partner_status`, once a game minute while within 2 km. GameTest `yearofice:rules/expedition_partner_range` (batch `m9`): partners 1.9 km apart see each other's row; at 2.1 km both read "out of range."

**Risk.** Hidden status could feel like playing blind. The partner line and the real calendar give the pair a shared clock and a reason to meet, which is where the format's stories come from.

### Data-pack paths

- `data/yearofice/weather/interior_alaska.json`, its `alerts` block: alert texts and triggers (thunderstorm, snowfall, freezing overnight, leaf fall, greening).
- `data/yearofice/season/leaf_stages.json`, `season/green_up.json`: stage day offsets and tint steps.
- `data/yearofice/fire/danger.json`: index thresholds and spark chances; the item data map `data/yearofice/data_maps/item/fuel.json`: species heat, burn time and moisture.
- `data/yearofice/worldgen/placed_feature/coal_seam.json`, `rock_overhang.json`, `lily_pad.json`.
- The block data map `data/yearofice/data_maps/block/heat.json` (RECONCILE 21): adds `earth` (U 0.5, C 4) and `dressed_stone` (C 10).
- `data/yearofice/multiblock/<id>.json` for the dugout and hearth multiblocks, and the block data map `data/yearofice/data_maps/block/piece.json` for per-block numbers: shoring, flue, awning, deck, path, retaining wall, marker post (RECONCILE 22).
- Entries in the item data map `data/yearofice/data_maps/item/food.json`: worm, animal fat, game guts; `food/stomach.json`: capacity and empty rates.
- `data/yearofice/`plant/\<species>\_bush.json: per species regrow days and pick yield (RECONCILE 130).
- The `wound` and `climb` blocks of each `data/yearofice/species/<id>.json`: wound healing and climbing tables. There is no `animal/` data kind (RECONCILE 6).

### GameTests

1. `yearofice:build/dugout_never_yields` (batch `m8`): dig a full 4 × 2 × 6 hillside dugout on 50 seeds; 0 resource items drop, and no block outside the footprint changes.
2. `yearofice:build/dugout_heat` (batch `m8`): a 3 × 4 × 2 dugout with a log front at −35 °C outside settles between −12 and −8 °C with only the player; with a fireplace on Low it settles between −2 and +2 °C.
3. `yearofice:fire/moisture_drying` (batch `m8`): split green birch at 50% in a woodshed from June 1 reaches under 20% between July 22 and August 5 (B4, Proposed; was June 30 to July 8); scattered unsplit logs from October 1 are still above 35% on November 30.
4. `yearofice:worldgen/coal_seams` (batch `m2`): over 200 seeds, every seed has 3–6 seams, at least one between 1.5 and 3 km from the start, none within 600 m, and none below the surface.
5. `yearofice:story/mist_count` (batch `m7`): over 20 simulated years, mist mornings per year fall between 25 and 40, and none form on a day with wind over 2 m/s at 05:00.
6. `yearofice:food/stomach_limits` (batch `m5`): a full stomach empties to under 5% in 13–15 game hours awake; eating stops at 100%; a feast from a cache never pushes the stomach above 100%.
