# Open world in depth

The detailed rules for the open world, its story and the engine. The summaries stay in the main blueprint tab, under The open world, Data formats and Engine rules; this tab carries the full specifications.

## Rare events

**How events roll.** On May 1 each event rolls once on its own against its chance, for about 1.7 surprises a year on average from year 2, and its date is drawn inside its window. The hard winter, big berry year and storm of the decade are not rolled in year 1, so the M7 test counts each event over the years in which it could roll.

## Outposts

**What counts as an outpost.** Any place with a fire ring or stove, a roofed shelter piece (a lean-to counts) and a store within 20 m of each other. The messenger then registers it, names it after its region ("Salmon Bend camp"), lets the player rename it, and shows an outpost card like the cabin card.

**Travel to.** Choosing a linked outpost on the messenger map offers Travel to. The player walks the route at the work-until-done rate instead of the travel rate and pays the full game hours, calories, cold and wear. It stops for anything that would stop a job: an animal that notices the player, a change to storm or blizzard, a new hypothermia stage, energy below 3, or a pinned trap due for checking on the way. It is not offered over unlinked ground, so a network has to be built before it is used.

**What the network knows.** The messenger lists each outpost with its stock and condition as last seen, with the date. It never shows live status: a raid is discovered on arrival. Crafting still draws only from storage within 20 m, so goods move between outposts on the player's back, sled or canoe.

**Upkeep.** An outpost unvisited for 60 days rolls each week: a ground store in a bear range is raided 5% of the time, a raised cache never; a wolverine breaks into any cache without metal-wrapped legs 3% of winter weeks. Mice and snow load follow the cabin rules. Each outpost adds about 20–60 KB to the save.

## Traversal

**Time scale.** Speeds are world speeds at the travel rate in Survival tuning: while the player moves on foot, snowshoes, sled, raft or canoe with no job running, the clock runs 1 game minute per 5 real seconds. Stalking, waiting, fishing and camp time stay on the world clock. At the 3.0 m/s walking pace, 1 km on open ground takes about 1 game hour and 250 kcal. The Travel rate is fixed, so these game hours per km, and the 5.5 real minutes per km, hold at every day length the player picks (24–72 real minutes, decided 2026-10-19); a longer day only slows the world clock.

**Ground.** Walking speed = 3.0 m/s × ground or trail factor × snow factor × load factor (the encumbrance table). A trail factor replaces the ground factor. Snow over 40 cm comes over the boot tops for a player without snowshoes: the feet zone's wetness rises 10% per 100 m walked, so about 1 km soaks the boots and starts the frostbite timer for feet below −20 °C (Clothing). Snowshoes keep the feet on top and the boots dry, which is the video's chapter 19 lesson.

| Ground | Factor |
| --- | --- |
| Open ground, gravel bar, tundra, wind-packed snow on river ice | 1.0 |
| Spruce taiga, willow thicket | 0.75 |
| Bog and muskeg | 0.6, and boots get wet |
| Scree and slopes over 25° | 0.5 uphill, 0.7 downhill |
| Worn path, worn trail, cut trail | 1.1, 1.2, 1.25 |

**River current.** The main river runs at 1.2 m/s on average and 2.5 m/s in Upriver Canyon. Rapids and Long Falls block any canoe going up the canyon; the 400 m portage is the only way. Logs float at the current's speed and jam at snags, as in Building. Tipping: on a raft or in a canoe, each surge of a hooked fish over 3 kg and each beaver strike rolls a capsize chance of 5% on a raft and 10% in a canoe, ×2 standing, ×0.5 kneeling, ×2 below 3 energy bolts, and ×1.5 in current over 1.5 m/s. A tipped player is in the water (Swimming and working in water), and unlashed gear drifts or sinks (Items › Items in the river). Lashing gear takes 2 game minutes per item at the bank.

**The shoulder seasons.** For about 2 weeks at freeze-up and 2 at breakup the river is neither paddled nor walked. An outpost across or up the river is cut off then, and the messenger names those weeks a season ahead.

## Taming a wolf

**Daily needs.** A grown wolf eats 1 kg a day resting and 2 kg on a working day (pulling, hunting, guarding a kill). A pup taken at 6–8 weeks eats 0.5 kg, rising to 1 kg at 6 months. It eats meat, fish, organs, fat, guts and hide scraps, never berries or grain. It drinks wherever there is open water or snow. Below −30 °C, a wolf with no roofed spot (a lean-to, a bough doghouse or the cabin) loses 5 Condition a night.

**Feeding.** At Wary, food is left on the ground. From Tolerant it can be fed by hand. At Companion, the cache feeds it automatically at a set ration, as the player's auto-eat does, and the cache's days-left figure counts the wolf.

**Trust day by day.**

| Action | Trust | From stage | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Food left on the ground | +2 | Any | Once a day |
| Food from the hand | +3 | Tolerant | Once a day |
| Sitting within 10 m for 10 game minutes | +1 | Wary | Twice a day |
| Petting (right mouse on the wolf) | +1 | Bonded | Once a day |
| Play: throwing a stick | +1, and Spirits +2 | Bonded | Once a day |
| Sharing a kill (giving it the organs) | +5 | Any | Once per kill |
| Sleeping in the same shelter | +1 | Bonded | Once a night |
| A command obeyed and rewarded | +1 | Bonded | 3 a day |

Where Ideas from viewers › Taming a wolf without bones sets a different per-action limit (food left 3 times a day, sitting within 20 m, talking with the V key), that limit applies first (RECONCILE 30). Daily gain is then capped at +3 below 50 trust, +2 from 50 to 80, and +1 above 80. A den pup and a yearling start at 0, an orphan at 10. At the caps, a pup needs at least 32 days to reach Companion, which matches the M9 test.

**Growth.** Trust and size are separate: a Companion pup still waits for its body.

| Age | Weight | What it can do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2–4 months | 5–12 kg | Follows and plays; chews any hide left on the floor (10% chance a night to ruin it) |
| 4–8 months | 15–25 kg | Pins hares, tracks blood trails, growls at bears and wolves |
| 8–12 months | 25–35 kg | Carries a 5 kg pack; joins a wolf drive |
| 12 months on | 35–45 kg | Carries 10 kg; holds a moose at bay; can be harnessed |

**Commands.** A tap of G whistles Come. Holding G opens a command wheel (Minecraft engine › Controls). A command not yet learned obeys at half the chance below.

| Command | Needs | What the wolf does | Learned by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Come | Bonded | Returns to the player | Known at Bonded |
| Free | Bonded | Roams within 150 m; hunts hares and grouse and eats them | Known at Bonded |
| Heel | Bonded | Stays within 3 m and ignores game | 10 practice sessions |
| Stay | Bonded | Holds a spot for up to 2 game hours or until danger | 10 sessions |
| Track | Bonded, 4 months | Follows the track or blood trail the player points at, across rock and ice | 5 trails followed together |
| Guard | Companion | Sleeps at a spot (garden, cache, door); keeps hares off and howls at anything that comes | 10 sessions |
| Drive | Companion, 8 months | Circles and pushes game toward the player | 3 drives ending in a kill |
| Hold | Companion, 12 months | Keeps a moose or bear at bay for 60 game seconds; 20% chance the wolf is kicked or swiped | 1 moose hunt together |
| Pack | Companion, 8 months | Takes the pack on or off | Known |
| Harness | Companion, 12 months | Pulls a sled | 15 sessions with a light sled |

A practice session takes 10 game minutes and 0.1 kg of meat as reward, at most twice a day per command. The chance a learned command is obeyed:

```latex
P_{obey} = \min\left(1,\ 0.5 + \frac{trust - 50}{60}\right) \cdot m_{distraction}
```

m\_distraction is 1.0 normally, 0.5 with game in sight, and 0.3 mid-chase.

**Reading the wolf.** Before any growl, a wolf that scents danger stiffens, raises its hackles and stares. The messenger's compass shows a small mark where it is looking, so its nose works as the player's early warning.

**Injury and loss.** A wolf has its own Condition from 0 to 100. Porcupine quills cost 10 and must be pulled (2 game minutes); a bear swipe costs 40; a fight with a wild pack 20–60; each day after 3 without food costs 10. Rest heals 5 a day. At 0 the wolf dies, the journal records it, and Spirits fall as in the Spirits table. A wolf that leaves for a pack may be seen with it later and never attacks the player first. Leaving depends on the realism preset (Ruhan, 2026-10-19), set by the dial companion.wolf\_leaves in yearofice-common.toml: never on Gentle, recoverable on Standard, permanent on Brutal. The February roll runs once a game day while trust is under 80, with p = (80 − trust) ÷ 400, so 5% a day at 60 trust; it is halved on a day the wolf was fed and slept under a roof. On Gentle the roll never runs. On Standard the Trail Messenger warns on January 25 when trust is under 80 ("Restless; keep it close."), and a wolf that leaves returns to camp within 10–20 game days at 40 trust, unless it was killed meanwhile. On Brutal a wolf that leaves is gone for good. Test (batch m9): over 1,000 simulated Februaries at 60 trust, Standard loses a wolf in 70–85% of runs and gets it back in all of them; Gentle loses none.

**The wolf card.** Sneaking and pressing right mouse on the wolf shows its name, age, stage word with a five-pip bar, hunger, Condition and learned commands.

**Team rules.**

- **Who pulls.** 3–6 wolves, each a Companion and at least 12 months old. The highest-trust wolf leads.
- **Bolting.** When the team sees caribou or moose within 150 m, it bolts on 30% of sightings, or 50% if the leader is under 90 trust. It never bolts if every wolf is above 90. A bolt runs at 2.5× for up to 300 m and tips the sled 20% of the time on rough ground, spilling anything not lashed.
- **Braking.** Holding S sets the claw brake, which stops a bolt within 5 seconds if the leader is above 80 trust, or 15 seconds otherwise.
- **Work limits.** A team pulls 4 game hours, then rests 1, for at most 8 hours a day. Every wolf in harness eats 2 kg that day.
- **New members.** A new wolf shares the yard for 14 days before joining. Harnessed earlier, it starts a fight 20% of the time, costing both wolves 10 Condition.
- **Breeding.** A male and a female, both Companions and at least 22 months old, can breed in February. Given a roofed den or doghouse, the female has 4–6 pups in May. Pups born to tame parents start at 30 trust and grow on the table above. One litter per pair per year is how a team grows without raiding dens.

## Story

**Who the player is.** The player is the new observer for a small research program that has logged the valley's seasons for 15 years. They land by floatplane on May 1, one year after the last observer vanished. The pilot cannot wait for the weather, and the messenger can call a flight out at any time, so staying is the player's choice. The player has no name on screen; the radio calls them "Observer".

**Why the outpost was abandoned.** The outpost is Station Nine, built by the program 15 years ago on a flat riverbank where Arvid Lund once read the ice. Its last observer, Dr. Elin Marsh, wintered there alone for nearly three years and disappeared in April a year ago. Nobody wintered after her. Ninety centimeters of unshoveled snow broke the north half of the roof, bears and mice emptied the stores, the radio's battery died, and a windstorm bent the mast. The program reopened the station on a small grant, so the breakup record begun in 1938 would not break. The crate is everything the grant could fly in.

**Four layers of past.** The valley's earlier people are written in the set pieces in Biomes and resources. Elin is the layer that ties them together, because she found them all before the player did.

| Layer | Who | When | Where it is found | What it gives the player |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The miners | Kessler and Doane | 1906–1912 | The ledger and grave board at the mining camp, the roadhouse guest book, "D. 1909" in the ice cave | Found metal, and the valley's oldest dates |
| The trapper | Arvid Lund | 1938–1971 | His cabin (trapline board, lynx tally, last note), his 2–3 caches, his drowned line cabin in the beaver valley | Trapline sites, tool upgrades, the canoe recipe, and 34 breakup dates pencilled on his cabin wall |
| The pilot | J.O. | September 1979 | The note in the wreck's windscreen; the lookout log's entry two days later | The wreck's salvage, which the radio needs |
| The observer | Dr. Elin Marsh | Her years 1–3, ending a year ago | 24 logbook pages: 7 at the station and its yard, 7 at her numbered plots, 10 at set pieces and wild places | Plant and sign identifications, recipes, fishing spots, and the story of her last spring |

- **The cabin wall, beside the lynx tally.** Breakup dates in pencil, one a year from 1938 to 1971, some with a word: "May 6 '62. Lost the lower sets to the ice again." *Gives:* the first 34 entries of the ice record.
- **Inside each cache lid.** "Take what you need. Leave the can. A.L." *Gives:* nothing more than the cache's tool upgrade.

**How it unfolds.** Nothing is in order and nothing is required. The outpost pages and the nearest set pieces are reached in week 1; the plots draw the player across the core and its neighbors in the first summer; the far pages reward the travel of years 2 and 3. The notebook shows each thread as a count ("Elin Marsh: 9 of 24 pages") and a timeline of her dates. A player who reads every layer sees one valley through 120 years: the miners' meat in the ice cave, Arvid's lynx tally, J.O.'s fire on the bar, Elin's wolf.

**The wolf with the rawhide strip.** From year 1, one grey wolf in the home pack wears a frayed rawhide strip. He keeps 100 m from the player, never attacks first, and cannot be tamed: his trust stops at 25. If he is killed, the player finds Tok's collar, Spirits fall 10, and Kari's next call changes.

**No ending.** After the radio works, each year brings one new thread on the radio or in the land, from a pool, in order:

1. **First contact.** Dana: "Elin Marsh is presumed lost. The search last May found nothing past the cirque. Please don't go looking farther than is safe."
2. **The first summer after contact.** Pete: "Flew the upriver route Tuesday. Camp smoke 25 km east of the pass, out over the Kettle Hills. No camp I know of out there."
3. **Each spring, the first sked after breakup.** Kari Marsh, Elin's sister: "She always said she wanted to be on the ice when it went. Tell me the date when it goes out."
4. **Two years after contact.** Whitlock: "A collared caribou's camera caught a pack and a figure behind it. Too far to tell. I'm not sending it to anyone else."
5. **Three years after contact.** A woman's wool mitten turns up in a cache in Windgap Pass that was empty the year before.
6. **Every year after that.** One of six rumors a year: a trapper's story downriver, a musher who saw a person's tracks among wolf tracks, a letter with no return address.

None of them confirms anything. The notebook's last line for the thread always reads "Elin Marsh: not found", and the game never shows her.

**Earning a drop.** The year has four seasons: May–July, August–October, November–January and February–April. A season that reaches 100 points earns one drop at its end, on the first flyable day. - **Unattended crates.** A crate holding food that sits 2 days draws bears by its scent.

**Salt from the mineral lick (video 2, A4; RECONCILE 170).** The drop's 5 kg of salt stays the easy route; a mineral lick is the slow one. The video boiled seawater for salt, but there is no sea in the interior, and real interior licks carry a salty crust.

**Where.** Every lick carries `yearofice:salt_crust` blocks: the valley's 2–4 lick set pieces (Blueprint › Set pieces) and its 6–12 natural-feature licks (World map). The seep variant caps at 1 kg of crust; the clay-bank variant shows a thinner crust and caps at 0.5 kg. The crust exists only while the ground is thawed, May to September; from October it reads "Frozen mud. Nothing to scrape."

1. **Scrape.** Hold RMB with a knife on a crust block: 0.05 kg of `yearofice:lick_crust` in 15 game minutes, at the light-work rate.
2. **Boil down.** At a fire with a pot, 2 kg of crust in 4 L of water is strained through cloth or grass and boiled down over 2 game hours. It burns 1 armload and yields 0.15 kg of `yearofice:lick_salt`.

**Regrowth.** A lick regrows 0.2 kg of crust a week up to its cap. Regrowth doubles in the first dry week after rain, as salts wick up and dry on the surface (the Environment field gives rain and dryness). One lick fully scraped through a summer gives about 5 kg of crust; the valley's licks together give 1–2 kg of salt a year.

**Uses.** `lick_salt` and the crate's `rock_salt` are both in `#yearofice:salt`, so every recipe that asks for salt takes either. Salting meat or fish costs 1 kg of salt per 5 kg, packed in a barrel; that dose is what Blueprint › Preserving's "salt doubles every figure" costs. A salted green hide takes 0.1 kg per kg (Decay and time), and sauerkraut 30 g per 1.5 kg of cabbage. So lick salt goes on what matters most: a few hides, one batch of winter fish, the sauerkraut.

**Danger.** Moose and Dall sheep visit licks at dawn and dusk (Water, calls and encounters › Water draws animals). Scraping at midday is safe; scraping at dusk risks a cow with a calf at under 30 m, by Blueprint › Danger in detail › Moose. Game trails converge on the white crust, so a lick is easy to read and easy to find.

**On screen and sound.** Inspecting a lick reads "White crust: about 0.6 kg to scrape." The scrape plays `yearofice:work.crust_scrape`. A pot of lick brine boils down to a grey crust ring, and the yield line reads "0.15 kg of salt from 2 kg of crust."

**Failure cases.** Boiling unstrained crust gives 0.15 kg of `lick_salt` at Poor quality, which salts at half strength. A pot left past dry on a Hot fire scorches: the salt is lost and the pot takes −10 condition. Rain on the crust before it is scraped washes 0.1 kg away.

**Ties.** Preserving (Blueprint), Decay and time, the fire and cooking pot, set pieces and natural features (World map), and moose and sheep at the lick.

**Build.** Items `yearofice:lick_crust` and `yearofice:lick_salt`; tag `#yearofice:salt` (with `rock_salt`); the existing block `yearofice:salt_crust` with state `crust` 0–4 (0.25 kg a step); action `data/yearofice/action/scrape_lick.json`; recipe `data/yearofice/recipe/station/lick_salt.json` at the fire; regrowth in `forage.LickCrust` on the day pass, with catch-up in closed form; tuning `[forage] lick_regrow_kg_per_week = 0.2`. **GameTests.** `yearofice:forage/lick_salt_yield` (M5): 40 scrapes over 2 full licks give 2.0 kg of crust in 10 ±0.1 game hours, and one boil gives 0.15 kg of salt and burns 1 armload. `yearofice:forage/lick_regrowth` (M5): an empty seep lick holds 0.2 ±0.01 kg after 7 dry game days, 0.4 after a dry week that follows rain, never more than 1 kg, and grows nothing from October to April.

## Data formats

**New data kinds.** The open world uses these kinds under `data/yearofice/` (Minecraft development › Data kinds): `region/<cell>.json` for the 16 home-valley regions (the Interior's districts and named areas are in the World map tab), `event/<id>.json` for rare events, `ambition/<id>.json`, `prompt/<id>.json`, `note/<id>.json` for story notes, `setpiece/<id>.json` with its structure templates, and the weather table `weather/interior_alaska.json`. Each kind has one codec in `com.yearofice.data.schema`; the reload listener decodes every file in strict mode, keys are snake\_case with unit suffixes, and region and set-piece placement files are frozen into `yearofice_world` at world creation. *Test:* a bad field fails the load with the file name, entry id and field path, and a camelCase key fails with its snake\_case form as the fix.

**Condition language.** Prompts, events and ambitions share one tiny expression language, parsed by `data.expr` into a tree when the data loads and evaluated in Java; nothing is compiled or run as a script.

- **Values.** Numbers, strings, `true` and `false`.
- **Operators.** `< <= > >= == !=`, `&& || !`, `+ - * /` and brackets.
- **State.** Read through namespaced facades: `calendar`, `forecast`, `player`, `stores`, `need`, `known`, `traps`, `wolf`, `region`, `year`. A field the schema marks `hidden` is refused when the file loads.
- **Functions.** A short fixed list, for example `forecast.min_c(days)`, `need.armloads(days)`, `known.has(id)`, `traps.oldest_h()`, `calendar.between("06-10", "08-20")`. Names are snake\_case, like every data key.

**Item entry.** An item is a registered id; its numbers live in the item data maps (Minecraft development › Data kinds): `weight` in kg, `food` (kcal, macros, vitamin C, `spoil_days_at_5c`), `props`, `fuel` and `tool`. Freshness is the `yearofice:fresh` component and condition is vanilla durability (RECONCILE 69, 79), and weighed food counts in 100 g stack units. A catalogue item adds `"drop": { "points": 30, "landing_only": false, "min_year": 1 }` to its `props` entry. Food and hunting owns the final food fields; the example below, from `data/yearofice/data_maps/item/food.json`, shows the shape.

```json
{
  "values": {
    "yearofice:flour": {
      "kcal_per_kg": 3640,
      "macros_pct": { "carbs": 76, "protein": 10, "fat": 1 },
      "vit_c_mg_per_kg": 0,
      "spoil_days_at_5c": 365,
      "damp_days": 30,
      "frozen_stops": true,
      "scent": 0.1,
      "uses": ["bread", "bannock", "hen_feed"]
    }
  }
}
```

**Biome entry.** Minecraft biomes set only the look (Minecraft development › YoiBiomeSource and the biome list); gameplay reads our cover class. Cover-class rules are tested top to bottom, as in the biome table, and the first match wins. They sit in the `cover` list of `data/yearofice/worldgen_params/valley.json`, frozen into `yearofice_world` at creation; plant ids follow the Plants and fungi tab, with every berry block ending in `_bush`.

```json
{
```

## Engine rules

**Save layout.** Minecraft's world folder is the save (Minecraft engine › What is saved where). The open world's state maps onto it like this.

| Old store | Minecraft form | Written |
| --- | --- | --- |
| meta | SavedData `yearofice_world`: seed, attempt k, generator version, mode, realism preset, day length | At creation; on a settings change |
| state | SavedData `yearofice_clock`, `yearofice_populations`, `yearofice_region_<cell>`, `yearofice_radio`; outposts in `yearofice_markers` | Minecraft's autosave and the forced saves below |
| chunks | Minecraft chunks; placed pieces are blocks; chunk attachments hold tree records, trail wear per 2 m cell, the season stamp and sign | Only changed chunks |
| journal | The `yearofice:journal` player attachment and monthly snapshots | Appended |
| backups | 3 rotated copies of our SavedData under `<world>/yearofice/backups/` | Each autosave |

**Edit records.** A felled tree is a stump block plus its tree record with the felling day; regrowth is worked out from that date by `REGROW` ops and never written daily. A placed piece is an ordinary block or block entity. Dropped items last 7 game days (RECONCILE 89, 97); a pack left at a collapse or gear lost in water becomes a `left_pack` or `lost_gear` block, which sets `placed_mask` and never expires. Nothing merges into ground piles.

**Writing safely.** Minecraft writes region and `.dat` files atomically, and the mod keeps 3 rotated copies of its SavedData. Autosave is Minecraft's every 5 real minutes, plus a forced save on sleep, on crossing a region border and before any fast-forward. A world is exported as its folder. A world made by an older generator version opens only under its save rule (Minecraft development › Content pipeline), because Ruhan's own worlds must survive every update (decided 2026-10-19).

**Save budget.** The mod's own data (SavedData and attachments) must stay under 5 MB after five years of heavy play. Minecraft's region files are budgeted separately (World map › Performance and storage).

| Part | Five-year estimate |
| --- | --- |
| Tree records for about 2,000 felled trees | 10 KB |
| Piece, room and multiblock records for about 3,000 placed pieces | 75 KB |
| `left_pack` and `lost_gear` records | 20 KB |
| Trail wear and the revealed map (`yearofice:map` tiles) | 400 KB |
| Outposts and stores as last seen | 300 KB |
| Journal, weight graph, ice record, year cards | 400 KB |
| Populations, animals, wolf, radio | 50 KB |
| Total, before 3 backups | About 1.3 MB |

Generator layers are cached outside the save (an LRU of 10 in memory and `<world>/yearofice/layers/` on disk; World map › Performance and storage) and may be dropped at any time, because they rebuild from the seed.

**Runtime randomness.** Each runtime system draws from `hash(seed, system, day)`, and catch-up rolls from `hash(seed, chunk, "catchup", day)`. Reloading a save never rerolls a snare or a catch, and a recorded play session replays the same way, which the balance simulator relies on.

**Tier handover.** An animal is promoted to Active at 150 m and demoted at 180 m, so it never flickers between tiers at the border. Individuals drawn from a region's population get fixed slots from `hash(seed, region, species, slot)`, so the same moose returns to the same willow flat. At most 40 animals are Active and 200 Nearby; past that, the farthest are demoted first. Ties to the player (the wolf, anything stalking or following) never count against the cap.

**Catch-up.** On return to a chunk, elapsed game hours are settled with closed-form rolls: snares as one roll per dusk and dawn missed, spoilage by integrating the temperature record, regrowth by date. Catch-up runs in world.catchup.CatchUp at up to 2 ms per chunk, spread over ticks, and LoadGate holds a chunk back from the client until its catch-up is done (RECONCILE 98). Fires far away run on 32 m cells in the Abstract tier.

**Fast-forward.** Sleep, wait, work-until-done and Travel to run the clock faster than the world clock, at the rates in Survival tuning. During any of them, Nearby animals update every 10 game minutes and Active animals keep full senses, so they can interrupt it.

**Performance budget.** The Minecraft engine tab's Performance budget is binding: 20 TPS with MSPT under 30 ms on average and never over 50 ms, and 60 fps at a 12-chunk render distance on a mid laptop. The open world's share of it:

| Item | Budget |
| --- | --- |
| Region and district layers | Under 3 s each on a background thread (World map › Generating the three rings) |
| Catch-up | 2 ms per chunk, spread over ticks |
| Animals | 10 ms a tick for 40 Active and 200 Nearby, tiers as in Tier handover |
| Rare events, prompts, ambitions, radio | Under 0.5 ms a tick together; prompts are built once a morning |
| Horizon | The 32 m home field and the 128 m Interior plan, about 650 KB sent at login |
| Hitches | None over 50 ms crossing chunks or region borders |
| Mod save data | Under 5 MB after five years |

*Test:* a scripted five-year run on a dedicated server stays inside every line of this table and the engine's budget, measured by spark in CI, and the mod data stays under 5 MB, matching M9.
