# Creative concepts

Eighteen new gameplay concepts that give the endless year its long-term reasons to play: knowing the land, legendary animals, making the valley yours, rituals, trade, mysteries, memory and ways to play. Each concept gives its loop, rules, numbers, rewards, screen and sound, the systems it ties to, and one sample moment. Everything is built into the Minecraft mod `yearofice`, with registry ids, data paths and GameTests in Data and build.

## How to use this tab

**What is already specified.** Spirits, the run journal, the year card, naming habituated animals, the willow whistle, whittling, the solstice fire, ambitions, rare events, the radio and its catalogue, the ice record, morning prompts and Elin's 24 pages live in the Blueprint and Open world in depth tabs. This tab builds on them by name and changes none of their numbers.

**Guard rules for every concept here.**

- No concept makes survival irrelevant. Extra research points still land in the radio's 200-point credit cap.
- Nothing is supernatural. Every strange event has a natural cause the notebook can explain.
- The messenger still never reports trap catches. Cameras and boards are read in person.
- Nothing punishes a missed ritual, a broken pledge or a spared animal. The cost of a choice is time, calories and risk.
- No hidden scaling: legends and prices never react to how well the player is doing.

## Knowing the land

### 1. Field study

**What it is.** Each species, sign type and sky condition gets a study level from 0 to 5 in a new Study tab of the messenger notebook. Identification, as specified in Foraging and Tracks and sign, is levels 1 and 2. This concept adds levels 3 to 5, earned by watching behavior and sketching.

**Where it starts.** Elin's spare field book sits in the station desk drawer with 3 pencils. The book holds 80 sketches; a pencil lasts 40. A charcoal stick from any dead fire draws 10 rougher sketches, and one sheet of birch bark adds 4 pages.

**Study levels.**

| Level | Name | Earned by | What it gives |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Unknown | — | "Unknown animal" on inspect |
| 1 | Seen | Seen within 60 m | The species name on inspect |
| 2 | Identified | The existing rules: sign beside the animal, a page, the guide | Its sign is named |
| 3 | Observed | 3 behaviors from its list, each watched 20 game seconds without alerting it | Track age read to the game hour instead of a band; its calls get a compass mark within 150 m |
| 4 | Sketched | 2 sketches: one at rest, one in a behavior | Its calls are heard 50% farther; an ear icon on the crosshair shows its alert state when in view |
| 5 | Known | 10 game days of total observation, spread over 2 seasons | For each home range the player has entered, its bedding and feeding areas by hour show on the map within 100 m |

**Behavior lists.** Each species has 4–8 behaviors. Moose: browsing, bedding, wading for pond weed, rut grunting, cow guarding a calf, shedding velvet. Beaver: tree felling, dam repair, tail slap, towing branches, grooming. Behaviors only happen in their season, so level 3 for many species takes a year.

**The sketch.** Holding right-click with the field book starts it. The close clock runs, and the sketch takes 30 real seconds, or 3 game minutes. The animal must stay inside a frame box on screen; each second outside it wobbles the line, and the result scores 1 to 3 stars.

**Practice.** A new skill, Sketching, counts 1 rep per sketch with N = 20. At Practiced a sketch takes 15 real seconds, so the habit stays fast.

**What it gives and costs.** A sketch burns 100 kcal per game hour, the light-work rate. Sketching a grizzly at 40 m is a real risk. Each new level 3 or 4 sends a field note worth 2 radio points, and each level 5 is worth 5, capped at 4 points a week.

**Ties.** The notebook, Tracks and sign, Practice, the radio's research logs, and Spirits: the first sketch of a day gives +1, counted with the journal line.

**On screen.** The field book opens as a paper overlay. Pre-drawn line art for the species appears stroke by stroke, shakier at fewer stars. A pencil scratches while the river and wind keep playing.

**Sample moment.** Day 61, the player lies on the Esker for 3 game minutes while a beaver tows a birch across the pond. The sketch finishes with 3 stars and Beaver reaches level 4. That night a tail slap 200 m off gets a mark on the compass.

**Field-note cards (video 2, A8; RECONCILE 170).** The first time the player does or meets something on a list of about 120 triggers, a small card pairs one real fact with the game rule it drives. The second video's "Wilderness Tip" cards were the part more than five commenters said they loved. These cards are not the Field study's radio field notes, which are research logs.

**What a card says.** Two lines: a real-world fact, then the rule. "Spruce tips carry vitamin C. 1 cup = 1 C-day." Triggers include needle tea, yarrow, snow blindness, a first mineral lick, the first ice-thickness read, fish guts left at camp, a moose in rut, the first warn-once of each kind and the first plant identified in each family. Each card is `data/yearofice/field_note/<id>.json`, naming its trigger event and its two lang keys.

**Display rules.**

- The card shows bottom left for 8 real seconds; the key `key.yearofice.dismiss_card` (default J) closes it sooner.
- At most one card per 10 real minutes. Later triggers queue and show in order; a card queued more than 1 game day files to the notebook unseen, marked new.
- Never during the close clock or a danger alert: it waits until both end.
- Every card is kept in the messenger notebook › Field notes, by date, and never shows twice.
- Client option `field_note_cards`: On (default), First year only, or Off. Off still files them in the notebook.

**Accuracy.** Every card's fact is checked by the Docs department against a cited source in its data file, and no card gives medical advice: a card on yarrow says what the game does, never what a person should take.

**Failure cases.** A trigger that fires while the messenger is off or flat still files its card, and it shows the first time the device works. Two triggers in one minute show 10 real minutes apart. A card's game-rule line reads the live tuning, so a Gentle world prints Gentle's numbers.

**Ties.** Warn once and the messenger (Blueprint › Moment-to-moment mechanics), the notebook, plant identification, the close clock and the HUD layer stack.

**Build.** GUI layer `yearofice:field_note_card` (the 16th, bottom left, Minecraft development › Layer 8), drawn by `client.hud.FieldNoteCardLayer`; first-trigger bookkeeping in `story.notes.FieldNotes`, reusing the warn-once plumbing and the `yearofice:journal` attachment; line type `card` on the HudBus; data kind `field_note/`; sound `yearofice:ui.field_note_card`, a soft paper slide. Owner Look, sound and UI. Milestone M3, with onboarding. GameTests `yearofice:hud/field_note_cap` (M3): 30 triggers in 5 real minutes show 1 card, and the rest at 10-minute intervals in order; `yearofice:hud/field_note_quiet_in_danger` (M3): a trigger during a bear charge's close clock shows only after the clock ends, and never over a danger alert.

### 2. Reading the sky

**What it is.** The player's own forecast, made from signs in the sky and in animals, written in the notebook and scored the next morning. It works with the messenger switched off, which matters in midwinter when the panel barely charges.

**Signs.** Each sign is learned by seeing it once and sketching it once. A learned sign shows its meaning and reliability in the notebook.

| Sign | When it shows | What it predicts | Lead time | True |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Halo round the sun or moon | Thin high cloud, any season | Rain or snow | 12–24 h | 70% |
| Sun dogs | Ice crystals, October–March | Cold holds or deepens | 24 h | 65% |
| Cap cloud on Twin Horn | Before strong westerly flow | Wind over 10 m/s at Windgap | 6–12 h | 75% |
| Mares' tails thickening from the west | Before a front | Front arrives | 24–36 h | 65% |
| Stove smoke hanging flat | Falling pressure | Storm | 12–18 h | 60% |
| Grouse and squirrels feeding hard at midday in winter | Before snow | Snow over 5 cm | 24 h | 60% |
| Clear, still evening on snow | Radiative cooling | 8–12 °C colder by dawn | 12 h | 80% |
| Red dawn with cloud building in the west | Moist air arriving | Rain by evening | 12 h | 60% |

**The read.** Opening the Sky page offers "Read tomorrow." It combines only the signs the player saw today. How many signs the player notices and how often the read is right are set by the one sky-reading skill, Sky lore (RECONCILE 138).

**One skill, one screen.** This page is the screen of Sky lore, which Land and seasons in play › Two new skills defines: 30 reps, Clumsy notices the strongest sign and is right 55%, Handy notices 3 signs and is right 70%, Practiced notices all signs and is right 80%. The old Sky level formula is retired, and learned signs only fill the page.

At Practiced the read matches the messenger's 80% next-day forecast.

**Messenger dark.** A new messenger setting switches the device off except for SOS. Vitals then show only through the body cues in Cold you can see, and the battery draws nothing. A week dark saves a full charge, so a skilled sky reader can run the radio through December.

**What it gives.** A right read logged at the sky gets 1 radio point, at most 2 a week, under a new Whitlock request: "Keep your sky notes. We want a folk-forecast series." A wrong read costs only what the player planned around it.

**Ties.** Weather and the forecast, the messenger battery, the radio, travel planning, and Practice through Sketching.

**On screen.** The notebook writes the read in the survivor's hand: "Tomorrow: cold, clear, calm." The next morning a tick or a cross appears beside it. Sun dogs and halos are drawn as sky particles, visible for 20–60 game minutes.

**Sample moment.** January, battery at 9%. The player sees sun dogs and stove smoke rising straight, writes "cold, clear" and stays out on the trapline. The next dawn is −41 °C, as written, and the battery has not moved.

### 3. Cameras and footage

**What it is.** Three camera types turn watching into a keepsake and a small income. Footage is scored by what it shows, and the best frames become real image files the player can share.

| Device | Where | Power or film | What it records | Limits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| J.O.'s Super 8 film camera | Under the pilot's seat in the wreck, in a canvas bag | Needs 2 headlamp batteries (catalogue, 15 points); 1 exposed cartridge inside, 2 blank ones in the bag | Moving film, 200 real seconds per cartridge | Film must go out on a landing drop to be developed; it returns 2 drops later |
| Elin's camcorder | Station shelf, in a dry bag | Its battery charges on the crate's panel, one device at a time: 1 sunny day gives 2 game hours of recording | Clips of 30 game seconds, 120 on the card | Dead below −25 °C unless kept inside the parka |
| Elin's 2 trail cameras | Strapped to trees at Plot 4 (the willows) and Plot 7 (Beaver Valley) | Lithium cells with 6 months left; replacements cost 15 points | Motion-triggered stills within 15 m, with infrared at night; 500 frames | Read only when the player walks there and pulls the card |

**Shot value.** Every clip, film burst or trail frame gets a value from what is in it.

```latex
V = r_{species} \cdot b_{behavior} \cdot f_{distance} \cdot f_{light} \cdot f_{steady}
```

- r\_species: hare or grouse 1, fox 2, moose or caribou 3, wolf or bear 4, lynx 5, wolverine 6, any legend 10.
- b\_behavior: standing 1, feeding or travelling 1.5, hunting, fighting, nursing or fishing 3.
- f\_distance: 1 within 30 m, 0.5 at 30–80 m, 0.2 beyond.
- f\_light: 1 by day, 0.5 at dusk, 0.1 at night, 0.6 for trail-camera infrared.
- f\_steady: 1 braced or crouched, 0.6 standing, 0.3 moving. Trail cameras count 1.

**What it gives.** A clip of V 6 or more can go out in a film can on a landing drop. It earns V points, capped at 15 a clip and 30 a season. Reviewing a clip of V 6 or more for the first time gives Spirits +2, and the season's best clip becomes a frame in the memory book.

**Real frames.** In Minecraft, a clip of V 6 or more also saves a screenshot to `screenshots/yearofice/<world>/<day>-<species>.png`, on by default. These are the pictures players post.

**J.O.'s cartridge.** Developed, her film shows fog on the bar, the plane nose-down, and her own boots walking. Its last 40 frames show a cow moose crossing the bar, entirely white. The white line in Five legends is older than any one animal.

**Ties.** Solar power and the one-device rule, the radio drops, Five legends, the journal and memory book, and Spirits.

**On screen and sound.** The camcorder shows a red REC dot, a battery bar and a 4:3 frame. The Super 8 whirs, and its viewfinder is yellowed. Trail-camera frames show as grey stills with a time stamp, reviewed on the camcorder's screen.

**Sample moment.** August, the Plot 4 trail camera holds 212 frames. Moose, a fox, and on frame 198 at 03:12 a wolverine carrying something white. That evening, Peeper is not at the woodpile.

## Legends

### 4. Five legends

**What a legend is.** A single animal with a fixed name, an age, a home, a unique sign and a history the player can learn. Each world places 3 of the 5 legends, drawn from `hash(seed, "legends")`, so friends on different seeds meet different ones. A legend counts as a named animal for Spirits and the journal.

**The arc.** Every legend moves through five stages, each logged in a Legends page of the notebook.

1. **Rumor.** A radio line, a page or an old note names it.
2. **Sign.** The player finds its unique sign; the notebook opens its entry.
3. **Sighting.** It is seen within 150 m.
4. **Encounter.** A moment that offers a choice.
5. **Ending.** Killed, released, outlived or lost. The ending is recorded for good.

**The five.**

| Legend | Species and size | Home | Unique sign | Where the rumor comes from |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Old One-Eye | Grizzly boar, 22 years, 320 kg | The head of the riffle at Salmon Bend, July–August | A left fore track missing its outer toe; claw rakes 2.4 m up on the White Bluff birches | Elin's page 8 is about him; Pete: "Fish camp crews called him the Foreman ten years ago" |
| The White Cow | Leucistic moose cow, 9 years | Slough Country in summer, the Hot Spring Basin yard in winter | Long white hairs on willow snags where she beds; white shows from 1 km | J.O.'s film; the roadhouse guest book: "Saw the white cow at the slough. Nobody here would shoot her. 1924" |
| The Lake Mother | Northern pike, 24 years, 21 kg, 1.35 m | Loon Island weed shelf, Big Lake | Ducklings taken in a swirl in June, about 1 a week; a heavy wake at dusk | Arvid's carving at the ice-shack site: "Big one off Loon Island took my spoon. 1966. A.L." The shelf has always held one giant |
| The Black Wolf | Lone black male, 4 years, 48 kg | Windgap Pass and the Barrens edge | Single tracks of 13 cm, always alone; one low voice howling on clear nights at the pass | Pete: "A black one, always alone, three winters now" |
| The Banker | Wolverine, 7 years | The 15 km range of the world's wolverine | Musk on every raided cache; raids in the same order along the player's trails | Arvid's trapline board: "The Banker took his cut" beside 11 sets. Arvid gave every wolverine on his line that name |

**Choices beyond killing.**

- **Old One-Eye.** Blind on his left side: detection range from that side is halved. Fishing the tail of the riffle in the mornings, as Elin did, never draws him; after two runs kept that way he ignores the player at 40 m, a truce the journal records. Breaking the rule draws a bluff charge the first time each year. A kill gives 120,000 kcal and a hide 1.5 times normal; left alone, he dies of age between years 4 and 8, and his skull lies at the bend the next spring.
- **The White Cow.** Wolves try for her calf as a staged attempt, as named animals do, and the player can step in. If she lives 3 more years, a white calf is born (25% a year after that), and the journal counts generations. A kill gives normal moose yields and a white hide that mounts as a trophy.
- **The Lake Mother.** She bites only at dusk at the weed shelf, in June or September, on live bait of 0.5 kg or more, about once per 3 dusks fished there. She breaks a 10 kg line unless the drag stays under 70% for her whole fight. Kept, she is a trophy; released within 60 game seconds, she gives Spirits +8 and next year's Big Lake pike stock +10%.
- **The Black Wolf.** He never joins the home pack and never attacks first. When a tamed female is in season in February, he comes to the yard; allowed to stay 5 nights, he sires the litter, and half the pups are black. If the player has emptied a pack territory, he founds a new pack there the next year.
- **The Banker.** A toll stump on his route, 200 m before a cache, holding 1 kg of gut or fish, makes him skip that cache on 80% of visits. Deadfalls at a raided cache catch him only half as often as a normal wolverine, and each failure halves the next chance. A kill gives the ruff; a successor arrives from the Windgap den 1–2 years later.

**Rewards.** Reaching each stage gives Spirits +2, the Encounter +5. Each ending writes a full journal entry and a line on the year card. Filming a legend scores r 10.

**Ties.** Populations, the encounter director (legends are exempt from its post-kill dips), named animals, Spirits, the story, cameras and the wolf.

**On screen and sound.** Legends have their own GeckoLib model variants: the scarred face, the white coat, the black coat, the oversized pike. Each has one sound cue: One-Eye's huff is lower, the Black Wolf's howl is a single voice. The messenger never names a legend; the player must know it by its sign.

**Sample moment.** Year 2, a July dawn. The player wades the tail of the riffle as the page said. Upstream, a huge bear with a milky left eye stands in the white water, catches a chum and walks off without looking. The journal: "He let me fish."

### 5. Legends the player makes

**What it is.** Ordinary animals become legends through what happens between them and the player. The best stories come from the fish that got away.

| Species | Becomes a legend when | Its mark | Name suggested |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pike, sheefish, lake trout | Hooked and lost by the player 3 times, then alive on May 1 | The lost lure or hook in its jaw, the player's own | "The one with your spoon" |
| Grizzly or black bear | Survives an arrow, or reaches problem-bear count 3 twice | The arrow's fletching in its hump, in the player's colors | "Arrow-in-the-hump" |
| Bull moose | Escapes 3 stalks in one rut with antlers over 1.6 m | A torn ear from a rut fight | "The Ghost of the Willows" |
| Red fox | Robs the same trapline 10 times | A frost-nipped tail tip | "Snare-thief" |
| Any habituated, named animal | Survives 3 winters | Grey on the muzzle | Its own name |

**Rules.** An emergent legend grows 15% heavier a year up to its species' trophy band. At most 2 are active per region and 6 per world. It keeps its mark on its model until it dies.

**Ties.** Fishing and the lost-fish rule, the hit-zone model, bears learn, habituation, and the memory book.

**Sample moment.** Year 3, the player lands an 11 kg pike at the inlet. In its jaw is a scratched aluminum spoon: the one cut from the wreck and lost on day 47.

## Making it yours

### 6. Naming the land

**What it is.** The player names any feature on the messenger map, and the names live in the journal, the year card, the memory book and the radio. A valley with the player's names on it is the record of a life there.

**What can be named.** Landmarks, lakes, ponds, creeks, hills of 30 m prominence or more, fords, fishing spots, pinned traps, worn and cut trails, outposts and animals. Up to 200 names a world, 24 characters each.

**Suggestions.** Inspecting a feature offers "Name it" with 3 suggestions built from what happened there. A bear charge on a bar gives "Charge Bar"; seven pike at an inlet gives "Seven-Pike Inlet"; a fall through ice gives "Cold Bath Bend". The player may type their own.

**Old names.** Pages and notes reveal old names: "Doane's Bench", "Arvid's Slough", Elin's plot numbers. They show as a second line ("old name") and can be kept beside the player's name.

**The radio uses them.** A name reported at a sked enters the contacts' vocabulary. "Pete: Flew over Seven-Pike Inlet. The ice is grey."

**Name boards.** A carved board (a plank, a knife, 1 game hour, one Woodcraft rep) shows the name in the world. Boards weather and need recarving every 5 years, or the letters fade one stage a year.

**What it gives.** Naming a place gives Spirits +1, once a day. Naming an animal keeps its existing +2.

**Ties.** The messenger map, the journal tokens ({place}), the radio, the memory book and cairn mail.

**Sample moment.** Year 2, the player names the crossing "Elin's Ice". At the first sked after breakup, Kari says: "You called it Elin's Ice? Thank you. Tell me the date when it goes."

### 7. Crafts for their own sake

**What it is.** Small handwork for the long evenings that makes the cabin a home, feeds Spirits and becomes a trade good. Each craft makes a piece of quality 1 to 5.

| Craft | Tools | Materials | Time | Season | Makes | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Carved spoon or ladle | Knife | A green birch crook | 2 h | Any | A spoon | Decoration; trade |
| Quill band | Awl, knife | 50 porcupine quills, a hide strip, a dye (blueberry, alder bark, ochre) | 4 h | Dyes picked in summer | A band on a hat, mittens or the parka | Worn: Spirits +1 a day, counted once |
| Seed beadwork | Awl, sinew | 100 silverberry seeds, or 40 glass trade beads from the roadhouse | 3 h | Silverberries ripen in September | A beaded pouch or knife sheath | Decoration; trade |
| Birch-bark basket | Knife, awl | A bark sheet 60 × 40 cm, peeled from a live birch in June–July, and 3 m of spruce root | 2 h | June–July | A 9-slot berry basket, 2 kg | Berries in it are not crushed and keep 25% longer |
| Spruce flute | Knife | A straight spruce branch, split, hollowed and glued with spruce gum, bound with sinew | 6 h | Any | A 6-hole flute | Notes on keys 1–6 while held; counts as the whistle for Spirits; the wolf howls along above 50 trust |
| Ice lantern | Bucket | Water frozen outdoors 8 game hours below −10 °C | 10 min to tap out | November–March | A lantern block for a fat lamp | A lit ice lantern at the door counts as evening light |
| Ice sculpture | Hatchet, ice chisel | 1–4 blocks of 50 cm river ice | 2 h per block | December–March, below −10 °C | 1 of 12 shapes: wolf, moose, bear, raven, pike, owl, a figure and more | Spirits +3 for each new shape; melts in April |
| Ochre paint | Hands | 0.2 kg of red ochre from the Red Wall, 0.1 kg of fat | 1 h | Any | Paint for a door, a sled or a canoe | Color and a painted name on the item |

**Quality.** Each piece starts at 2. Practiced in the craft's skill adds 1, and a short minigame adds −1 to +2. A piece of quality 4 or 5 counts double in the decorated-room rule, as trophies do. The same craft counts once per room, so variety pays.

**Trade.** Pete carries crafts to Lorraine's village table on landing drops. A piece earns its quality × 3 points, capped at 15 points a season. A piece sent as a gift to Kari or Lorraine brings a letter back: Spirits +5, as mail does.

**Ties.** Practice (Woodcraft and Sewing), the seasons, the decorated-room rule in Spirits, the radio drops, the wolf, and porcupines and silverberry as sources.

**On screen and sound.** Each craft is a hold-to-work job with its own hand animation: quills pressed flat with a thumbnail, the knife curling birch. The flute plays real pitched notes (a 6-note scale), and the journal records the first tune.

**Sample moment.** February, −32 °C. The player cuts 4 blocks at the crossing and carves a wolf outside the station door, with a fat lamp inside an ice lantern beside it. The tamed wolf sniffs the ice wolf, then lies down next to it.

### 8. The cabin collection

**What it is.** About 45 natural and historical curiosities, in 6 sets, found on the land and shown on shelves. The collection is a reason to walk the land after it stops being needed for food.

| Set | Pieces | Where and when they turn up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Feathers | 8: spruce grouse, white winter ptarmigan, raven, mallard, Canada goose, great horned owl, loon, gyrfalcon | Under roosts, at kills, on Loon Island, at the Sheep Crags |
| Sheds and horns | 6: moose shed, matched moose pair, caribou shed, Dall sheep horn, wolf tooth, bear claw | Willows December–February; the Barrens after the herd; old kills |
| Skulls | 8: hare, squirrel, marten, fox, lynx, beaver, wolf, bear | Spring melt on old kill sites; the player's own kills |
| Stones | 7: agate, garnet in schist, flint core, rust-red ochre, quartz with gold, black obsidian flake, a river-polished egg stone | Gravel bars after floods; the Iron Range; tailings |
| Ice age | 5: mammoth tusk, mammoth molar, steppe bison horn core, horse leg bone, an American lion tooth | Discovery Creek tailings, the White Bluff after it slumps, earthquake scars |
| Relics | 11: square nail, 1908 coin, tobacco tin, Arvid's lard can, a trap tag "A.L.", J.O.'s map case, an orange stake cap, a pressed fireweed, a roadhouse bead, a pocket watch, a gold-pan bottom | Set pieces, caches, the old camps |

**Where finds come from.** The world makes new ones each year. A spring flood leaves 0–2 agates and a 20% chance of an ice-age piece at a slumped cutbank. An earthquake scar holds a 20% chance of the mammoth tusk already listed in Rare events, and 30% of another ice-age piece. Melting snow shows old kill sites as skulls in April.

**What it gives.** A full set gives Spirits +10 and a journal entry, as an ambition does. A full set on one shelf counts as a trophy. Whitlock pays for specimens: 3 points for a skull species he lacks, 5 for an ice-age piece, capped at 20 a season.

**Ties.** Floods and earthquakes, the radio, the decorated-room rule, Tracks and sign (old kills), and the memory book.

**Sample moment.** After the spring flood the White Bluff has slumped. At its foot lies a curved brown horn core as long as an arm. Whitlock, at the sked: "That's a steppe bison. Twelve thousand years old. Keep it dry."

## The calendar the player keeps

### 9. Rituals and traditions

**What it is.** The player turns any act into a yearly ritual, and the game keeps count. Rituals give a long run its own calendar.

**Making one.** In the journal, "New ritual" asks for a trigger and an act. Triggers: a date, any phenology first in the radio list (first sap, salmon at the bend, snow that stays), or arriving at a named place. Acts: a meal, a fire, a tune, a visit, a carving, an item left at a cairn. Up to 8 rituals a world.

**Rules.** A ritual done inside its window (the trigger day plus 3 days) gives Spirits +3. Done 3 years running, it becomes a tradition: +5 each year and a line on the year card. Missing one costs nothing and only resets the streak.

**Templates offered.**

| Ritual | Trigger | Act | What else happens |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First-salmon feast | The first chinook landed each year | Roast it whole on a plank by the open fire; give the wolf the head; return the bones to the river at the bend | Lorraine tells her village's own first-salmon story on the net that night |

### 10. The breakup board

**What it is.** A guessing game on the moment the river ice moves out at the station crossing, played against the radio contacts. It began with Arvid, who ran a pool with the roadhouse and the village from 1938 to 1971.

**Where it is.** A second plank in Arvid's cabin holds his neighbors' guesses beside the real dates. One name repeats every year: "H. Beck". Lorraine knows the board; her father bet on it.

**How it is played.**

1. **Raise the tripod.** From March 1, the player stands a 4 m tripod of 3 spruce poles on the ice at the crossing, with 10 m of cordage (1 game hour). A trip line runs to the station's wind-up clock on a bank stake.
2. **Guess.** By April 1, the player writes a day and hour in the notebook. The messenger's breakup estimate is still ±10 days then, so reading the land decides it.
3. **The contacts guess.** With the radio, each contact posts a guess at a sked: the true date plus noise of about 6 days. Pete always guesses 3 days early; Dana guesses Arvid's average.
4. **The ice goes.** When breakup moves the crossing's ice, the tripod tips within the game minute and the clock stops. The ice record logs the stopped time, whether or not the player is there.

**Reading the ice.** The player's evidence is all in the existing rules: ice thickness at the crossing, snow depth on March 31, the March warmth, the year profile's early signs, and Arvid's 34 dates with the station's 15. Field study of ice (candled, grey, rotting) adds its signs to the notebook.

**The pot.** Each contact puts in a stake each year. The closest guess takes all of it; the radio announces the winner.

| Contact | Stake |
| --- | --- |
| Pete | 10 kg of free freight on the next landing drop |
| Dana | 15 research points |
| Lorraine | A jar of birch syrup and a recipe |
| Whitlock | A field-guide plate that identifies one unknown plant |
| Kari | A letter, and in her first year of betting a wool hat that was Elin's (head inner, +3 °C) |

**Ties.** The ice record, weather and breakup stages, the radio, drops and the rituals above. A board kept 5 years counts toward "Ice keeper".

**On screen and sound.** The tripod stands with a red rag on top, seen from the station door. At breakup it leans, then drops through with a crack, and a small bell on the trip line rings. The journal records the guess and the true minute.

**Sample moment.** Year 3, the player guesses April 29 at 14:00. On April 29 at 16:12 the bell rings while they split wood. Pete was two days early. "Lorraine: Arvid would have bought you a drink."

## Trade

### 11. The fur trade

**What it is.** Prime furs and research specimens go out with Pete on landing drops and come back as catalogue credit. Prices move each year, so the trapline becomes a planned business and a real choice against warm clothing.

**Grades.** A pelt's grade comes from the month it was taken and its handling.

| Species | Prime months | Base points | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Marten | November–February | 8 | The steady earner |
| Lynx | December–February | 15 | Follows the hare cycle |
| Red fox | November–January | 6 | Rubs out by March |
| Beaver | January–April | 8 | Big pelts, slow to flesh |
| Muskrat | March–April | 1 | Many and small |
| River otter | November–March | 10 | Few |
| Wolf | December–March | 12 | Wanted for parka ruffs |
| Wolverine | November–March | 20 | One a world at a time |

**Points per pelt.**

```latex
points = base \cdot g \cdot m_{year}
```

g is 1.0 for a prime, finished pelt; 0.6 for an unfinished or off-season pelt; 0.2 for a rubbed one; 0 for slipped hair. A finished pelt is fleshed, stretched on a stretcher board and dried 3 game days below 15 °C. Arvid's steel frame in the drowned line cabin finishes beaver and lynx.

**Markets.** On May 1 each species draws a market factor m\_year between 0.6 and 1.6, log-normal around 1.0. Pete reads the fur report at the first October sked: "Marten's up a third. Nobody wants fox." The notebook charts every year beside Arvid's fur ledger of 1938–1971, in dollars.

**Orders.** Once a winter Pete brings an order with a deadline and double points: "A hatmaker in town wants 6 matched marten by January 15."

**Caps.** Furs earn at most 60 points a season and specimens 20. A diligent logger already reaches the 200-point credit cap, so furs mostly help a player who logs half the days (about 130 points) reach a drop.

**Specimens.** Whitlock buys a lynx carcass for the hare-cycle study (4 points), a wolf skull (3), and tissue in a sample kit (10 vials from the catalogue, 10 points).

**Trade-offs.** A sold pelt is not a mitten. Taking over 30% of a region's marten in a season halves its encounters for a year, as Populations says, so greed costs next winter's line.

**Ties.** Traps and the trapline, butchering and hide work, Clothing, Populations, the radio drops and the hare cycle.

**On screen.** Stretched pelts hang on the cabin wall as blocks, drying, with a grade tag on inspect. The trade list opens at the landing drop as a 27-slot crate the player fills; Pete reads back the points.

**Sample moment.** Year 2, the market draws marten at 1.5. The player runs a 20-set line through January, wears the hare mittens a second winter, and sends 9 prime marten out for 60 points: a bow saw blade and a wool blanket.

### 13. Strange but natural

**What it is.** Real, rare phenomena of the interior winter and summer, staged with exact triggers. Each is a story to tell, and each can be logged for research.

| Phenomenon | Trigger | Chance | What the player sees and hears | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Aurora sound | An aurora storm night, calm, below −20 °C, on ground 100 m above its surroundings | 10% of qualifying nights | Faint crackles and soft claps for 30–90 real seconds | Spirits +3; a phenology log worth 3 points. Whitlock: "Four reports in thirty years" |
| Frost quake | A drop of 15 °C or more in 24 h onto saturated ground, after a wet fall | 1–3 in such winters | A boom like a falling tree, a camera thump, a 20 m crack in the ground or lake ice | Wakes a sleeper; game within 200 m flees; the wolf whines |
| Singing ice | New black ice 5–10 cm thick, struck by a thrown stone | Every time | A falling "pew" tone; thinner ice sings higher | The pitch is a reading of thickness, learned in Field study |
| Ice circle | A river eddy in October–November | 1 a year at a seeded eddy | A rotating disc of ice 10–20 m across, perfectly round | A camera subject (V counts as 6) |
| Superior mirage | Calm, sunny days on Big Lake ice, March–April | 5% of such days | Loon Island and the far shore stretch into towers | For 10 game minutes the map reveals landmarks 2 km beyond normal |
| Frost flowers | New lake ice on a calm night below −20 °C | 30% | Fields of feathery crystals 2–4 cm tall | Spirits +1; the next snowfall wipes them |
| Whumpf | Snowpack with depth hoar, February–March, on open slopes | 5% a crossing | A deep thump and the snow drops 2 cm | Its notebook entry warns that slopes over 30° may slide |
| Noctilucent clouds | Late July and early August, near midnight twilight | 10% of clear nights | Thin electric-blue ripples low in the north | Spirits +1 and a phenology log |

**Explanations.** Each phenomenon starts as "Unexplained" in the notebook. Whitlock or Field study level 3 gives the natural cause: an inversion discharge, a cryoseism, ice acoustics, eddy rotation, refraction over cold air, vapor sublimation, a collapsing weak layer, high-altitude ice cloud.

**Ties.** Weather and inversions, ice, sleep, the wolf, cameras, the radio's phenology firsts and the map.

**Sample moment.** December, −38 °C, after a wet fall. A boom cracks the night at 02:10 and the player wakes on the bunk. In the morning a crack runs 20 m across the yard, straight through the snow, and the notebook writes "Unexplained".

## Neighbors and memory

### 14. Camp jays and the raven deal

**What it is.** Two bird relationships that make camp feel alive: gray jays (the Canada jay; the Bestiary's species yearofice:gray\_jay) that move in, and ravens that learn to lead the player to meat. Both use the habituation rules and add company before the radio and the wolf.

**Gray jays.** A pair of gray jays arrives at any camp where meat has been cooked on 3 days within 7. After 5 visits they take scraps from the open hand. They steal 0.1 kg an hour of daylight each from any meat left unwrapped outside. They alarm-call at a predator within 60 m, which shows as a compass mark, like the wolf's stare.

**The raven deal.** Leaving a gut pile at the same "raven rock" 5 times makes the local ravens habituated to the player. From then on, when a fresh carcass lies within 2 km, a raven flies over the player 3 times calling, then heads off in a straight line. The line leads to meat 70% of the time; the rest is old carrion. A fresh kill there may already have bears or wolves.

**Play.** Ravens slide down the station roof on fresh snow, and take one shiny item (a spoon lure, a coin) left outdoors, about once a month. The item lies at the raven roost, a findable stash.

**What it gives.** A named jay counts as a named animal seen for Spirits and company. A jay on the hand gives +2 the first time each winter. The raven deal finds winter-killed moose and wolf kills sooner.

**Ties.** Habituation, the cache and theft, Ravens tell the truth, the winter-killed moose event, company and solitude, and the collection's feathers.

**Sample moment.** March, the player is snowshoeing home empty-handed. A raven passes over three times, croaking, and flies east. A kilometre on, ravens rise off a winter-killed moose, 70% of it still there.

### 15. The valley remembers

**What it is.** The player's years stay written on the land, and returning to old places brings back what happened there. The base-from-above view and regrowth are in the Blueprint; this concept adds decay, memory and reuse.

**Old camps decay in stages.**

| Years unvisited | What shows |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Boughs brown, the lean-to's poles grey |
| 2 | The lean-to sags; moss on the ring stones |
| 3 | The lean-to falls; fireweed in the ring |
| 5 | The ring is a mossy circle, "an old fire ring" on inspect |
| 10 | A birch sapling grows in the ring |

A collapsed structure keeps a refund of 50% of its poles and stones if rebuilt. The messenger still lists the place under its name.

**Places remember.** Any spot where the player spent 20 game hours, or where a first or near miss was logged, becomes a known place. Arriving within 30 m shows its best journal line: "Here, Year 1, Day 12: first night out." A first visit each year gives Spirits +2.

**Animals move in.** A fallen lean-to has a 30% chance a year to become a marten den, a new trap site. Cliff swallows nest under the cabin eaves from year 2 and eat 10% of the mosquito index within 30 m. A porcupine chews salty wood: an outhouse or an axe handle left out loses durability. Red squirrels midden in a woodshed left 60 days.

**Marks the player makes.**

- **Cairns.** A letter cairn is 12 stones, 10 game minutes, permanent; a trail-marker cairn is 5 stones (Structures and buildables › Snow, ice and trails). A letter cairn can hold one item and one note, read by anyone who opens it.
- **The station wall.** Each logged breakup is pencilled beside Arvid's dates as wall text, so the wall fills up year by year.
- **Graves.** A lost wolf or a legend may be buried under a cairn with its name. The journal adds the grave to its story map.
- **Your fire.** A forest fire the player started is tagged in the journal and on the map, and its regrowth years are counted.

**Ties.** Regrowth and worn trails, outposts and upkeep, populations, bugs, the ice record, the journal and cairn mail.

**Sample moment.** Year 6, a trip to Salmon Bend passes the first trail camp. A birch now grows out of the ring. The messenger shows the old line: "Year 1, Day 64. Wet, cold, 3 grayling. The bear never came."

### 16. The memory book and story map

**What it is.** Each May 1, beside the year card, the game binds the year into a book and a map the player keeps. They are the long run's proof, and the easiest thing to show a friend.

**The memory book.** A written book item titled "Year 3", authored by the player. It has 14 pages:

1. A cover page with the year card's numbers.
2. Twelve month pages, May to April. Each holds the month's top journal line, the place's name, the date, and the file name of the month's best camera frame.
3. An index of every name given that year.

**The story map.** A map item of the 2 km home area and every region visited that year. Icons mark where things happened: firsts, near misses, big kills and catches, legends, rituals, graves and named places. Inspecting an icon in a map frame shows its journal line.

**Keeping them.** The books stand on a shelf as decorations; 5 or more count as one trophy. A shelf of 10 books opens the hidden ambition "Ten volumes".

**Ties.** The journal, the year card, naming, cameras, the base from above and Spirits.

**Sample moment.** Year 5, the player shows a friend the station shelf. Five volumes stand there; the Year 2 map shows a cross at "Cold Bath Bend" and a star at "Seven-Pike Inlet".

## Playing it your way

### 17. Pledges and shared seeds

**What it is.** Self-set challenges the player takes on for a year, and seed codes that let friends play the same valley. Both turn a sandbox into stories that can be compared.

**Pledges.** Chosen at world creation or any May 1, kept for one year. Breaking one ends it quietly with a journal line. A kept pledge earns a badge on the year card.

| Pledge | Rule | What makes it possible |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cold camp | No fire or stove lit or used from December 1 to February 28 | Furs, the hot spring, raw frozen fish sliced thin, water from open leads, and the new three-wolf night: each Companion wolf sleeping within 1 m gives +4 °C, up to 3 |
| One arrow | One arrow made on May 1; none other that year | It can be refletched and re-tipped; a broken shaft ends the pledge |
| Wolf-less | No wolf above 25 trust all year | Traps, the jays and the raven deal |
| Crate only | No supply drop ordered | The land alone |
| Messenger dark | The messenger off except SOS | Reading the sky and the map in the player's head |
| Deadwood | No live tree felled | Standing dead spruce, driftwood, the burn |
| Ironless | No iron item made or used | Flint, bone and antler tools |

**Seed codes.** A code carries everything needed to rebuild the same start: `YOI1-7F3K-Q9ZD-S-04`. The parts are the generator version, the seed in base 32, the preset letter (G, S or B) and the pledges as hexadecimal bits.

**Weekly seeds.** A "Weekly valley" button builds the seed from `hash("yoi-week", year, week)` and adds one twist from a list of 12. Twists include "The bear already knows the camp" (count 2), "Half the crackers", "A white cow is drawn" and "The big berry year comes early".

**Card codes.** The year card shows a short code made from its numbers, the seed and the week. Friends type it in to show the card on their own screen. It guards against typos, not cheating; it is a shared language, not a leaderboard.

**Ties.** Presets and modes, the year card, fire, the wolf, Reading the sky and the seed rules in Scope and pillars.

**Sample moment.** Week 43, three friends roll the same weekly valley with "Half the crackers". On May 1 they swap card codes. One of them kept a One arrow year and took a moose with it.

### 18. Cairn mail and the shared valley

**What it is.** Two optional ways for friends to share a valley, kept apart from co-op itself, which Ruhan placed after 1.0 (solo first, decided 2026-10-19). The first works in single-player today; the second waits for the co-op build.

**Cairn mail, single-player.** A player on a seed writes a note, puts it in a cairn with up to one small made item, and exports it. The export is a small file, `yearofice/cairnmail/<seed>-<x>-<z>.json`. A friend on the same seed and version imports it, and the note appears in a cairn at the same spot, or the nearest open ground within 20 m.

**What can travel.** A note of 600 characters, and one item from a list: a carved figure, a feather, a lure, a beadwork piece, a pressed flower. No food, tools, weapons or iron, so mail never changes survival.

**Friend marks.** A friend's memory book can be imported as faint notes at its places: "Here, {friend} fell through the ice. Year 1, Day 199." They are read like old camps, and can be hidden.

**The shared valley, once co-op is built after 1.0.** Minecraft multiplayer comes with the engine, so the build cost in Open decisions is lower than the first estimate assumed; that was the Director's input to the co-op decision.

- Each player has their own crate, messenger and year card. The station is shared.
- Other players' status is hidden beyond 60 m; their skeds are heard on the radio ("Observer Two logged 4 species").
- Mail between players goes through cairns or by hand, never by chat at a distance.
- "Last one standing" runs on Hardcore: the year card shows who is still in only after May 1.

**Ties.** Seeds, cairns, the radio, the memory book and the save format's reserved players list.

**Sample moment.** A friend imports a cairn letter at Windgap Pass on Elin's day: "Left you a raven feather. Day 350, −22 °C, wind 12 m/s. Don't cross in this." The feather is there, under the stones.

### GameTests

Each test is a NeoForge GameTest in `com.yearofice.gametest.CreativeConceptsTests`, run by the balance simulator at its milestone.

1. **Study levels.** A scripted observer who watches 3 beaver behaviors for 20 game seconds each and makes 2 sketches reaches level 4. At level 3, every track-age read is rounded to 1 game hour, against 3 bands below it.
2. **Shot value.** `FootageScorer` gives a wolf hunting at 25 m, by day, crouched, a V of 12.0. A hare standing at 100 m at night on handheld gives 0.012. No clip scores above 15 points, and a season never exceeds 30.
3. **Legends placed.** Over 200 seeds every world places exactly 3 of the 5 legends, each inside its home region role. Each of the 5 appears on 50–70% of seeds.
4. **Emergent legend.** A pike hooked and lost 3 times that is alive on May 1 is flagged as a legend, weighs 1.15 times its old mass, and carries the lost lure on its `mark` bone.
5. **Fur market.** Over 1,000 drawn years every market factor stays within 0.6–1.6, with a mean of 1.0 ± 0.05. In 100 simulated seasons of heavy trapping, fur points never exceed 60 a season.
6. **Breakup board.** Over 100 simulated years the tripod trips within 1 game hour of the ice record's breakup time. The contacts' guesses have a mean absolute error of 4–8 days, and Pete's average error is 3 days early ± 1.
